# Daily Market Briefing — August 25, 2026 (Tuesday)

Global snapshot: after Monday's Samsung-driven chip scare in Korea rattled Asia, today is a broad **relief-rally / recovery day** across the region — Taiwan, Korea, and (mildly) Hong Kong all bounced back, as chip stocks rebounded globally (Intel, Micron, and Nvidia all firmer early in the US too) on the read that Monday's move was company-specific, not a sector-wide re-rating. Europe extended its own separate, calmer winning streak (FTSE's sixth straight up day) on genuine macro relief — softer-than-feared Iran sanctions and easing oil prices. The US is quiet, holding near Monday's levels as investors wait for Wednesday's Nvidia earnings and Friday's Jackson Hole speech from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh — still the two events that matter most for every market in this folder. Bitcoin briefly cleared $80,000 intraday for the first time in three months, oil fell sharply (~3%) even with new Iran sanctions in force, and the dollar stayed pinned near recent lows. See `usa.md`, `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, and `uk_europe.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

**Reading the table:** Taiwan and Korea posted the day's biggest moves, and both are recoveries — Korea's KOSPI erased a 4%+ morning loss to close +0.68%, and Taiwan's TAIEX bounced +0.91% on heavier volume, both riding the same global "chip stocks rebound after Monday's Samsung scare" theme. Zoom out to the 1-month view, though, and the picture flips: **Korea's +11.94% and Taiwan's +8.57% over 20 trading days are both recovering from the sharp, well-documented late-July sell-offs in each market** (KOSPI fell as much as −10.84% in a single session on Jul 28; TAIEX fell −4.65% the same day) — so today's daily gain and the month's big percentage recovery are really two chapters of the same story, not unrelated signals. The 5-day window tells a third story: both Korea (−1.85%) and Taiwan (−0.31%) are still net lower than a week ago, meaning this week's volatility has, on net, been a step down from last Tuesday even after today's bounce. Europe is the most stable region across all three windows — FTSE is up on every timeframe, and the whole region's moves stay inside a tight ±2% band at every horizon, underscoring its role as the "calm zone" flagged in Monday's report. The US shows the same pattern in miniature: quiet today, mildly negative over the week, solidly positive over the month.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** No fresh Monday/Tuesday ETF flow print was available as of writing; the most recent published data (Friday, Aug 21) still shows the same $6.38B rotation into S&P 500 ETFs (SPY+VOO) against a roughly flat net Nasdaq-100 flow — a diversification move, not a tech exit. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** Foreign investors (外資) net-sold just **NT$786.58M** today — a fraction of Monday's NT$15.75B outflow — even as the index rallied +0.91% on heavier turnover. Foreign selling essentially paused today rather than reversing; the year-to-date cumulative outflow (~NT$2.28T / ~US$70.7B) and 48.7% foreign ownership stake are unchanged from Monday. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** Foreign investors net-sold **3.82 trillion won** today, but institutional (+1.17T won) and retail (+1.05T won) investors both bought the dip hard enough to flip the index from a 4%+ morning loss into a +0.68% close — domestic buying, not foreign buying, drove today's recovery. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** No daily flow print available; the index itself was essentially flat, with regional chip strength offsetting lingering pre-Nvidia caution. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Japan:** No fresh weekly JPX flow data was available for the period including today (JPX publishes on a weekly lag); the most recent published figure (from Monday's report) showed foreign investors net-selling ¥198.8B last week, framed locally as a pause rather than a reversal. See `japan.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** No new daily flow signals stood out today — Malaysia's session was described as "flat, awaiting an earnings catalyst" rather than driven by a specific flow story, and Thailand's and Singapore's/Indonesia's moves (the latter two one session behind) were sector- and stock-specific rather than broad money-flow events. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** No daily outflow signal; the region extended its winning streak (FTSE's sixth straight gain, STOXX 600 at a fresh monthly high) on genuine macro relief rather than a flow-driven rotation. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** The dollar-weakness trade that has dominated the month continued — DXY sat near ~98.95, still close to its 3-month low — while bitcoin briefly cleared $80,000 intraday (its highest opening in over three months) and gold stayed near multi-week highs despite a small pullback today. That combination (soft dollar + strong hard assets + a chip-stock rebound in equities) reads as risk-on across most asset classes simultaneously, rather than money rotating out of one region and into another.

**Net read:** today looks less like a new trend and more like a recovery/consolidation day after Monday's Korea-specific shock rippled through the region's chip stocks. The clearest, most consistent signal across every market in this folder is still the same one flagged Monday: the dollar-weakness trade benefiting gold and bitcoin, now joined by a broad, global chip-stock rebound ahead of Wednesday's Nvidia earnings — both of which face their real test this week, first from Nvidia's print, then from Fed Chair Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole speech.

## How to read the 5-day/20-day columns in this report

The 5-day (~1 trading week) and 20-day (~1 trading month) comparisons in this report are measured against each market's own closing level from 5 and 20 trading days before today, sourced from each index's own historical data series (linked in the References section of each file). Because different markets close at different times relative to Taipei (this report's home base), a small number of rows — flagged with an asterisk — use the most recent available close rather than today's, which shifts their comparison windows by one trading day. Bitcoin and Ethereum, which trade 24/7, use "week" and "month" figures reported directly by the cited source rather than a 5-/20-trading-day calculation.
