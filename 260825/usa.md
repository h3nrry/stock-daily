# USA Stock Market Analysis — August 25, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

US markets are little changed to modestly higher heading into midday — a quiet pause after Monday's rotation, with investors sitting on their hands ahead of Wednesday's Nvidia earnings and Friday's Jackson Hole speech from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Premarket futures were firmly green (S&P +0.32%, Dow +0.22%, Nasdaq +0.63%), and early trading showed chip stocks broadly rebounding — Intel +4.1%, Micron +2.9%, Nvidia +1.3% — a mirror image of the chip-sector rebound seen in Korea and Taiwan today (see `korea.md`, `taiwan.md`). Semiconductors, AI, and nuclear-power names led sector gainers; crude oil sank roughly 3% as traders shrugged off the new Iran sanctions.

## What's driving today

- **Chip-sector rebound**: after Monday's broad AI/semiconductor pullback and Korea's Samsung-driven shock, chip names bounced globally today — Intel, Micron, and Nvidia all firmer in early trading, consistent with the same theme playing out in Taiwan (TSMC +1.05%) and Korea (KOSPI +0.68%, KOSDAQ +1.7%).
- **Nvidia earnings, Wednesday**: still the single most important catalyst of the week. Nvidia notified customers of server price increases ahead of the print — a detail some strategists read as a margin/pricing-power positive, others as a sign of "rising costs, eroded margins" concern (per Capital.com's Kyle Rodda).
- **Oil sank sharply** — WTI down roughly 3% to the low $80s, Brent down roughly 3% to the high $80s — even as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's new Iran sanctions remain in force; traders reading the sanctions as already priced in, or judging supply disruption risk as overstated.
- **Bitcoin cleared $80,000** intraday for the first time in over three months, extending its multi-week rally (see Bitcoin/Crypto section below) on continued dollar-weakness and bond-buyback-driven flows into hard assets.
- **US-Canada tariff tension** remains a live, unresolved risk: reports of a 50% US tariff on Canadian auto imports effective January 2027 keep this a headline risk for autos and industrials even as it hasn't dominated today's price action.
- **New home sales, ADP weekly employment, and manufacturing data** are all due today — inputs that feed directly into the market's read on the Fed ahead of Friday.

## US Treasury yields

| Maturity | Yield | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 10-Year | **~4.66–4.70%** | Down modestly, third session of easing; "steadied around 4.7%" per Reuters/Trading Economics |

The 10-year continues to hover in the high-4.60s/low-4.70s range it's occupied since Monday, as investors weigh the Treasury's expanded long-bond buyback program against ongoing deficit-driven issuance concerns. Zoom out and the trend from a month ago is still mildly higher (10-year +0.01 pts over the month per Trading Economics), even after this week's pullback from the highs — the underlying "more supply, more buybacks" tension flagged in Monday's write-up (`usa.md` from Aug 24) hasn't resolved either way. Friday's Jackson Hole speech remains the key event that could move yields meaningfully in either direction.

## Bitcoin / Crypto

| Asset | Price | Day | Week (~5 trading-day equiv.) | Month (~20 trading-day equiv.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Ethereum (ETH) | **$2,476** | +0.8% | **+29.8%** | **+33.4%** |

Bitcoin's opening today was its highest in over three months, and it briefly cleared $80,000 intraday before easing back — the strongest multi-week run in roughly two years, per CoinShares' James Butterfill (quoted in Monday's write-up). The rally is being driven by the same two forces flagged all week: the Treasury's expanded bond-buyback program feeding dollar-weakness/hard-asset flows, and fresh optimism around potential crypto-friendly legislation following a White House meeting between President Trump and major crypto-platform representatives.

## What to watch this week

1. **Nvidia earnings, Wednesday** — still the single highest-stakes event for the AI trade. Today's chip-sector bounce (Intel, Micron, Nvidia all up premarket/early) suggests the market is leaning constructive into the print, but that can reverse fast on any guidance disappointment.
2. **Jackson Hole, Friday** — Fed Chair Warsh's first major keynote. The dollar-weakness/gold/bitcoin trade that's dominated markets all month is the single biggest cross-asset bet riding on this speech's tone.
3. **July PCE inflation data, Wednesday** — a direct input into how markets read Warsh's credibility on Friday.
4. **US-Canada tariff escalation** — the reported 50% auto-tariff plan (effective Jan 2027) is a genuinely new, still-unpriced risk for autos/industrials with Canada exposure.

## Bottom line

A quiet, wait-and-see session: US indices are little changed near Monday's closing levels, with a global chip-stock rebound (echoing Taiwan and Korea) providing some support after Monday's AI-sector wobble. Oil's sharp pullback and bitcoin's push through $80,000 both point to the same dollar-weakness backdrop that's shaped the whole month. With Nvidia earnings Wednesday and Warsh's Jackson Hole debut Friday both still ahead, today reads as a pause before the week's real catalysts rather than a resolved trend in either direction.
