# Korea Stock Market Analysis — August 25, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

**KOSPI** closed at **6,742.74**, up **45.78 points (+0.68%)** — a sharp intraday reversal after opening down 2.4% and falling more than 4% during the morning session. **KOSDAQ** closed at **827.15**, up **13.82 points (+1.7%)**. The story of the day is recovery: semiconductor stocks that opened sharply lower on lingering fallout from Monday's Samsung shareholder-return disappointment clawed back their losses as investors rotated into large-cap shares through the afternoon.

## What drove the reversal

- **Semiconductor stocks recovered from Monday's US tech sell-off spillover and the Samsung-specific shock**, with the morning's steep opening losses (KOSPI briefly breached 6,500, down over 4%) fully reversing by the close.
- **Samsung Electronics** closed at **257,000 won (+0.19%)** and **SK Hynix** at **1.678 million won (+0.42%)** — both chip giants recovering despite early selling pressure tied to shareholder-return uncertainty (Samsung) and wage-negotiation setbacks (SK Hynix).
- The same "chip stocks rebound" theme played out globally today — Taiwan's TSMC +1.05% and UMC's continued strength, plus early gains for Intel, Micron, and Nvidia in the US (see `taiwan.md`, `usa.md`) — suggesting Monday's Samsung shock is being read as company-specific, not a sector-wide re-rating.

## Money flow — investor activity

| Investor group | Net flow (Aug 25) |
|---|---|
| Foreign investors | **−3.82 trillion won (net sell)** |
| Institutional investors | **+1.17 trillion won (net buy)** |
| Retail/individual investors | **+1.05 trillion won (net buy)** |

Foreign investors extended their selling from Monday (which itself totaled roughly 3+ trillion won combined with institutions, concentrated almost entirely in the Samsung shock), while both institutional and retail investors bought the dip — domestic buying interest absorbing foreign selling pressure and helping drive the intraday reversal from a 4%+ morning loss to a positive close.

## Bottom line

A textbook V-shaped recovery day: KOSPI opened sharply lower on chip-sector jitters carried over from Monday's Samsung shock, then rallied through the session as domestic institutional and retail buyers stepped in and semiconductor names (including Samsung and SK Hynix themselves) recovered. Foreign investors remained net sellers, but domestic buying more than offset it. With Nvidia's earnings Wednesday and Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole speech Friday still ahead, today's resilience is an encouraging sign that Monday's scare was contained rather than the start of a broader chip-sector reassessment — though that read will be tested again by Wednesday's print.
