## What drove the session (so far)

- **Stronger-than-expected labor market data reinforced the "hike now behind us" relief trade**: initial jobless claims for the week ending Sept 12 came in at **196,000, well below the ~208,000 consensus**, with the four-week moving average at 203,250 versus an expected 206,000 — a genuinely positive labor print that helped underpin Thursday's broad rally.
- **Chip/tech stocks led decisively**, a reversal of Wednesday's bifurcated pattern: **Intel (INTC) opened up 5.08%** and was trading around **$109.26 (+8.04%)** on continued optimism around its "accelerating turnaround trajectory," preliminary SK Hynix memory-manufacturing talks, anticipated processor price adjustments, and strong AI-driven server-processor demand. **Micron (MU) rose 5.74%** and **SanDisk (SNDK) rose 6.17%**, and the Technology Equipment sector was up roughly 2.06% — a much broader chip-sector participation than Wednesday's narrow Intel/SK-Hynix-only rally (recall ON Semiconductor's −8.5% Wednesday).
- **Nvidia (NVDA) rose to ≈$219.56 (+2.65%)** and **Amazon (AMZN) gained ≈2.40%**, both contributing to the Nasdaq's outperformance.
- **Gold spiked further on the strong labor data**, trading at **$4,370.58/oz, up 2.50%** on the day per Kitco — a notable extension of Wednesday's already-elevated levels, seemingly at odds with a "risk-on, stocks rally" session, suggesting some investors are still hedging even as equities rallied (see the Gold section of `../260917/README.md` for the fuller cross-asset picture, including the apparent tension with Hong Kong's gold-mining-stock weakness the same day, `hongkong.md`).
- **Oil extended its multi-session decline**: **WTI ≈$100.34/barrel (−2.04%)** and **Brent ≈$102.94/barrel (−2.73%)**, continuing the reversal from the Saudi-pipeline de-escalation story that has now run for several sessions (see `../260916/usa.md`).
- **Bitcoin held firm, trading at ≈$76,440.54, up 0.71%** on the day — a modest extension of Wednesday's $75,000-$76,000 range, showing continued resilience.
- **The VIX fell sharply, down 9.77% to 15.98**, a clean signal that the market's fear/uncertainty premium around the FOMC outcome has largely unwound now that the decision is confirmed and behind the market.

## Money-flow analysis (preliminary, intraday)

Thursday's session so far reads as a genuine relief rally: with the Fed's hike and hawkish dot plot now a known, priced event rather than an overhang, and a stronger-than-expected jobless-claims print removing one source of near-term macro uncertainty, risk appetite returned broadly — the VIX's sharp decline and the Nasdaq's outsized gain versus the Dow both point to money flowing back into growth/tech names specifically, a mirror image of Wednesday's rotation out of them. Gold's continued strength even as equities rallied is the session's one genuine cross-current, suggesting some investors are keeping hedges in place (perhaps against the 10-year yield's continued proximity to elevated levels, or against a resumption of Middle East-linked oil volatility) even as the broader market re-risks. This is preliminary: the pattern could still shift materially by the close, especially given how much of Wednesday's own session reversed course in its final hours.
