# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 17, 2026 (Thursday)

**Europe got its first chance to trade on the Fed's actual FOMC outcome Thursday, and the reaction was calmly positive** — a genuine contrast with the "sell the (hawkish) news" reaction that hit US equities Wednesday (see `usa.md`). The **STOXX Europe 600 closed at 640.21, up roughly 0.5%** from Wednesday's confirmed 637.10 close, with **FTSE 100, DAX, and CAC 40 each posting gains in the +0.4% to +0.6% range** per available reporting. The **Bank of England held its policy rate at 3.75% in a 6-3 split vote**, with three MPC members dissenting in favor of a hike to 4%, even as UK inflation hit a five-month high of 3.1%.

## What drove the market

- **The Fed's 25bp hike, now an actual and confirmed event rather than an anticipated one, was digested calmly.** A Saxo Markets note framed the session as "First Fed hike since 2023 lifts the dollar," and Euronews described European markets opening higher specifically "after Fed hike as US dollar hits seven-week high" — the same hike that triggered a "sell the news" reaction in US equities Wednesday produced a relief-driven, buy-the-clarity reaction in Europe Thursday.
- **The Bank of England held rates at 3.75% in a 6-3 vote**, with the three dissenters favoring a hike to 4% — the BoE stood alone among major central banks this week (the Fed hiked Wednesday, the ECB lifted its deposit rate to 2.5% the week before). UK inflation hit a **five-month high of 3.1%**, driven by energy-price shocks tied to the Iran conflict — **Brent crude is up 36% and UK wholesale gas prices up 78% since July**, and the BoE explicitly warned that "inflation is likely to rise further over coming quarters," with risks to the outlook "tilted to the upside" on potential "second-round effects" into wages and broader pricing.
- **Easing oil prices provided a genuine cross-market tailwind**, continuing the reversal documented in Wednesday's session (see `../260916/usa.md`) — Brent traded down roughly 2-2.7% intraday to the $103/barrel area (see `usa.md` for the fuller oil picture), a boon for travel/airline stocks specifically.
- **Airlines and automakers led the day's gains**: **Ryanair rose 1.1%** and **Lufthansa gained 1.2%** on the oil tailwind, while **BMW, Renault, and Volkswagen each rose roughly 1%** — within the CAC 40 specifically, **Renault gained more than 2%** and **Stellantis rose 1.6%**, a notable reversal from Wednesday's auto-sector weakness (Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz had both fallen 2.6% Wednesday on Berenberg's overcapacity warning — see `../260916/uk_europe.md`).
- **Stock-specific moves stood out on both sides**: **Bilfinger plunged 19.5%** after a downward guidance revision, and **Raiffeisen Bank International fell 8.1%** following a short-position announcement — both genuine, idiosyncratic single-stock stories rather than sector-wide moves. On the UK side, **Capricorn Energy accepted revised takeover terms from DNO worth $396 million**, **Next raised its 2026-27 profit guidance by £12 million to £1.255 billion**, and **Drax said it expects 2026 adjusted EBITDA near consensus**.
- **Technology stocks were a rare soft spot within the CAC 40**: **Dassault Systèmes fell 2.4%**, a reminder that Thursday's rally was concentrated in cyclicals (autos, travel) and rate-sensitive/energy-linked names rather than being universal.
- **Trump signaled progress toward Middle East peace**, an additional factor cited alongside the Fed outcome in supporting Thursday's "measured investor optimism" per market commentary.

## Money-flow analysis

Thursday's European session was the mirror image of Wednesday's US reaction to the same FOMC event: where US investors "sold the (hawkish) news" on the dot plot's surprise, European investors treated the now-confirmed, no-longer-uncertain Fed outcome as a clearing event and bought into it, helped by a genuine oil-driven tailwind for travel and auto names and by the Bank of England's own decision to hold rates despite rising domestic inflation. The rotation was sector-specific rather than universal — airlines, automakers, and (within France) autos broadly outperformed, while technology (Dassault Systèmes) lagged — a pattern that echoes Wednesday's German auto-sector story in reverse (autos were Wednesday's laggard, Thursday's leader). The BoE's hold, despite three dissenting votes for a hike and inflation at a five-month high, is itself a notable divergence from the Fed's and ECB's more hawkish recent moves, worth tracking into the BoE's next meeting for whether the "second-round effects" risk it flagged materializes.
