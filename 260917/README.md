# Daily Market Briefing — September 17, 2026 (Thursday)

**Thursday was the day Asia, then Europe, got their first chance to trade on Wednesday night's actual FOMC outcome (a 25bp hike to 3.75%-4.00% paired with a hawkish dot plot), and the reaction across almost every market outside the US itself was calm-to-constructive rather than a continuation of Wednesday's US "sell the (hawkish) news" move.** **Taiwan's TAIEX rose a further 0.95% to 46,288.0**, with broad market breadth and all three institutional-investor categories turning net buyers after Wednesday's heavy net-selling. **Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 0.33% to 64,136.25**, though the TOPIX's stronger 0.80% gain revealed a genuine rotation from semiconductors into value stocks. **Korea's KOSPI closed essentially flat (−0.04% to 6,715.41)**, round-tripping from a +0.91% open as foreign investors extended their selling streak to a seventh consecutive session. **Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.44% to 24,604.29**, pressured by a stronger US dollar hitting gold and energy names, even as southbound Stock Connect logged a ninth consecutive day of net buying. **Southeast Asia was broadly positive**: **Thailand's SET surged 1.32%**, **Indonesia's IHSG rose 0.40%**, and **Singapore's STI opened higher (+0.47%, ~+0.5% headline)**, while **Malaysia's FBM KLCI (back open after Wednesday's holiday) slipped 0.26%**. **Europe closed higher across the board** — **STOXX 600 confirmed at 640.21 (+0.5%)**, with **FTSE 100, DAX, and CAC 40 each estimated in the +0.4% to +0.6% range** (exact index levels not yet confirmed from sources indexed at writing time — see `uk_europe.md`) — as the Bank of England held rates at 3.75% in a 6-3 vote despite inflation at a five-month high. **The US, still mid-session at writing time**, was trading solidly higher — Dow, S&P, and Nasdaq all up roughly 0.6-1.6% intraday — a clean reversal of Wednesday's decline, helped by a stronger-than-expected jobless-claims print. **Oil extended its decline** (Brent ≈$102.94-$103.64, WTI ≈$100.34-$100.72, both down 2-2.7%), **gold spiked further to $4,370.58/oz (+2.50%)** on the strong labor data even as equities rallied, and **bitcoin held firm near $76,440 (+0.71%)**.

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** a genuine reversal from Wednesday — all three institutional investor categories (foreign, investment trust, dealer) turned net buyers (+NT$21.477 billion combined) on a session with strongly positive market breadth (832 advancers vs. 273 decliners), a contrast with Wednesday's rally-despite-selling pattern. TSMC (+1.89%) and UMC (+3.87%) led a genuinely broad, multi-sector advance. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** the headline Nikkei gain (+0.33%) undersells a real story underneath — the TOPIX's stronger +0.80% gain reflects a rotation from semiconductors (which "stalled" per local coverage) into value stocks, alongside a new, distinct headwind (rising AI-regulation discourse) flagged separately from the macro/rate narrative dominating the week. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** a genuine intraday round-trip — the KOSPI opened +0.91% on Fed relief, then gave it all back as foreign investors extended their selling streak to a seventh consecutive session (−₩2.1795 trillion), the week's largest single-day foreign outflow in this project's coverage. The KOSDAQ's broader, positive breadth (914 advancers vs. 706 decliners) stands in contrast to the large-cap KOSPI's flatness. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a stronger US dollar (following the Fed's hawkish messaging) hit commodity-linked names directly — gold-mining stocks (Zhufeng Gold −7%+) and energy (China National Petroleum −2%+) led declines, even as the automotive sector bucked the trend (Guangqi Group +7%+ on a FAW stake deal) and southbound Stock Connect logged a ninth consecutive day of net buying (>HK$3.0 billion). See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** broadly positive and, notably, finally showing some read-through from the "Fed hike digested calmly" theme visible across North Asia — Thailand's SET surged 1.32% on falling oil and Fed-relief, Indonesia's IHSG rose 0.40% on consumer/energy/tech strength, and Singapore's STI opened higher on bank-led buying, while Malaysia (back open after Wednesday's Malaysia Day closure) was the region's lone laggard on continued US-rate caution. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **UK/Europe:** a genuine relief rally — the STOXX 600 (confirmed 640.21, +0.5%) and FTSE/DAX/CAC (each estimated +0.4-0.6%) all rose as Europe, for the first time, got to trade on the actual FOMC outcome and treated it as a clearing event rather than a shock. The Bank of England's hold at 3.75% (6-3 vote, three dissents for a hike) despite UK inflation at a five-month high of 3.1% is a genuine policy divergence from the Fed's and ECB's more hawkish recent moves. Airlines and automakers led the day's sector rotation, a reversal of Wednesday's specific German-auto weak spot. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **United States (in progress):** early signs of a clean reversal from Wednesday's "sell the news" close — a stronger-than-expected jobless-claims print (196K vs. ~208K consensus) and the Fed decision now being a known, priced event rather than an overhang drove a broad risk-on move, with the Nasdaq's ≈+1.64% intraday gain far outpacing the Dow's ≈+0.62%, the mirror image of Wednesday. Chip-sector participation broadened notably (Intel, Micron, SanDisk all higher), a contrast with Wednesday's narrow Intel/SK-Hynix-only rally. Gold's continued strength even as equities rallied is a genuine cross-current worth watching into the close. See `usa.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** oil's multi-session decline continued (Brent/WTI both down another 2-2.7% intraday Thursday), a genuine, sustained de-escalation from the Middle East supply-disruption story; gold spiked further to $4,370.58/oz (+2.50%) on strong labor data even as risk assets broadly rallied, an unusual combination worth tracking for whether it resolves into a genuine "everything rally" or a sign some investors are still hedging; and bitcoin held firm near $76,440 (+0.71%), extending its resilience through the FOMC week.

## Oil

**Brent crude traded in a $102.94-$103.64/barrel range and WTI in a $100.34-$100.72/barrel range Thursday, both down roughly 2-2.7% on the day** as of the intraday reads captured for this file — a continuation of the multi-session decline that began with the Saudi East-West pipeline de-escalation story flagged repeatedly this week (see `../260916/README.md`). The decline provided a genuine cross-market tailwind Thursday, most visibly lifting European airline stocks (Ryanair +1.1%, Lufthansa +1.2%) and Thai banking/DELTA names, even as it weighed on Hong Kong energy names (China National Petroleum −2%+) via the stronger-dollar/costlier-dollar-commodity channel. Ongoing Strait of Hormuz tensions mean this remains a fluid, monitorable situation rather than a fully resolved one.

## Gold

**Spot gold traded at $4,370.58/oz, up 2.50% on the day**, extending Wednesday's already-elevated levels and getting a further boost from a stronger-than-expected US jobless-claims print (196,000 vs. ~208,000 consensus) — a genuinely notable move given it came on a day when US equities were also rallying broadly, an unusual "risk-on with gold also up sharply" combination worth tracking into Friday. Silver data was not available from the specific sources checked for this file (see `usa.md`); Wednesday's confirmed silver close was $64.92/oz (+1.66%). Notably, this equity-rally-plus-gold-price-rally combination sits in some tension with Hong Kong's gold-mining *equities*, which fell sharply (Zhufeng Gold −7%+) the same day on the Fed's dollar-strengthening dot-plot messaging — a genuine divergence between the metal's spot price and gold-mining stocks worth flagging rather than smoothing over (see `hongkong.md`).
