# Hong Kong Stock Market Analysis — September 17, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

The **Hang Seng Index fell 109.49 points (−0.44%) to close at 24,604.29**, giving back a portion of Wednesday's modest gain (see `../260916/hongkong.md`) as Hong Kong got its first chance to trade on the Fed's actual FOMC outcome — a 25bp hike to 3.75%-4.00% paired with a hawkish dot plot (see `../260916/usa.md`). The **Hang Seng TECH Index fell 14.71 points (−0.34%) to 4,310.74**, the **Mainland/China Enterprises Index dropped 31.01 points (−0.38%) to 8,175.36**, and the **Red Chip Index declined 37.35 points (−0.92%) to 4,040.85** — a broadly negative, if not dramatic, session across all the major sub-indices, a reversal from Wednesday's sector-rotation-driven modest gain.

## What drove the session

- **The Fed's hawkish dot-plot messaging strengthened the US dollar, weighing directly on commodity-linked names.** A stronger dollar makes dollar-denominated commodities costlier internationally, and this was the session's clearest, most direct read-through from Wednesday night's FOMC outcome.
- **Gold-mining stocks fell sharply** — **Zhufeng Gold (聚峰黃金) dropped over 7%** — a genuine reversal from Wednesday's broad gold-stock rally, tracking spot gold's own more complicated reaction (spot gold itself actually rose to $4,370.58/oz Thursday on strong US jobless-claims data, even as Hong Kong's gold *equities* sold off on the stronger-dollar/rate-hike read — see `../260917/README.md`'s Gold section for the apparent tension between the two).
- **Energy names weakened notably**, with **China National Petroleum (中石油) down more than 2%**, as the same stronger-dollar dynamic made oil costlier in dollar terms even as oil's own spot price continued falling (Brent −2.7% to ~$103, see `usa.md`).
- **Technology stocks were mixed but skewed negative**: **Meituan (美團) and Bilibili (嗶哩嗶哩) both dropped over 2%**, **Tencent (騰訊) and Alibaba (阿里巴巴) both fell beyond 1%**, while **Lenovo (聯想) bucked the trend, gaining over 1%** — a continuation of the megacap-internet weakness first flagged in Wednesday's session, now joined by broader tech softness.
- **The automotive sector was the session's clear bright spot, resilient against the broader weakness**: **Guangqi Group (廣汽集團) surged over 7%** after announcing an equity issuance to acquire a stake in a vehicle joint venture from FAW (一汽), making FAW its "second major strategic shareholder" — a genuine, dateable corporate-action catalyst distinct from the macro story driving the rest of the session.

## Money flow — 南向資金 (southbound Stock Connect)

**Southbound Stock Connect recorded net buying of more than HK$3.0 billion** on Thursday (Shanghai-linked flows: ~HK$1.862 billion; Shenzhen-linked flows: ~HK$1.147 billion) — extending the streak flagged in Wednesday's session (which was itself an eighth consecutive day of net buying) to what appears to be a **ninth consecutive session of southbound net buying**, based on the continuity of the streak reported through Wednesday. This continued mainland-investor demand for Hong Kong-listed names, even through Thursday's broadly negative session, is a notably persistent signal this project has now tracked for nearly two weeks.

## Bottom line

Thursday's −0.44% Hang Seng decline was Hong Kong's first chance to react to the Fed's actual hawkish dot-plot surprise, and the market's response was a broad, moderate pullback rather than a sharp sell-off — the stronger dollar that followed the Fed's hawkish messaging weighed most visibly on gold-mining and energy names, while megacap internet stocks extended a second straight day of weakness. Guangqi Group's 7%+ surge on a dateable corporate-action catalyst was the session's standout bright spot, and southbound Stock Connect's continued net buying (a ninth consecutive session) remains this project's most persistent, durable signal of underlying mainland demand even through a down session. Friday's session will be the one to watch for whether the megacap-tech weakness stabilizes or deepens into a more sustained rotation.
