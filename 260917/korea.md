# Korea Stock Market Analysis — September 17, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

The **KOSPI closed at 6,715.41, down 2.56 points (−0.04%)**, essentially flat after opening sharply higher at **6,779.02 (+0.91%)** and giving back the entire gain as the session wore on — a "hit first, then defended a narrow loss" pattern, per Money Today's own framing (**"'매' 먼저 맞은 코스피, 약보합 방어"**). The **KOSDAQ outperformed, rising 6.20 points (+0.76%) to 822.18**, with advancers (914) comfortably outpacing decliners (706) — a contrast with the large-cap KOSPI's essentially flat close. (One source, EBN, put the KOSPI close marginally higher at 6,716.72, −0.02% — an immaterial variance not requiring reconciliation.)

## What drove the session

- **Foreign investors extended their selling streak to a seventh consecutive session**, net selling **₩2.1795 trillion** — a continuation, not a new development, of the selling pressure already visible in Wednesday's session (see `../260916/korea.md`), but now stretched across a full week.
- **The session opened strongly (+0.91%) on relief that the Fed's hike itself was "as expected,"** but persistent foreign selling capped the advance and pulled the index back to essentially flat by the close — a genuine intraday reversal, not merely a quiet session.
- **Institutional investors (기관) turned modest net buyers (+₩141.8 billion)** and **individual investors (개인) were also net buyers (+₩333.7 billion)** — both providing some offset to the foreign selling, but not enough to hold the morning's gains.
- **Program trading recorded a net sell of ₩1.6719 trillion**, broadly tracking the foreign-selling pattern and reinforcing that Thursday's pullback from the highs was systematic/program-driven at least in part, not solely a discretionary foreign exit.
- **The won strengthened to roughly 1,381.70/USD**, and **US 10-year Treasury yields remained close to the 5% level** that has been a persistent overhang for Korean equities all week — the same macro backdrop cited across this project's coverage of Wednesday's FOMC outcome.
- **The KOSDAQ's stronger, broader advance (+0.76%, 914 advancers vs. 706 decliners)** stands in contrast to the KOSPI's large-cap-driven flatness, suggesting smaller-cap and domestic-demand-sensitive names held up better than the foreign-investor-exposed large caps Thursday.

Thursday's flow marks a genuine escalation from Wednesday: foreign selling nearly doubled in scale (₩2.1795T vs. Wednesday's ₩1.68T) even as Wednesday's institutional buying (+₩1.21T) flipped to a much smaller net buy (+₩141.8B) Thursday, and individuals — net sellers Wednesday — turned net buyers Thursday. The net effect was a session that opened on Fed-relief optimism but was steadily sold down by persistent, now week-long foreign outflows, leaving the index essentially flat rather than extending Wednesday's rally.

## Bottom line

Thursday's essentially flat KOSPI close (−0.04%) masks a genuine intraday round-trip: the index opened up nearly 1% on relief that the Fed's hike was "as expected," then gave back the entire gain as foreign investors extended their selling streak to a seventh consecutive session (−₩2.1795 trillion, the week's largest single-day foreign outflow in this project's coverage). Institutional and individual investors both turned modest net buyers, providing a partial offset, but not enough to prevent the pullback. The KOSDAQ's stronger, broader advance is a genuine bright spot, suggesting the foreign-selling pressure is concentrated in large-cap, KOSPI-listed names rather than the market as a whole. The seven-day foreign-selling streak is now this project's most persistent, trackable signal in Korea and the one to watch heading into Friday.
