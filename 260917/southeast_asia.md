# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 17, 2026 (Thursday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia was mixed-to-positive on Thursday as the region got its first chance to trade on the Fed's actual FOMC decision. **Thailand's SET surged 1.32%**, the region's standout gainer, on easing oil prices and relief that the Fed's hike came in as expected. **Indonesia's IHSG rose 0.40%** despite lingering global-rate-sensitivity concerns, while **Malaysia's Bursa Malaysia (back open after Wednesday's Malaysia Day holiday) slipped 0.26%** on continued US-rate-hike-related caution, and **Singapore's STI opened higher (+0.47%) on banking-sector strength**, tracking a reported +0.5% daily gain despite Wall Street's Wednesday pullback.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index opened at 5,662.06, up 26.65 points (+0.47%)**, led by banking counters — **DBS rose 0.68% to S$77.32**, with **UOB and OCBC Bank also gaining** — alongside strength in **Yangzijiang Shipbuilding, Singtel, and SGX**; among REITs, **CapitaLand Integrated Commercial Trust rose 0.44%**. A separate headline (BusinessToday Malaysia) put the day's move at **"STI Rises 0.5%"**, consistent with the opening figure, "despite Wall Street's Fed-fuelled retreat" on Wednesday. Market breadth at the point checked was roughly balanced-to-slightly-negative (69 gainers vs. 75 decliners on ~46.93 million shares traded), suggesting the gain was concentrated in large-cap banks and blue chips rather than broad-based. **A confirmed, same-day closing level for the STI was not available from sources checked at writing time** — a persistent gap this project has flagged nearly every session for Singapore; applying the reported +0.5% move to this project's own confirmed Wednesday close (5,635.41) implies a close near **5,663**, but this is a derived estimate, not a confirmed closing print.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,462.43, up 25.58 points (+0.40%)**, recovering from Wednesday's −0.38% decline even as the report itself noted the index gained "di tengah tekanan sentimen global" (amid global sentiment pressure) following the Fed's rate increase. **Consumer, energy, and technology sectors led gains**, while **healthcare, property, utilities, and raw materials declined**. Market breadth was positive, with **384 advancing issues against 244 decliners** (164 unchanged). Trading value came in at **Rp13.92 trillion** on **28.52 billion shares traded** across **1.86 million transactions**.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

With Bursa Malaysia back open after Wednesday's Malaysia Day holiday closure, the **FBM KLCI fell 4.47 points (−0.26%) to close at 1,674.74** — reconciling cleanly against this project's last confirmed close (1,679.21, from `../260915/southeast_asia.md`, since Wednesday's session did not trade). Analyst Kenneth Leong of Berjaya Research attributed the pressure to **"rising United States bond yields and the possibility of further monetary tightening by the US Federal Reserve."** Market breadth was negative, with **losers (673) outnumbering gainers (465)**. **Decliners included Hong Leong Bank (−36 sen to RM23.10), Petronas Chemicals (−34 sen), and AirAsia (−13.5 sen to 50.5 sen)**, while **Vitrox (+48 sen to RM9.58), Malaysian Pacific Industries (+40 sen), and Nestlé (+34 sen)** were among the session's gainers. The analyst flagged the index's approach to oversold territory as a potential draw for bargain hunters, with upcoming Bank of Japan and Malaysian inflation data cited as near-term watch items.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index surged 20.61 points (+1.32%) to close at 1,583.34**, on trading value of **฿62.46 billion**, decisively reversing Wednesday's −1.01% decline (see `../260916/southeast_asia.md`). The rally was attributed to **"declining oil prices and the Federal Reserve raising policy rates as anticipated"** — a genuinely different driver from Wednesday's domestic-policy-specific weakness (diesel pricing, tourism-stimulus delay). **DELTA and major banking stocks led the buying interest**, while **PTTEP faced selling pressure**, consistent with the broader region's energy-sector softness on Thursday's stronger US dollar.

## Bottom line

Thursday was a broadly constructive session for Southeast Asia, with Thailand's SET the standout on falling oil prices and a "hike as expected" relief trade, and Indonesia posting a genuine recovery from Wednesday's decline on strength in consumer, energy, and technology names. Malaysia, back open after Wednesday's holiday closure, was the region's one clear laggard, extending the same US-rate-hike caution that has weighed on the market for over a week. Singapore's session showed a positive open concentrated in banking names, but — consistent with this project's now-recurring documented gap — a confirmed same-day closing level was not available from sources checked, and the ≈5,663 figure above should be read as a derived estimate rather than a confirmed print. Notably, unlike Wednesday's session (which showed little read-through from the North Asian semiconductor rally), Thursday's Southeast Asia session tracked the same broad "Fed hike digested calmly" theme visible in Taiwan, Japan, and (to a lesser extent) Korea.
