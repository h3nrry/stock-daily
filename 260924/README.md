# Daily Market Briefing — September 24, 2026 (Thursday)

**Summit day, with rates still in charge.** Xi Jinping arrived in Washington, and the **US and China extended their trade truce by two months to Jan 10, 2027**. Markets barely reacted: **US yields at their highest since 2007 (10Y ≈5.11–5.15%)**, Brent back above $103, and hawkish central banks (Norges Bank hiked; the Fed is priced ~65–75% for an October hike) set the tone. **Japan reopened after its three-day holiday**, and the **Nikkei rose 0.76% to 65,513.99** (4th straight gain) on chip-equipment strength. But it kept only ~27% of the holiday-futures premium, and **TOPIX fell 0.39%**. **Taiwan fell 0.28% to 48,024.60** on its last session before a four-day holiday. **Institutions sold NT$44.4 billion**, ending a five-day foreign buying streak, though the TAIEX still gained **+1.79% on the week**. **Hong Kong −0.29%** (oil stocks up; memory and gold stocks down after reports that **Michael Burry had increased his Micron short**). **All four Southeast Asian markets fell**, led by **Indonesia −1.20%**. **Korea was closed** (Chuseok). **Europe was mixed** (FTSE ≈+0.2%, DAX/CAC/STOXX lower, near-close), and **the US session is still open**: at the time of writing it was lower for a second day, with Oracle −6% on a data-center force-majeure notice.

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** **de-risking before the holiday.** Foreign −NT$32.97bn and trusts −NT$12.82bn (total −NT$44.45bn) as turnover fell to NT$736.6bn. Money rotated *within* tech into **MediaTek (record NT$5,285, +1.93%)**, AI cooling (Auras +8%, Jentech +6%) and substrate/CCL (Kinsus +6%, ITEQ limit-up), while TSMC fell 1%. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** buying concentrated in **Advantest, Tokyo Electron, SoftBank Group, TDK, Fanuc, HOYA**; selling in domestic defensives and trading houses (Recruit, Toyota, KDDI, Mitsui & Co.). More stocks fell (838) than rose (674). The BOJ's 1.25% rate took effect; yen ≈158.2. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** closed. Monday Sept 28 will catch up on the US rate shock, the truce extension and the Micron run-up. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a rotation **into energy (PetroChina +2%)** and **out of memory (GigaDevice −5%, on Burry's reported Micron short) and gold miners (−8%)**. Southbound flow for the day was not confirmed. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** **US yields and the dollar hit emerging markets**. Indonesia had 487 decliners vs 198 advancers, and Malaysia saw foreign outflows. Singapore was the most resilient. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **UK/Europe (preliminary):** **defensive rotation.** Staples and personal care rose; tech and industrials fell. London's energy and staples weighting kept the FTSE positive. Norges Bank hiked 25bp. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **United States (in progress):** out of AI-infrastructure credit risk (Oracle, Blue Owl, Bloom Energy) and chips (Arm, Intel), into cybersecurity (PANW, CRWD ~+5%) and select earnings winners. Small caps remain under rate pressure. See `usa.md`.

## Oil

**Oil kept rising for a second day after Wednesday's +3.9% rebound.** Brent traded around **$104.70 (+1.5%)** and WTI around **$93–94.7** in US morning trading (TheStreet, Schwab), after **aggressive Iranian statements about controlling the Strait of Hormuz** (Newsquawk). ## US–China summit (Trump–Xi, Washington)

- **Trade truce ("Busan Agreement") extended two months to Jan 10, 2027** (Bessent). The original deal from October 2025 was due to expire in November.
- Schedule: Oval Office bilateral 11:30am ET, expanded meeting at noon, **state dinner with tech and banking CEOs on Sept 25**.
- Agenda: trade (agriculture, low-tech consumer goods, possible tariff relief; average US tariff on Chinese goods ~23%), technology, **Taiwan**, and **China's support for Iran**. Trump said he expects no progress on AI guardrails.
- Market read: **so far, a non-event for prices.** Asian markets had priced "no expectations" (Sing Tao). Watch for chip export-control statements, which matter most for Taiwan and Korea when they reopen.
