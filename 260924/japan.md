# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 24, 2026 (Thursday) — reopening after the three-day holiday

## Headline

Tokyo reopened after the Silver Week holiday (Sept 21–23), and the **Nikkei 225 rose 495.04 yen (+0.76%) to 65,513.99**, its **fourth straight gain** (Sept 16, 17, 18, 24). But it held only part of what holiday futures had priced. The index touched **66,249.11** in the morning (back above 66,000 for the first time since Sept 8), then faded as profit-taking set in (戻り待ちの売り). **TOPIX fell 15.84 points (−0.39%) to 4,075.30**, and **more stocks fell (838) than rose (674)**. The broad market was weaker than the headline index: a few chip-equipment heavyweights lifted the price-weighted Nikkei. Prime-market turnover was **¥9.2 trillion** on 2.49 billion shares. **USD/JPY ≈158.2.**

## The holiday-futures gap: priced in, then largely lost

Osaka holiday futures had settled at **66,820 on Sept 23**, **1,801 yen above** Friday's cash close (see `../260923/japan.md`). The cash index peaked about 1,230 yen higher in the morning and **closed only 495 yen higher, keeping about 27% of the futures gap**. The reason: the holiday futures stopped trading **before** Wednesday's US rate shock (10Y at a 2007 high, Nasdaq −1.13%; see `../260923/usa.md`). The same pattern appears in this project's notes on Japan holidays: thin holiday-session futures volume can overstate the eventual move in cash stocks.

## What drove the session

- **The US AI rally during the holiday, led by Meta (Nikkei: "メタ発の買い波及").** Chip and electronic-parts stocks led: **Advantest (アドバンテスト, 6857) +1,010 yen**, **Tokyo Electron (東京エレクトロン, 8035) +810 yen**, **SoftBank Group (ソフトバンクG, 9984)**, **TDK (6762)**, **Fanuc (ファナック, 6954)**, **HOYA (7741) +535 yen** and **Nikon (ニコン, 7731) +112.5 yen**. Strongest sectors: precision machinery, electrical equipment, rubber, glass/ceramics, chemicals.
- **Rising rates held back the rest of the market** (Nikkei: "AI成長期待と金利高綱引き", AI growth hopes vs higher rates). Expectations of more US hikes and higher Japanese long-term yields weighed on **Recruit (リクルートHD, 6098)**, **Toyota (トヨタ自動車, 7203)**, **Mitsui & Co. (三井物産, 8031)**, **KDDI (9433)** and **Daiichi Sankyo (第一三共, 4568)**. Services and utilities lagged.
- **The BOJ's 25bp hike to 1.25% took effect today** (decided Sept 18, 7–2 vote; see `../260921/japan.md`). Even with a higher BOJ rate, the yen stayed weak near **158.2** because US yields are rising faster, and intervention risk remains on traders' minds.
- **Trump–Xi summit:** Wealth Advisor's afternoon wrap said caution increased ahead of the US-China meeting (米中首脳会談控え様子見姿勢強まる), which limited further gains.

## Money flow

JPX's weekly investor-type data for the week containing Sept 24 will not be published until the following week. The day's price action shows buying concentrated in chip equipment and AI names, with selling in domestic defensives and trading houses. Holders cut positions after a strong three-day futures run, which is what you'd expect after a holiday.

## Bottom line

Japan's reopening **matched the direction of the holiday futures but not their size**. The Nikkei rose 0.76% to 65,513.99, keeping only ~27% of the 1,801-yen futures premium, and TOPIX fell 0.39% as most stocks declined. AI and chip-equipment stocks kept the headline index up while the BOJ's 1.25% policy rate took effect and US yields hit 2007 highs. With the yen near 158 and the Trump–Xi summit under way, Friday's session will show whether the chip-led gains hold.
