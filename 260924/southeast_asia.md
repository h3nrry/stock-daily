# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 24, 2026 (Thursday)

**Summary:** **All four markets fell** as Wednesday's US rate shock (10Y ≈5.1%, a 2007 high) and the oil rebound reached the region. **Indonesia fell hardest (−1.20%)**, giving back most of Wednesday's BI-hold rally. **Thailand −0.50%**, **Malaysia −0.25%** (a second straight drop, with foreign outflows), and **Singapore held up best (−0.09%)**.

A near-flat session. The level is shown, and the drivers are left unconfirmed.*

**Drivers (CNBC Indonesia, Sindonews, Okezone):** selling from the open on **the US 10-year at 5.11%**, expectations of more Fed hikes after strong US PMIs, and a **stronger dollar pressuring emerging markets**. Bank Indonesia's Wednesday hold at 5.75% had already been priced in. Wednesday's +1.56% bounce was mostly reversed.

## Thailand — SET

| Item | Reading |
|---|---|
| SET close (Sept 24) | **1,602.58, −8.12 points (−0.50%)** |
| Value traded | THB 68.53 billion |

**Drivers (Kaohoon):** investors "shifted into a risk-off mode" on higher oil, high bond yields, and **talk of US diesel export restrictions** (which would raise inflation but help refiners). Thailand also released its **first national strategy for semiconductors and advanced electronics**, covering chip R&D, manufacturing and packaging, a long-term positive for the DELTA/electronics theme. The US–China truce extension and Xi's arrival in Washington left sentiment for the next session "uncertain."

## Stocks/sectors to watch

| Market / Sector | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Indonesia (banks, commodities) | Most sensitive to US yields and the dollar | Further weakness if the US 10Y stays above 5.1% |
| Thailand refiners and electronics | Diesel-export talk; new semiconductor strategy | The White House denied the diesel ban, so the headline may not hold |
| Malaysia | Foreign outflows; petrochemicals now helped by oil | Rates pressure on yield stocks |
| Singapore | Most resilient in the region | High-dividend REITs if yields keep rising |

## Bottom line

Thursday brought **the US rate shock to Southeast Asia**: all four markets fell, led by **Indonesia (−1.20%, 487 decliners)**. Singapore barely moved (−0.09%). The Trump–Xi truce extension did not lift regional stocks, which are trading on US yields and the dollar more than on trade headlines right now.
