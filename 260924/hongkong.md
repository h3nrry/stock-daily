# Hong Kong Stock Market Analysis — September 24, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

The **Hang Seng Index fell 0.29% to 24,761.13**, a second straight decline but a much smaller one than Wednesday's −1.01%. The index had been down 0.52% at midday. The **Hang Seng Tech Index fell 0.41%** (−1.11% at midday) and the **H-share (China Enterprises) index fell 0.09%**. **Oil stocks rose against the trend** after Brent's settlement moved back above $103. **Memory-chip and gold stocks led the decline**: memory on reports that short-seller **Michael Burry had increased his short position in Micron** and warned of a cyclical "violent sell-off", and gold after hawkish Fed comments. This was the day of the **Trump–Xi summit in Washington** and the **US–China truce extension to Jan 10, 2027**.

## What drove the session

- **Energy:** **PetroChina (中石油, 0857) rose more than 2%**. Sina: "布伦特原油结算价重上103美元，终结此前连续五日的下跌走势" (Brent settled back above $103, ending a five-day losing streak).
- **Memory chips led the decline:** **GigaDevice (兆易创新) fell more than 5%** after reports that Burry had added to his Micron short and warned that a cycle turn would bring a "剧烈抛售" (violent sell-off). This hits the memory rally just before Micron's Sept 30 earnings.
- **Gold stocks fell:** **Zhufeng Gold (珠峰黄金) −8%+**, as a Fed governor's hawkish signal strengthened expectations that high rates will last (gold spot below $4,300).
- **Tech was mixed:** **Lenovo (联想, 0992) and Baidu (百度, 9888) −2%+**, **Bilibili (哔哩哔哩) −1%+**; **Xiaomi (小米, 1810) and NetEase (网易, 9999) +1%+** rebounded after Wednesday's selloff.
- **The summit was not the main driver.** On Wednesday, Sing Tao's quoted expert said the market had "no expectations" for the Xi–Trump meeting. The morning's truce-extension news ("Busan Agreement" extended two months, per Bessent) did not lift the index.

## Bottom line

Hong Kong **slipped 0.29% to 24,761.13** on summit day, less than half of Wednesday's drop. Oil stocks rose, and memory and gold stocks fell (Burry's reported Micron short; a hawkish Fed). The US–China truce extension was known before the session and did little for prices, which fits the view that the market expected little from the summit.
