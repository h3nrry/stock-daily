# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 24, 2026 (Thursday) — NEAR-CLOSE / PRELIMINARY

> **Status: preliminary.** European cash markets closed at 17:30 CET (23:30 Taipei). This file was written about 10 minutes later, when vendor pages still showed late-session readings rather than confirmed official closes. The levels below are **near-close readings, shown as ranges**, and need a confirmation pass, as on Sept 21 (see `../260921/uk_europe.md`). **Europe stayed mixed and defensive:** the **FTSE 100 was the only major index up (≈+0.2%)**, helped by energy and consumer staples. The **DAX (≈−0.1% to −0.45%)**, **CAC 40 (≈−0.3% to −0.4%)** and **STOXX 600 (≈−0.2%)** extended Wednesday's rate-driven losses. This was a heavy central-bank day: **Norges Bank hiked**, the **SNB and Riksbank held**, and **German Ifo beat expectations**.

## What drove the market

- **Central banks (Newsquawk):** **Norges Bank raised rates 25bp to 4.50%**. **The SNB held at 0.00%** and softened its FX-intervention wording (from "increased willingness" to its standard willingness). **The Riksbank held at 1.75%** but projected hikes starting later this year. The SNB and Riksbank held, but both leaned hawkish, and **Norway hiked**, matching the global trend of tighter policy.
- **German Ifo business climate: 89.9 vs 89.0 expected**, another data beat. As wallstreet-online put it, strong economic data are increasingly read as a reason for more rate hikes rather than a support for stocks ("Konjunktur als Doppelschwert").
- **Rates and oil:** US 30-year yields hit 20-year highs; USD/JPY moved back toward 160 (Newsquawk, wallstreet-online). **Brent rose above $100–104** after aggressive Iranian statements about controlling the Strait of Hormuz (Newsquawk: Brent $100.94, WTI $94.69 at the time). One German wire printed Brent at $101.50 (−1.86%), which contradicts all other sources, and is noted but not used.
- **Defensive rotation:** Food, Beverage & Tobacco and Personal Care led; Tech and Industrials lagged (Newsquawk). In London, **Shell +0.8%, BP +1.2%, Unilever +0.8%, BAT +1.9%** (Trading Economics). "Cyclicals were avoided while defensive sectors were in demand" (wallstreet-online).
- **H&M −0.6%** despite a profit beat, citing supply-chain challenges.
- **Trump–Xi:** the US–China truce extension to Jan 10, 2027 had little effect on European trading.
