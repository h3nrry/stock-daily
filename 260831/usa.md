# USA Stock Market Analysis — August 31, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

US indices opened the new week lower as **oil prices surged past $90/barrel WTI (Brent ~$93.03) following weekend US military strikes on Iranian targets** (including Iran's Larak Island near the Strait of Hormuz), reviving inflation and rate-path anxiety just three days after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech. **Dow Jones closed around 53,186 (−0.70%)**, **S&P 500 around 7,686 (−0.33%)**, and the **Nasdaq Composite around 26,371 (−0.12%)** — tech held up best, while energy-sensitive and rate-sensitive sectors lagged.

*Note on sourcing: outlets diverged on exact closing levels for this session — Washington Post reported Dow 53,885.10/S&P 7,711.76/Nasdaq 26,402.42, while 247wallst reported Dow 52,820.80/S&P 7,645.80. All sources agree on the direction (modestly lower) and the driver (oil/Iran). Figures above are drawn from Fool.com's report, chosen because they reconcile most consistently with Friday Aug 28's confirmed close (Dow 53,598.14).*

## The weekend's defining event: US strikes on Iran

Over the weekend, US forces struck Iranian military targets — reported strikes hit Iranian rocket launchers and, per CNBC, targets near **Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz** — reigniting the Iran conflict that has periodically roiled oil markets throughout 2026. The strait is a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments, and the market's reflex was immediate: **Brent crude jumped to $93.03/barrel (+$1.06)** and **WTI climbed above $90/barrel**, its highest levels in weeks. The renewed hostilities compound an already-hawkish macro backdrop: Fed Chair Warsh's Friday remarks that "the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices" had already pushed the market-implied probability of a September rate cut down sharply; the oil spike adds a fresh inflationary impulse on top of that.

## Market reaction

- **Energy and consumer-defensive stocks were the session's relative winners**, the natural beneficiaries of higher oil prices and a flight to safety.
- **Growth/tech held up better than the Dow's old-economy, energy-sensitive names** — the Nasdaq's smaller decline reflects that split.
- **US Treasury yields extended their post-Jackson Hole rise**: the **10-year yield pushed above 4.75%, its highest level since January 2025**, as markets priced both persistent inflation risk (oil) and a more hawkish Fed reaction function. The CME FedWatch-tracked probability of a 25bp September rate hike (not cut) rose sharply through the day, reported at points between 57.5% and 66.4% depending on the exact snapshot — a genuine reversal from the market's earlier-August expectation of a rate *cut*.
- **Gold and bitcoin both fell** despite the risk-off/inflation backdrop, as rising real yields outweighed their traditional "geopolitical hedge" and "inflation hedge" appeal — see the README's cross-asset section.

## What to watch next

1. **Whether the Iran situation escalates further or de-escalates** — the single biggest swing factor for oil, and by extension for the inflation/rate-path narrative driving both bonds and equities.
2. **Confirmation of the September rate-hike (not cut) repricing** — a genuine narrative shift from earlier in August, worth tracking via CME FedWatch as the week progresses.
3. **The approaching September jobs report and other early-September economic data**, which the market will read against this newly hawkish backdrop.

## Bottom line

Monday's session was a straightforward geopolitical-and-macro one-two punch: weekend US strikes on Iran sent oil sharply higher, compounding Friday's already-hawkish Jackson Hole tone and pushing the 10-year Treasury yield above 4.75% — its highest since January 2025 — while US equities gave back a modest amount of ground, with old-economy/energy-sensitive names (Dow) underperforming growth/tech (Nasdaq). The rate-cut narrative that dominated much of August has clearly cracked; markets are now pricing meaningfully higher odds of a September *hike*. Whether this is a durable regime shift or a geopolitics-driven overreaction is the key question heading into the rest of the week.
