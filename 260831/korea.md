# Korea Stock Market Analysis — August 31, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

**KOSPI closed at 6,820.02, up 31.14 points (+0.46%)** — a modest gain that puts the index back above the 6,800 level following Friday's sharp 1.79% selloff (see `../260828/korea.md`). Korea's benchmark opened the week digesting the same global headwinds as its regional peers — Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone and the weekend's US strikes on Iran pushing oil higher — but found enough buying support to post a modest recovery rather than extend Friday's decline.

## Context

Friday's session had been driven by a concentrated wave of foreign and institutional selling in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix ahead of the Jackson Hole speech. Monday's modest rebound suggests that selling pressure did not extend into the new week at the same intensity — consistent with the earlier read that Friday's move was pre-event de-risking in Korea's two largest, most AI-sensitive names rather than a broader trend change. Detailed foreign/institutional/individual investor flow figures for this specific session were not available among sources checked at writing time; KOSDAQ's same-day level was also not confirmed in the sources reviewed.

## Bottom line

A modest, unspectacular recovery day: KOSPI added back roughly a quarter of Friday's sharp loss, holding the psychologically important 6,800 level even as the rest of the region digested a fresh oil-price shock from the weekend's Iran strikes. With Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix having absorbed the brunt of Friday's institutional selling, Monday's stabilization is a reasonable, if unconfirmed-in-detail, sign that the de-risking wave did not deepen into the new week.
