# Daily Market Briefing — August 31, 2026 (Monday)

Global snapshot: markets opened the new week absorbing a genuine geopolitical shock — **weekend US military strikes on Iranian targets** (including near Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz) sent **oil prices sharply higher (Brent to $93.03/barrel, WTI above $90)**, reviving inflation and rate-path anxiety just three days after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech. The result was a broadly risk-off day, but with a genuinely interesting technical counter-current: **MSCI's Taiwan (and regional) weighting increase took effect after Monday's close**, and passive-fund buying tied to that rebalancing helped several Asian markets — Taiwan, Japan, Thailand — claw back much or all of their intraday losses. **US indices fell modestly (Dow ~−0.7%, S&P ~−0.3%, Nasdaq ~−0.1%)**, **Taiwan's TAIEX dropped −0.44%** after a dramatic 880-point intraday round trip, **Japan's Nikkei nearly erased a 1,500-point plunge to close just −0.14%**, and **Korea's KOSPI actually rose +0.46%**, recovering a slice of Friday's sharp selloff. See `usa.md`, `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, and `uk_europe.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

**Reading the table:** the standout story is the divergence between the US/Europe reaction (straightforwardly risk-off on the Iran/oil shock) and several Asian markets' V-shaped intraday recoveries (Taiwan, Japan) driven by MSCI-rebalancing flows landing on the same day as the geopolitical shock — pure coincidence of timing, but it materially cushioned the region's reaction versus what a similar shock might have produced without that technical tailwind. Korea's outright gain is notable as a partial reversal of Friday's sharp, semiconductor-concentrated selloff. Watch whether Tuesday (Sept 1) shows Asian markets giving back the MSCI-flow cushion now that the rebalancing flows are a one-off, already-spent tailwind.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** weekend US strikes on Iran sent oil sharply higher (Brent $93.03, WTI >$90), pushing the 10-year Treasury yield above 4.75% (highest since January 2025) and reviving September rate-*hike* odds just days after the market had been pricing a cut. Equities fell modestly, with old-economy/energy-sensitive names (Dow) underperforming growth/tech (Nasdaq). See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** a volatile session — an 880-point intraday round trip, closing −0.44% — with **foreign investors net selling a modest NT$14.39B** (a sharp comedown from Friday's record NT$366B buying spree), while MSCI-linked passive buying cushioned the close. Petrochemicals (+3.51%, oil-driven) and shipping were the standout sectors; semiconductors broadly lagged. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** a 1,500-point intraday plunge nearly fully erased by the close, aided by ~¥220 billion in MSCI-linked flows and systematic trend-following buying; TOPIX extended its win streak to eight sessions on domestic-demand-sector strength even as Nikkei-heavy semiconductor names (Advantest, Tokyo Electron) lagged. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** a modest +0.46% recovery from Friday's sharp, Samsung/SK Hynix-concentrated selloff; detailed same-day investor-flow figures were not confirmed among sources checked. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a quiet −0.22% decline, notably underperforming mainland Shanghai's +0.86% gain — an offshore/onshore divergence worth watching into September. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Thailand's SET rose +0.44% on the same MSCI-rebalancing dynamic supporting Taiwan and Japan, after an early dip on Fed-driven caution; Singapore, Indonesia, and Malaysia closing data were not confirmed for this date. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** a straightforwardly risk-off session on the Iran/oil shock — Germany's DAX (−1.17%) and France's CAC 40 (−0.79%) both fell, while London's energy/defensive tilt left the FTSE 100 roughly flat. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** **gold fell** despite the risk-off backdrop, as rising real yields (10-year >4.75%) outweighed its usual safe-haven appeal — futures opened near $4,498.70/oz but slid toward $4,432 by mid-morning US trading. **Bitcoin also declined**, opening around $77,695 (−0.7% from Sunday) and drifting to roughly $77,999 by mid-morning, pressured by the same higher-rate-expectations dynamic. **Oil was the day's clear standout mover**: Brent crude +$1.06 to $93.03/barrel, WTI above $90, both driven directly by the weekend's Iran strikes.
