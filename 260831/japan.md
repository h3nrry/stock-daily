# Japan Stock Market Analysis — August 31, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

**Nikkei 225 closed at 66,311.93, down 93.63 points (−0.14%)** — a modest headline decline that masks a genuinely wild intraday session. The index **plunged more than 1,500 points in early trading** as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone (from Friday) and the weekend's US strikes on Iran hit global risk sentiment, but **recovered almost the entire drop by the close** on systematic trend-following buying and roughly ¥220 billion in MSCI index-related rebalancing flows. The **TOPIX gained 0.23%, its eighth consecutive day of gains**, showing the broader market held up notably better than the headline-grabbing Nikkei swing suggests.

## What drove the market

- **Early sharp selloff**: the index opened the week absorbing Friday's hawkish Fed signal and fresh Iran-driven oil-price gains, sending it down over 1,500 points at the low.
- **Semiconductor weakness**: **Advantest, Tokyo Electron, and SoftBank Group** all came under pressure as rising US Treasury yields reduced the relative appeal of long-duration growth stocks with distant earnings.
- **Afternoon recovery**: systematic trend-following (CTA-style) buying plus an estimated **¥220 billion in MSCI-linked flows** (tied to the same rebalancing affecting Taiwan — see `taiwan.md`) drove a recovery of nearly 1,500 points from the intraday low.
- **Broader resilience**: automakers, banks, utilities, and other domestic-demand sectors offset the semiconductor decline, extending TOPIX's win streak to eight sessions.
- **Currency**: the yen held near **¥160/dollar**, a level that supports exporters' earnings translation but also raises import costs and adds to inflation pressure — a genuinely two-sided effect for Japanese equities right now.

## Bottom line

A session that looked calm at the closing bell (Nikkei −0.14%) but was anything but calm intraday — a 1,500-point plunge followed by an almost-complete recovery, driven by systematic buying and MSCI-linked flows rather than a genuine sentiment shift. TOPIX's eighth straight gain, powered by domestic-demand sectors, is arguably the more informative read on underlying market health than the more volatile, semiconductor-heavy Nikkei. With the yen sitting near ¥160 and US yields still climbing post-Jackson-Hole, Japan's equity market enters September with a genuine tug-of-war between exporter-friendly currency weakness and higher-global-yield headwinds for growth stocks.
