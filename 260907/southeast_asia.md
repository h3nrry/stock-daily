# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 7, 2026 (Monday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia had another genuinely mixed session to start the week: Thailand and Malaysia both advanced on regional strength and AI-linked buying, while Singapore and Indonesia both pulled back modestly, with the fresh Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz tanker strikes over the weekend a live undercurrent across all four markets even where it didn't dominate the headline move.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,792.28, down roughly 9.68 points (−0.17%)**, a modest pullback from Friday's fresh record close of 5,801.96 (see `../260904/southeast_asia.md`). No same-day narrative article specific to Monday's session could be confirmed from sources checked at writing time; the modest decline is consistent with the region's live Iran/oil-risk undercurrent (which pressured Indonesia more directly the same session) rather than any Singapore-specific story identified. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+0.64% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **+0.66% over roughly 20 trading days (~1 month, measured from Aug 11 since Aug 10 data wasn't available in the series checked)**.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,619.67, down 16.80 points (−0.25%)**, extending Friday's decline for a second straight session. The pullback was driven by **profit-taking on big-cap and LQ45 constituents**, with **ESSA (−2.13%), MAPI (−1.75%), and INCO (−1.21%)** among the largest losers. **Foreign investors were significant net sellers in large-cap financials**, particularly **BBCA and BBRI**. Not every sector fell, though: **BUMI, PTBA, and AADI** — energy and commodity names — advanced, a sectoral rotation pattern echoing Friday's session (see `../260904/southeast_asia.md`) where commodity names also outperformed a falling broader index. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+1.44% over 5 trading days** and **+4.00% over 20 trading days**, continuing its position as the region's standout longer-horizon gainer even amid two straight down sessions.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,714.79, up 6.69 points (+0.39%)**, snapping Friday's pullback (see `../260904/southeast_asia.md`). **Banking and utility heavyweights led the gains**: **PETRONAS Gas (+44 sen to RM17.70)**, **Kuala Lumpur Kepong (+24 sen to RM22.50)**, and **Tenaga Nasional (+22 sen to RM13.84)**. Market breadth was actually negative despite the index gain — **610 decliners versus 488 advancers** — on volume of **3.6 billion units worth RM2.46 billion**, a similar "narrow, large-cap-led" pattern to several other markets this session. Dealers cited **broadly positive regional sentiment, led by South Korea's and Japan's strong gains**, as a supporting factor, alongside continued monitoring of crude oil price movements. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−0.64% over 5 trading days** (measured against Aug 28's close, since Aug 31 was Malaysia's Merdeka Day holiday) and **−1.19% over 20 trading days**.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,618.82, up 23.24 points (+1.46%)**, on trading value of **THB 60.08 billion** — the region's second-strongest gainer of the day after Korea. **Buying interest in DELTA (Delta Electronics Thailand)** led the rally, tied to global AI/data-center enthusiasm following **OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra launch** — the same catalyst cited for Korea's semiconductor surge the same session. **Foreign buying in the energy and telecommunications sectors** provided additional support. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+1.48% over 5 trading days** and **−0.34% over 20 trading days**.

## Bottom line

A genuinely split session for a second straight trading day: Thailand and Malaysia advanced — Thailand on the same AI/data-center enthusiasm (OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra) that drove Korea's much larger rally, Malaysia on banking/utility strength and positive regional sentiment despite negative internal breadth — while Singapore and Indonesia both pulled back modestly, Indonesia specifically on big-cap profit-taking and foreign selling in financials even as commodity names bucked the decline. Indonesia remains the region's standout longer-horizon performer (+4.00% over 20 trading days) despite two consecutive down sessions, a reminder that day-to-day moves and the underlying trend can diverge meaningfully.
