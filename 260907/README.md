# Daily Market Briefing — September 7, 2026 (Monday)

*Note: this replaces the pre-market outlook originally published for this date once markets opened; that outlook's central calls are checked against the actual session in the "vs. Monday's pre-market outlook" section below.*

Global snapshot: Monday was defined by one fact above all others — **US equity and bond markets were fully closed for Labor Day**, removing the usual American anchor from the day's cross-market picture. Elsewhere, Asia-Pacific delivered the day's most dramatic action: Korea's KOSPI **surged +4.61%** to just under 7,000 on a fresh AI/memory-demand catalyst (OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra model, Nvidia's Hugging Face acquisition), Japan's Nikkei **jumped +2.12%** to extend Friday's rebound, and Taiwan's TAIEX **rallied +1.66%** to a fresh one-month high on a record NT$113.865 billion institutional net-buy day — fully validating the bullish overnight-futures signal flagged in Friday's pre-market outlook for this date. Hong Kong was the region's outlier, **falling −0.93%** as Friday's bank and internet-platform leaders reversed sharply even as optical/hardware names and Stock Connect flows rallied. Southeast Asia was split again: Thailand (+1.46%) and Malaysia (+0.39%) advanced, Singapore (−0.17%) and Indonesia (−0.25%) pulled back modestly. Europe traded in a narrow, directionless range (FTSE −0.06%, DAX −0.16%, CAC +0.33%/+0.55%, STOXX 600 roughly flat) with no US market open to react against. The weekend's Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation — fresh US tanker strikes Saturday and Iranian retaliation — was the live undercurrent nearly everywhere: **Brent crude pushed to roughly $98, its highest level since late July**, gold consolidated modestly lower (~$4,423.69), and bitcoin extended a mild weekend pullback into the $78,000–80,000 range.

**Reading the table:** Monday's clearest story is the split between Asia's strength and everywhere else's quiet — Korea, Japan, and Taiwan all rallied more than 1.5%, while Hong Kong fell nearly 1% on a sector-rotation story that ran opposite to Friday's own leadership. Southeast Asia stayed genuinely mixed for a second straight session. With the US closed, Europe had no anchor and traded within half a percent of flat across the board. Korea's KOSPI is now up over 11% on a 20-day view — by far the most extreme number in the table — a reminder of just how sharp this month's regional whipsaw has been in both directions.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** equity and bond markets were fully closed for Labor Day — no Dow/S&P/Nasdaq close, Treasury yield, or credit-spread reading to report. Overnight futures pointed to a mixed, tech-tilted Tuesday open (Dow futures −0.57%, Nasdaq-100 futures +0.49%), with Micron, Oracle, Intel, and Bloom Energy all higher in pre-market/futures trading. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** a genuinely institutionally-backed rally — TAIEX +1.66% to a fresh one-month high of 47,326.27, on a record NT$113.865 billion net institutional buy (the largest single-day figure tracked in this project), led by MediaTek (+7.81%) and TSMC (+2.07%). Largan Precision's limit-down on a CPO-narrative downgrade was the day's clearest divergence. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** the region's biggest mover — KOSPI +4.61% to just under 7,000 on a fresh AI/memory-demand catalyst (OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, Nvidia's Hugging Face acquisition), with foreign and institutional investors together buying roughly ₩4.9 trillion even as individual investors sold ₩6.525 trillion into the rally. SK Hynix (+8.26%) led. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** the region's outlier — Hang Seng −0.93% as domestic banks and Friday's internet/AI leaders (Baidu, Meituan, Vanke) all reversed sharply, even as optical/PCB hardware names surged and Groupcore Technology jumped nearly 30% on Stock Connect inclusion. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Japan:** a second straight strong but narrow session — Nikkei +2.12%, extending Friday's rebound, but breadth stayed negative (885 decliners vs. 633 advancers on the TSE Prime Market) for a second day running, with SoftBank Group again the single largest driver. See `japan.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** genuinely split for a second straight session — Thailand (+1.46%) and Malaysia (+0.39%) advanced on regional strength and AI-linked buying (DELTA, banking/utility names), while Singapore (−0.17%) and Indonesia (−0.25%) pulled back modestly, Indonesia specifically on big-cap profit-taking and foreign selling in financials (BBCA, BBRI). See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** a quiet, digestive session with no US market open to react against — FTSE, DAX, CAC, and STOXX 600 all traded within roughly half a percent of flat, absorbing the weekend's Iran/oil escalation without a clear directional resolution. See `uk_europe.md`.

## Oil

**Brent crude pushed to roughly $97.08–$97.93 during the session — its highest level since July 23 — and WTI rose to around $92.41–$93.10, its highest since July**, as the weekend's fresh US-Iran tanker strikes (Saturday, Sept 5) and Iranian retaliation kept the Strait of Hormuz risk fully live, exactly as flagged as a two-sided wildcard in Friday's pre-market outlook. With US markets closed, some trading activity migrated to decentralized crypto-exchange venues (Hyperliquid) for oil-linked contracts. See `usa.md`.

## Gold

**Spot gold pulled back to around $4,423.69**, down roughly 1.25% from Friday's $4,479.85 close — a continuation of Friday's own pullback rather than a reversal, even as oil and other risk-sensitive commodities moved higher on the same Iran-conflict backdrop.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin opened Monday at $80,351.40 (+0.7% from Sunday) before slipping to around $79,349.91 by mid-morning US time, trading within a broad $78,000–$80,000 range through the session** — a continuation of the mild weekend pullback flagged in Friday's outlook, rather than a sharp reversal. **Ethereum** followed a similar path, opening at $2,514.80 and slipping to around $2,497.60. Crypto commentary Monday centered on "rate watch" — this week's CPI/PPI data as the swing factor between a Fed hold and hike at the September meeting — with bitcoin still up +3.4% over the past week and +25% over the past month despite the day's softness.

## vs. Monday's pre-market outlook

Friday's pre-market outlook made two central calls, both of which held up well: **(1) US markets closed for Labor Day** — confirmed exactly. **(2) Taiwan as "the clearest bullish setup on the board"** on the strength of Friday's overnight-futures premium — confirmed emphatically, with TAIEX +1.66% and a record institutional net-buy day, though the session fell short of one analyst's illustrative 48,000-point target. The outlook's **Iran/oil "two-sided wildcard"** also resolved toward its bullish-for-oil side: Brent pushed to a six-week-plus high near $98, and Indonesia's IHSG was again the market most directly pressured by that dynamic, just as it had been Friday. The mild **weekend bitcoin pullback** flagged in the outlook also continued into Monday itself rather than reversing. Overall, Monday's actual session tracked the outlook's central themes closely — a reminder that the market-based futures signal, rather than any single analyst's price target, was the more reliable pre-market read.
