# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 7, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

European markets traded in a narrow, mixed range to start the week, with US markets closed for Labor Day removing the usual American cross-check and Asia-Pacific mostly rallying sharply in the background. **FTSE 100 closed at 10,822.13 (−0.06% to −0.08% depending on source)**, **DAX 40 at 26,006.53 (−0.15% to −0.16%)**, **CAC 40 at 8,306.15 (+0.33% per Armenpress; +0.55% computed against Friday's confirmed 8,261.11 close — a modest source divergence)**, and the pan-European **STOXX 600 at 649.90 (essentially flat, ~0.00%)** — a session best described as digestive rather than directional.

## What drove the session

- **The weekend's Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation was the live undercurrent across the region**: US Central Command's Saturday strikes on Iranian oil tankers and Iran's retaliatory strikes pushed Brent crude toward its highest level since late July — a genuine two-sided input for European markets, lifting energy-linked names while adding to inflation-risk concerns for the broader market.
- **With Wall Street closed for Labor Day, there was no US equity cross-check for the first time all week** — European markets traded on their own momentum and the region's own news flow, with Asia-Pacific's mostly strong session (Korea +4.61%, Japan +2.12%, Taiwan +1.66%) in the background but not obviously driving European sector rotation.
- **Paris outperformed London and Frankfurt on the day**, continuing a pattern of relative CAC 40 strength seen intermittently through the week, even as the index remains the region's clear laggard on a 20-day basis (see table below) due to its own ongoing domestic political overhang (France's confidence-vote story, see `../260904/uk_europe.md` and earlier files).

## Bottom line

A genuinely quiet, digestive session with no clear directional catalyst — Europe traded within roughly half a percent of flat in every direction, digesting the weekend's Iran/oil escalation without a US market open to react against. Paris's modest outperformance continues an intermittent pattern this week, though the CAC 40 remains by far the region's weakest performer on a monthly view. With the ECB's meeting and German inflation data due Thursday, and US CPI the week's real catalyst on Friday, Monday's quiet session looks more like a pause than a trend change in either direction.
