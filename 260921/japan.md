# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 21, 2026 (Monday) — MARKET CLOSED (HOLIDAY)

**The Tokyo Stock Exchange was closed all day Monday for Respect for the Aged Day (敬老の日), and stays closed Tuesday Sept 22 (a bridge holiday) and Wednesday Sept 23 (Autumnal Equinox Day, 秋分の日). Cash equities resume Thursday, Sept 24.** There is therefore **no Nikkei 225 cash close for Sept 21** — the "1-day" column for Japan in this folder's `README.md` is marked n/a and the last confirmed close (Friday, Sept 18) is shown instead. (Japan Exchange Group's *derivatives* markets kept normal holiday trading throughout.)

| Index | Sept 18 close | Change |
|---|---|---|

The Nikkei/TOPIX divergence on Friday (a +1.38% price-weighted gain vs. a slightly lower cap-weighted index) points to a **narrow, heavyweight-led advance** — Japanese coverage credited the **BOJ rate-hike decision not proving a headwind** and **Kioxia (キオクシア) gains** for lifting the Nikkei back above 65,000 (headline-level detail only; full sector data was not retrievable in this session).

## The key event: Bank of Japan hiked to 1.25%

- On **Friday Sept 18 the BOJ raised its policy rate from 1.0% to 1.25% by a 7–2 vote** — the highest level in about 31 years per Japanese coverage. The two dissenters were **Asada (浅田) and Sato (佐藤)**, who argued that inflation is still below 2% and growth is weak/that the timing wasn't appropriate. The higher rate takes effect **Sept 24**. The BOJ described the economy as "gradually recovering" with core inflation in the "upper 1% range."
- **The split vote pushed the yen weaker.** USD/JPY spiked toward **158.50**, then the BOJ was reported to have conducted what traders read as a **"rate check"** (a signal of possible intervention rather than actual intervention), pulling the pair back toward **156.70**. Monday's holiday-session levels were around **157.06–157.28** per Japanese FX coverage, with commentators flagging **intervention risk in thin holiday liquidity**.

## Holiday-session derivatives (Osaka Exchange)

**Nikkei 225 futures (December contract) closed the holiday session at 65,580, up 480 yen from Friday's settlement**, on volume of only about **9,863 contracts — roughly 40% of a normal weekday (25,752)**. The overnight session that preceded it (Friday night) had closed at 64,900 (−200). A Japanese analyst (TBL Advisory) cautioned that "today's 480-yen gain was a price created by thin trading," and that its validity will only be confirmed when physical markets reopen on Sept 24. **Treat 65,580 as an indicative level for Thursday's open, not a forecast.**

## Bottom line

Japan has no new cash-market close today: the Nikkei last closed at **65,018.95 (+1.38%) on Friday**, after the BOJ's 25bp hike to 1.25% (7–2 vote) and a yen scare. Versus reference points (all measured to Friday), it is **+2.40% vs. the Sept 14 close (63,492.99) and −0.78% vs. Aug 24 (65,528.09)** — noting this project's standing caution that Investing.com's Nikkei history can be corrupted, so these baselines come from this project's own prior folders.
