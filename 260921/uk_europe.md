# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 21, 2026 (Monday)

**Europe bounced sharply after Friday's broad selloff, led by technology and banks, and helped by a fourth straight decline in oil prices.** Confirmed closes: the **FTSE 100 at 10,739.01 (+0.75%)**, the **DAX at 25,575.01 (+1.07%)**, the **CAC 40 at 8,138.94 (+0.92%)** and the **STOXX 600 at 641.92 (+1.02% vs this project's own Friday close, Reuters called it "+1.0%" — its biggest one-day jump since July 2)** — with the DAX again clearly outpacing the UK, and the STOXX 600 notably beating Monday's national indices, indicating breadth beyond the three headline markets. All four figures now cross-checked against this project's own confirmed Friday Sept 18 closes and converge cleanly with the Armenpress/Reuters wires and (for the DAX) a second independent German-language source.

## What drove the market

- **Oil fell for a fourth straight session.** Brent dropped to around $100.3–101.3 (roughly −2.5% to −3.4% depending on vendor/timing) on **US–Iran diplomacy hopes** (President Trump signalled openness to meeting Iran's leader at the UN General Assembly) and US Central Command's report that **crude and LNG flows through the Strait of Hormuz have reached a six-month high with main transit lanes clear of mines**. Airlines rose (about +0.9% for the sector) while energy shares fell (about −0.6%) in the Reuters early read, a pattern that held into the close.
- **Technology and banks led.** The **DAX was led by Infineon (confirmed +4.35% at the close)** and chip-equipment names (Aixtron ~+5.08% pre-open, Siltronic, Suss Microtec), with **Siemens (+3.6%) and MTU Aero Engines (+3.0%)** also among the day's strongest gainers; **Commerzbank rose roughly 3%**, while **Volkswagen preferred (−2.0%), Fresenius (−1.8%) and Vonovia (−1.24%)** were the DAX's laggards. In Paris, **Soitec rebounded a confirmed 9.8%, STMicroelectronics rose 2.9%,** and **Société Générale rose 1.76% to €73.87** on its newly presented 2029 strategic plan (targeting 13–14% ROE and €1.9 billion of cost savings via simplification and AI). Euro Stoxx 50 was reported up about 1.3% intraday.
- **US–China diplomacy** ahead of Thursday's Trump–Xi summit and Treasury Secretary Bessent's "very successful" description of preliminary talks lifted sentiment across the region.
- **German regional elections dented politics but not the market.** Trading Economics reported that state elections were a major blow to Chancellor Merz's CDU (it fell to 4.9% in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, dropping out of the state parliament; Die Linke was strongest in Berlin), with populist parties gaining. **Vonovia closed down about 1.24%** on concerns tied to the Berlin result (expropriation risk), and **Volkswagen preferred (−2.0%), Porsche SE (−3.4% intraday on an analyst downgrade), Daimler Truck, Henkel and Adidas** were among the day's laggards.
- **Stock-specific stories confirmed at the close:** **Ipsen fell a confirmed 5.13% to €147.90** (more moderate than the intraday −7% read) after the FDA approved a generic competitor to its cancer drug Somatuline (about 31% of first-half revenue) with immediate commercialization rights; **Berentzen jumped ~21%** on the Sazerac acquisition announcement; **Nordnet rose ~4.6%** on a buyback.

## Money-flow analysis

Monday reversed part of Friday's sharp selloff (STOXX 600 −1.11%, DAX −1.60%, FTSE −1.45%, CAC −1.49%), which Trading Economics attributed to "a worsening macroeconomic backdrop" with **rising energy prices and sovereign yields and expectations of further ECB rate hikes** pressuring credit-sensitive sectors. With oil falling for four sessions and yields easing from their US-10-year 5% peak, investors **rotated back into cyclicals and AI-infrastructure-linked technology** (chips, chip-equipment, electrical equipment) and banks (Société Générale, Commerzbank), and away from defensive/energy shelters. Confirmed closing gains put the **STOXX 600's +1.02%/+1.0% move as its biggest one-day jump since July 2** — a genuinely strong, broad rebound, not merely a technical bounce concentrated in a handful of names, though it still leaves the index below its earlier September highs. The UK's relative underperformance (+0.75% versus DAX's +1.07%) reflects Friday's heavier damage (banks −2.1%, Lloyds −2.9%, HSBC −1.6%, telecoms −4.8%) and the BoE's stance (rates held at 3.75% on Sept 17 by a 6–3 vote; next decision Nov 5) with UK CPI at 3.1%.

## Bottom line

Europe's Monday rebound is now confirmed at the close: **DAX +1.07% to 25,575.01, STOXX 600 +1.02% to 641.92 (its biggest one-day jump since July 2), CAC 40 +0.92% to 8,138.94, FTSE 100 +0.75% to 10,739.01** — undoing part of Friday's ~1.1–1.6% declines as oil slid for a fourth session and tech/banks led. The DAX and CAC's confirmed moves match Armenpress's reported percentages almost exactly against this project's own Friday baseline, leaving no material vendor-divergence issue for those two; the STOXX 600's long-running small prior-close discrepancy versus Investing.com persists but does not change the headline story. The political shock in Germany's state elections and the still-fragile US-Iran diplomacy remain the two things most likely to test the rebound this week, alongside Tuesday's Micron earnings (a read-through risk for European chip-equipment names) and Thursday's Trump–Xi summit.
