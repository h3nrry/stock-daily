# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 21, 2026 (Monday)

**Summary:** A split session. **Thailand (+0.88%) led** on a Fitch outlook upgrade, **Singapore (+0.34%)** and **Malaysia (+0.08%)** eked out gains, while **Indonesia fell 0.88% to a 4.5-week low** on a single-stock drag and rupiah weakness ahead of a Bank Indonesia rate decision.

**Drivers:** **Fitch Ratings upgraded Thailand's credit-rating outlook from Negative to Stable**, which encouraged foreign inflows, and **banks** benefited from improved credit sentiment; **electronics** stocks gained on regional tech momentum (Kaohoon International). The analyst expects the market to "move sideways within range" next session. Reference points: **+0.44% vs. Sept 14 (1,591.07)** and **−0.19% vs. Aug 24 (1,601.05)** — the SET is back to its late-August level.

## Money-flow read-through

Regional flows followed a **"tech-rally + oil-relief"** template (Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia all up as Brent fell for a fourth straight session), but Indonesia's dependence on commodities/coal and a weakening rupiah — plus a looming central-bank decision — made it the exception. Sovereign-rating news (Fitch on Thailand) was the single most powerful country-specific driver of the day.

## Bottom line

The session confirms a familiar pattern in this project's coverage: **rating and policy catalysts matter more than the global tape for individual Southeast Asian bourses**. Thailand's Fitch upgrade gave the SET the day's best gain (+0.88%); Indonesia's 4.5-week low (−0.88%, second straight loss) shows that commodity-heavy names and a weakening rupiah can still override a supportive global backdrop.
