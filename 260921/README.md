# Daily Market Briefing — September 21, 2026 (Monday)

**Monday was a risk-on, AI-led session across most of the world, helped by oil falling for a fourth straight session on US–Iran diplomacy hopes and by anticipation of Thursday's Trump–Xi summit in Washington — but with two big calendar footnotes: Japan's market was closed for a three-day holiday (Sept 21–23), and Taiwan and Korea both have long holiday closures later this week.** **Taiwan's TAIEX rose 1.14% to 47,718.84** (fourth straight gain, a three-month high) as **MediaTek closed above NT$5,000 for the first time and Delta Electronics jumped 8%**, with three institutional-investor groups net-buying NT$47.038 billion. **Korea's KOSPI gained 1.65% to 7,007.72**, reclaiming 7,000 for the first time in seven sessions on a Samsung Electronics-led rally (Samsung +4.98%). **Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1.18% to 25,042.71**, back above 25,000 on biotech, property and PCB-supply-chain names, with southbound money buying for an 11th straight day. **Japan was closed** (last close Friday: Nikkei 65,018.95, +1.38%, after the Bank of Japan's 25bp hike to 1.25%). **Southeast Asia was split**: Thailand +0.88% (Fitch outlook upgrade), Singapore ≈+0.34%, Malaysia +0.08%, Indonesia −0.88% to a 4.5-week low. **Europe rebounded from Friday's selloff, now confirmed at the close** — FTSE 100 +0.75% to 10,739.01, DAX +1.07% to 25,575.01, CAC 40 +0.92% to 8,138.94, and the STOXX 600 +1.02% to 641.92 (its biggest one-day jump since July 2). **The US closed sharply higher** — Dow +0.71% to 52,048.83, S&P 500 +1.49% to 7,764.70 and the Nasdaq Composite +2.26% to a **record close of 27,122.09** — led by a confirmed Intel–AUO Micro LED/advanced-packaging partnership (Intel +12%), AMD (+10%, topping $600 for the first time) and a new catalyst, Meta's Muse AI agent (+11%), while the 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.95–4.96%. **Gold slipped about 0.6–0.7% (≈$4,352–$4,397), and bitcoin closed the day up roughly 7% at about $87,000** in a short squeeze that liquidated over $710 million of bearish bets.

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** into the AI supply chain — MediaTek (+6.37% to a record NT$5,010), Delta Electronics (+8.07%), TSMC (+0.81%), plus AUO's limit-up on a since-confirmed Intel Micro LED/advanced-packaging partnership (TSMC also reportedly courting AUO — see `usa.md`); institutions net-bought **NT$47.038 billion** (foreign +NT$20.376 bn, trusts +NT$3.932 bn, dealers +NT$22.730 bn, about half of which was hedging accounts). Memory chips, construction and the OTC market lagged. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** no cash trading. The Osaka holiday-session Nikkei futures closed at 65,580 (+480) on thin volume — about 40% of normal — and the yen stayed under intervention watch after the BOJ's split (7–2) hike to 1.25%. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** a Samsung Electronics-led rally (Samsung +4.98%, preferred +6.07%) supported by **institutions (+₩1.4924 trillion)** while **individuals sold ₩2.979 trillion** and foreigners were small net sellers (−₩171.9 billion); breadth was poor (310 gainers vs. 552 decliners on the KOSPI). See `korea.md`.

- **UK/Europe:** back into tech, chip-equipment and banks after Friday's rout, now confirmed at the close — Infineon +4.35%, Soitec +9.8%, Société Générale +1.76% on a new 2029 strategic plan; energy and the German autos lagged; German regional elections were a political jolt (Vonovia −1.24%). - **United States:** into AI-chip and AI-platform names — a confirmed Intel–AUO Micro LED partnership (Intel +12%), AMD's AI-accelerator price hikes (+10%, topping $600), and a new catalyst in Meta's Muse AI agent (+11%, with read-through to ARM/Intel/AMD) — plus crypto-linked stocks (Strategy +9%, Coinbase +5.6%) on a bitcoin short squeeze to ≈$87,000; small caps (Russell 2000) lagged the mega-cap rally even on a same-day basis. See `usa.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** oil down four sessions in a row (diplomacy and a Hormuz supply recovery), the US 10-year easing to ≈4.95–4.96%, gold slightly lower, and a confirmed bitcoin short squeeze (≈+7% to $87,000, over $710 million of shorts liquidated) — a classic risk-on mix that would be undone quickly by a diplomacy failure or a hawkish Fed speaker (Chicago Fed's Goolsbee already struck a hawkish note Monday — see `usa.md`).

## Oil

**Brent fell for a fourth straight session — to roughly $100.3–101.3 depending on vendor/timing (−2.5% to −3.4%) — and WTI fell to about $95.78–97.79 (−2.5% to −4.5%, a same-day vendor divergence flagged in `usa.md`)** (Friday's WTI close: $100.70, −1.19%). Drivers: **hopes for US–Iran diplomacy** (Trump signalled openness to meeting Iran's leader at the UN General Assembly, though the US denied visas to some of Iran's delegation), **Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline capacity being restored** (exports back near 4 million barrels/day), and US Central Command saying **crude and LNG flows through the Strait of Hormuz reached a six-month high** with the main lanes clear of mines. Oil remains very high compared with July (see previous folders); it is a fluid, two-sided risk rather than a resolved one.

## Gold

**Spot gold was about $4,352 (−0.72%) in Trading Economics' reading and $4,396.60 (−0.64%) at TheStreet's 11:04am ET snapshot** (different timestamps), after **Friday's close of $4,421.30 (+0.49%)**. Trading Economics attributed the dip to a stronger US dollar and profit-taking after last week's rally, and noted that markets price an **88% probability of another US rate hike by December**. **Silver was about $66.80 (−0.52%)**, versus Friday's $67.64 (+2.34%).

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin closed the day at roughly $87,000, up about 7%**, in what CoinDesk described as a **short squeeze that liquidated over $710 million across nearly 135,000 traders over 24 hours** (about 96% of one hour's liquidations were shorts, per CoinGlass data); **Strategy (MSTR) rose about 9%** after resuming Bitcoin purchases following a three-week pause, ending the day **positive year-to-date (+5%)**, and **Coinbase (COIN) rose 5.6%**.
