# Daily Market Briefing — September 1, 2026 (Tuesday)

Global snapshot: Asia opened September in a genuinely split fashion, with one standout story dominating the region. **NVIDIA made its first-ever direct investment in a Taiwan-listed company — US$3.5 billion into MediaTek via convertible bonds, tied to a 10-year AI-datacenter product roadmap** — and **MediaTek (2454) gapped straight to a locked limit-up (+9.94%, NT$4,315)** that never cleared its 26,000+ lot buy queue all day. That single stock, plus broad semiconductor-supply-chain follow-through, drove **Taiwan's TAIEX to +1.78%, closing at 46,948.72** — a clean reversal of Monday's dip and the region's best-performing major index by far. Most of the rest of the region traded far more cautiously, still digesting the weekend's US-Iran military escalation and the resulting oil-price spike: **Hong Kong fell −0.93%** (tech and property both sold off hard), **Singapore's STI dropped −0.8%**, **Malaysia's FBM KLCI broke technical support**, and **Thailand's SET eased −0.41%**. The other clear outlier was **Indonesia, where the IHSG rallied +1.14% against the regional trend**. **Japan's Nikkei was roughly flat (~−0.14%)** in a directionless, consolidative session, and **Korea's KOSPI edged up +0.23%** — though entirely on Samsung/SK Hynix buyback-related "other corporation" buying, with individuals, foreigners, and institutions all net sellers. **The same Iran/oil/yield thread carried straight through to the US and Europe once those sessions closed**: US indices fell for a second straight day (Dow −0.8%, S&P −0.7%, Nasdaq −1.0%) as the 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.79% (highest since January 2025) and oil pushed WTI above $90/barrel for the first time in weeks, while every major European index closed lower too (DAX −1.10%, STOXX 600 −0.56%, CAC 40 −0.39%, FTSE 100 −0.32%). **Reading the table:** the day's clearest story is the divergence within Asia itself — Taiwan and Indonesia rallied while Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand all traded cautiously to negative on the same oil/Iran/hawkish-Fed backdrop. That's a genuinely different pattern from Monday, when the divergence ran more along an "MSCI-rebalancing-cushioned" vs. "not cushioned" line — today looks more like company/sector-specific catalysts (TSMC/SEMICON Taiwan for Taiwan; unclear domestic drivers for Indonesia) outweighing the shared macro headwind in a couple of markets, while it dominated in the others. Once the US and European sessions closed, they confirmed the same oil/yield-driven pressure seen in Asia's more cautious markets — Germany's DAX was the day's single worst major-index performer anywhere, hit by both the shared oil shock and a domestic inflation print tied directly to energy costs.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** a second straight down day — Dow −0.8%, S&P −0.7%, Nasdaq −1.0% — as oil (WTI above $90 for the first time in weeks) and Treasury yields (10-year at 4.79%, highest since Jan 2025) kept climbing. Tech led the losses (Palo Alto Networks −5.24%, CrowdStrike −6.90%) while defensive positioning outperformed and the VIX rose to 15.87. Credit spreads (IG 0.80%, HY 2.63%, most recent confirmed readings) have not yet widened meaningfully — worth watching as a signal of whether this stays equity-specific. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** the day's headline global story — NVIDIA's first-ever direct investment in a Taiwan-listed company (US$3.5B into MediaTek via convertible bonds, tied to a 10-year AI-datacenter roadmap using NVLink Fusion/NVHBM) sent MediaTek to a locked limit-up (+9.94%, 26,000+ lot unfilled buy queue) and drove TAIEX to +1.78%. TSMC and the broader semiconductor supply chain contributed too, but were clearly secondary to MediaTek. Three institutions combined net-bought a modest NT$5.61B — small relative to the size of the index move. The 0050 ETF closed +2.07%. See `taiwan.md` for the full deal breakdown.
- **Korea:** a headline +0.23% KOSPI gain that masks a genuinely cautious underlying market — individuals (−₩472.4B), foreigners (−₩541.1B), and institutions (−₩675.3B) were all net sellers, with the index held up entirely by ₩1,688.8B in "other corporation" buying tied to Samsung/SK Hynix buybacks. KOSDAQ fell −1.56%, arguably the more honest read on risk appetite. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a rough "door-opening" to September — Hang Seng −0.93%, Hang Seng Tech −1.49% — with big-cap internet names (Alibaba, JD.com >−3%; Tencent, Meituan, Bilibili, Baidu >−2%) and property developers (Yuexiu >−7%, Evergrande >−5%) both selling off hard. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** a genuine split — Indonesia's IHSG rallied +1.14% "against the Asian trend," while Singapore (−0.8%), Malaysia (technical breakdown below its 9-/20-day moving averages), and Thailand (−0.41%) all fell in line with the region's broader oil-driven caution. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** every major index closed lower for a second straight session — DAX −1.10% (the region's worst performer, hit by an inflation print tying energy costs directly to the oil shock), STOXX 600 −0.56%, CAC 40 −0.39%, FTSE 100 −0.32%. Energy stocks (TotalEnergies, Orlen, OMV) were the lone sector winners, up 1.5–3.5% on higher oil prices. Rate-hike speculation ahead of next week's ECB meeting is a new thread worth tracking alongside the Fed's own hawkish repricing. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** **gold opened higher (December futures $4,498.70, +0.4%) but faded to ~$4,432 by mid-morning US trading** as rate-hike expectations firmed. **Bitcoin and ethereum showed the same pattern** — BTC opened ~$78,559 (+1.1%) before easing to ~$77,946; ETH opened ~$2,467 (+2%) before easing to ~$2,454. The common thread across gold, crypto, and now confirmed equities: firmer opens/starts giving way as the market prices in higher September rate-hike odds on top of the Iran-driven oil shock — a pattern that held through to the actual US and European closes.
