# Daily Market Briefing — September 1, 2026 (Tuesday)

**⚠️ Built mid-session: Asia/Pacific markets are fully closed and reported below; Europe and the US are still trading as of writing (Tuesday ~10pm Taipei time) and are flagged as pending — see `usa.md` and `uk_europe.md` for pre-market/early-session notes, to be updated once those sessions close.**

Global snapshot: Asia opened September in a genuinely split fashion. **Taiwan's TAIEX surged +1.39% to 46,770.22**, powered by TSMC and semiconductor optimism ahead of Wednesday's SEMICON Taiwan 2026 show — a clean reversal of Monday's dip. But most of the rest of the region traded cautiously to negative, still digesting the weekend's US-Iran military escalation and the resulting oil-price spike: **Hong Kong fell −0.93%** (tech and property both sold off hard), **Singapore's STI dropped −0.8%**, **Malaysia's FBM KLCI broke technical support**, and **Thailand's SET eased −0.41%**. The clearest outlier alongside Taiwan was **Indonesia, where the IHSG rallied +1.14% against the regional trend**. **Japan's Nikkei was roughly flat (~−0.14%)** in a directionless, consolidative session, and **Korea's KOSPI edged up +0.23%** — though entirely on Samsung/SK Hynix buyback-related "other corporation" buying, with individuals, foreigners, and institutions all net sellers. See each market's file in this folder for the full write-up; `usa.md` and `uk_europe.md` carry pre-close notes only.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** market just opened (~30 minutes before writing); pre-market indications point to continued pressure from oil/yields, with gold and bitcoin both fading from firmer opens as September rate-hike odds firm further (CME FedWatch: 66.4% probability of a 25bp hike, up from 39.6% a week ago). See `usa.md` — to be updated once the session closes.
- **Taiwan:** a strong +1.39% day led by TSMC (+NT$20) and broad semiconductor optimism ahead of SEMICON Taiwan 2026; the 0050 ETF closed +2.07%. Same-day three-way institutional flow data was not yet published at writing time. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** a headline +0.23% KOSPI gain that masks a genuinely cautious underlying market — individuals (−₩472.4B), foreigners (−₩541.1B), and institutions (−₩675.3B) were all net sellers, with the index held up entirely by ₩1,688.8B in "other corporation" buying tied to Samsung/SK Hynix buybacks. KOSDAQ fell −1.56%, arguably the more honest read on risk appetite. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a rough "door-opening" to September — Hang Seng −0.93%, Hang Seng Tech −1.49% — with big-cap internet names (Alibaba, JD.com >−3%; Tencent, Meituan, Bilibili, Baidu >−2%) and property developers (Yuexiu >−7%, Evergrande >−5%) both selling off hard. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** a genuine split — Indonesia's IHSG rallied +1.14% "against the Asian trend," while Singapore (−0.8%), Malaysia (technical breakdown below its 9-/20-day moving averages), and Thailand (−0.41%) all fell in line with the region's broader oil-driven caution. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** session still open at writing time; last confirmed data is Monday's close (DAX −1.17%, CAC 40 −0.79%, FTSE roughly flat). See `uk_europe.md` — to be updated once the session closes.
- **Global cross-asset:** **gold opened higher (December futures $4,498.70, +0.4%) but faded to ~$4,432 by mid-morning US trading** as rate-hike expectations firmed. **Bitcoin and ethereum showed the same pattern** — BTC opened ~$78,559 (+1.1%) before easing to ~$77,946; ETH opened ~$2,467 (+2%) before easing to ~$2,454. The common thread across gold, crypto, and now potentially equities: firmer opens giving way as the market prices in higher September rate-hike odds on top of the Iran-driven oil shock.
