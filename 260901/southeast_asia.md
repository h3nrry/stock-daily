# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 1, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Singapore — Straits Times Index (STI)

**STI closed at 5,710.37, down 0.8% (44.99 points)**, as **rising crude oil prices and continued geopolitical tensions** (the Iran situation, see `../usa.md`) made investors cautious — higher energy costs raise company expenses and consumer costs while adding to inflation and interest-rate uncertainty. Not all stocks fell: **Hong Leong Asia rose nearly 2%** on a subsidiary's renewed Hong Kong listing application, and **Keppel DC REIT and Vibrant Group** both gained on newly announced data-center and trading acquisitions respectively.

## Indonesia — IHSG / JCI

**IHSG closed at 6,599, up 1.14% (~407 advancing stocks)** — a genuine standout, rising **against the broader Asian trend** on the same day most regional markets were pressured by the oil-price spike. Local coverage explicitly framed this as **"IHSG dibuka menguat melawan tren Asia"** (IHSG opened stronger against the Asian trend). This divergence is worth flagging clearly: Indonesia's domestic-liquidity and sector-rotation dynamics evidently outweighed the regional oil/Iran headwind today.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

**FBM KLCI declined, falling over 20 points to 1,705.52 in the first 30 minutes of trading alone** (full-day close level not independently confirmed at writing time), pressured by **Brent crude futures surging past $91/barrel** on fresh US-Iran strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, hawkish Fed commentary from Jackson Hole, weakness tracking Wall Street overnight, and a technical breakdown — Apex Securities noted the index **"slipped below its nine- and 20-day moving averages for the first time during the recent recovery, signalling a loss of near-term momentum."**

## Thailand — SET Index

**SET closed at 1,588.68, down 6.48 points (−0.41%)**, on trading value of **THB 72.56 billion**. The market **moved lower later in the session due to external negative factors** — elevated oil prices from Middle East tensions and elevated bond yields following the Fed's hawkish policy signal.

## Bottom line

A genuinely split session across the region: **Indonesia bucked the regional trend entirely (+1.14%)**, while **Singapore (−0.8%), Malaysia (technical breakdown), and Thailand (−0.41%)** all fell in line with the oil-price-driven, Fed-hawkishness-driven caution seen across Asia and globally today. Indonesia's divergence is the clearest story here and worth tracking for whether it's a durable domestic-liquidity story or a one-day rotation.
