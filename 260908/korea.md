# Korea Stock Market Analysis — September 8, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

Korea's KOSPI gave back a slice of Monday's explosive +4.61% rally in a genuine intraday round-trip. The index **closed at 6,954.52, down 40.87 points (−0.58%)**, after reaching an intraday high of **7,171.52** — briefly threatening a fresh push above 7,000 — before geopolitical risk and profit-taking dragged it back below Monday's close by the final bell. The close reconciles exactly against Monday's confirmed 6,995.39 (see `../260907/korea.md`).

## What drove the reversal

- **A two-act session**: the morning extended Monday's AI/memory-semiconductor enthusiasm, pushing KOSPI as high as 7,171.52, before **the overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation and the resulting jump in oil prices triggered afternoon profit-taking** that erased the gains and pushed the index into the red.
- **Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both initially surged on strong foreign buying before retreating with the broader market**: **Samsung Electronics closed down 0.19% to ₩269,500**, while **SK Hynix still managed to close up 0.56% to ₩1,793,000**, holding onto a modest gain even as the headline index reversed — a sign the underlying AI/memory demand story from Monday remained intact even as the broader market pulled back.
- **Sector performance was sharply negative outside chips' relative resilience**: **IT stocks fell 3.21%** and **medical/precision equipment dropped 2.10%**, while **energy-related shares gained modestly** on the same rising-oil dynamic pressuring equities elsewhere.
- **The won weakened into the close**: the won/dollar rate settled at **1,345.6**, initially appreciating on early risk sentiment before oil-price concerns reasserted themselves and outweighed any safe-haven yen-adjacent strength.

## Money flow — 외국인/기관/개인 (foreign/institutional/individual)

**Individual investors sold approximately ₩3.33 trillion**, continuing the pattern from Monday's rally where individuals sold into strength; **foreign and institutional investors together provided support with combined net buying of roughly ₩1.3 trillion** — a far smaller figure than Monday's ₩4.9 trillion combined buy, consistent with a session that reversed rather than extended the prior day's institutional conviction.

## Stocks/sectors to watch

| Stock/Sector | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| SK Hynix | Closed +0.56% even as the broader index fell — one of the few names to hold a gain, continuing Monday's AI/memory-demand story | A further reversal here, rather than just cooling off, would be a much stronger signal that Monday's rally has fully run its course |
| Samsung Electronics | Initially surged on foreign buying before closing modestly lower (−0.19%) — tracking the broader index's round-trip almost exactly | As Korea's largest-weight stock, its direction is the single best proxy for whether KOSPI's next move is a resumption of the rally or a deeper pullback |
| IT sector broadly | Fell 3.21%, the day's worst-performing major sector despite chip-specific strength in SK Hynix — a genuine divergence worth tracking | If IT weakness broadens rather than staying concentrated outside chips, it would be a more troubling signal for the AI-rally thesis |
| USD/KRW (1,345.6) | The won's late-session weakening tracked the same oil-driven risk-off dynamic pressuring equities — a level to watch into Wednesday | Continued won weakness alongside elevated oil prices would compound imported-inflation pressure on the Bank of Korea's own policy calculus |

## Bottom line

A genuine intraday round-trip that gave back a modest slice of Monday's historic rally — KOSPI −0.58% to 6,954.52 after touching 7,171.52 intraday — driven by the same overnight Iran/oil escalation weighing on risk appetite across the region. The composition beneath the headline number is more reassuring than alarming: foreign and institutional investors still bought on net, SK Hynix held a gain, and the reversal looks like ordinary profit-taking into geopolitical uncertainty rather than a fundamental repricing of the AI/memory-semiconductor story that drove Monday's surge. Whether KOSPI can reclaim 7,000 once the Iran-driven volatility settles is the clearest thing to watch next.
