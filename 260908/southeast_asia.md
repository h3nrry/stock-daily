# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 8, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia stayed split for a third straight session, with Indonesia again the region's clear outlier. Singapore fell in line with the broader region's Iran/oil-driven risk-off tone, Malaysia and Thailand both finished essentially flat, and Indonesia bucked the trend entirely with a strong rally on raw-materials strength.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,767.45, down roughly 0.43%** from Monday's 5,792.28, opening weak (**SGX fell 0.55% at the open**) and staying under pressure through the session as the region-wide Iran/oil-driven risk-off tone (see `../260908/usa.md`, `japan.md`) weighed on sentiment. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+1.00% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **+0.23% over 20 trading days (~1 month)**, per Investing.com's historical-data series — both remain positive even after Tuesday's pullback, underscoring that the decline was a single-session pause within a still-constructive medium-term trend rather than a reversal.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,686.44, up 66.77 points (+1.01%)** — a genuine outlier against the rest of the region's Iran/oil-driven caution, and a reversal of Monday's own modest decline. Market breadth was strongly positive, with **363 advancing stocks versus 244 declining** (185 unchanged) on turnover of **Rp15.75 trillion** across 37.51 billion shares and 2.30 million transactions. Broader gauges confirmed the rally's breadth: **LQ45 rose 1.40%, IDX30 gained 1.20%, JII (the Sharia index) climbed 1.81%**, and **Sri-Kehati added 0.71%**. **Raw materials led sector gains (+2.00%)**, followed by non-cyclicals (+1.39%), industrials (+1.22%), and energy (+1.17%) — the energy-sector strength itself a direct read-through from the same oil-price spike that pressured most other regional markets. **Merdeka Battery Materials (MBMA), Citra Borneo Utama (CBUT), and Merdeka Copper Gold (MDKA)** led gainers, all commodity-linked names benefiting from the stronger raw-materials and energy backdrop.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,714.40, down just 0.39 points (−0.02%)** — essentially flat, holding onto the bulk of Monday's gain. Rakuten Trade's Thong Pak Leng noted trading interest rotating toward **smaller-cap technology and construction stocks**, with headwinds from Strait of Hormuz concerns, elevated US Treasury yields, and anticipated US inflation data broadly offset by selective buying. **Maybank declined 4.0 sen to RM10.54**, while **Tenaga gained 6.0 sen to RM13.90** and **Gamuda added 21 sen to RM4.80**. **United Plantations (RM33.30), Nestlé Malaysia (RM94.16), and Solarvest (RM3.55)** led gainers; the **Plantation Index surged 87.95 points to 9,476.63** while the **Financial Services Index fell 41.27 points to 20,147.47**. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+0.82% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **−1.21% over 20 trading days (~1 month)**, per Investing.com's historical-data series.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,617.83, down just 0.99 points (−0.06%)** on trading value of **THB 34.92 billion** — a genuinely range-bound session per Kasikorn Securities, with Middle East geopolitical concerns weighing on sentiment even as rising crude prices supported the index's substantial oil-related holdings, a rare instance of the same oil-price spike acting as both headwind and tailwind within a single market. Profit-taking in prior gainers and ex-dividend movements in banking stocks added further downward pressure. **TOP rose 3.13% to THB 66.00** and **KTB gained 1.16% to THB 43.75**, while **DELTA eased 0.72% to THB 274.00** and **SCB (trading ex-dividend) fell 1.32% to THB 150.00**. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+1.84% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and roughly **+0.32% over 20 trading days (~1 month, approximate — see note below)**.

## Bottom line

A genuinely split session for a third straight day: Indonesia's IHSG rallied +1.01% on broad-based raw-materials and energy strength, directly benefiting from the same oil-price spike that pressured sentiment almost everywhere else in the region, while Singapore's STI fell −0.43% in line with the region's Iran-driven risk-off tone. Malaysia and Thailand both finished essentially flat, each absorbing the same geopolitical headwind without a clear directional resolution. Indonesia's divergence — now a recurring pattern this month — continues to reflect its market's own domestic commodity-sector weighting reacting differently to oil-price moves than the broader region's growth/tech-sensitive indices.
