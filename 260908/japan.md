# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 8, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

Japan's Nikkei 225 snapped its two-session winning streak with a sharp decline, the region's biggest mover on the day. The index **closed at 65,269.33, down 1,130.51 points (−1.70%)** — its first drop in three trading sessions, after touching an intraday high of 66,791.84 shortly after the open before reversing hard. The close reconciles exactly against Monday's confirmed 66,399.84 (see `../260907/japan.md`).

## What drove the decline

- **A rapid yen appreciation was the single biggest driver**: the dollar-yen pair strengthened sharply during the session, with USD/JPY briefly touching the 152 level — a genuine risk-off, safe-haven flow into the yen tied to the overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation (see `../260908/usa.md`) — and export-oriented names sold off hard as a stronger yen directly pressures their overseas earnings.
- **The session's own internal shape confirmed a broad reversal rather than a narrow one**: the market opened firmer, briefly extending Monday's own rally, then faded steadily through the morning and afternoon sessions to close near its low (65,596.74 low-of-day close reference), a "3営業日ぶりの下落" (first decline in three sessions) per local wires.
- **Semiconductor names offered a partial offset**: some chip-related issues held up better than the broader export complex in early trading, a continuation of the AI/semiconductor strength that has driven much of the Nikkei's recent two-way volatility, though this support was not enough to prevent the broader index from giving back the bulk of its opening gain.
- **The same overnight Iran/oil escalation weighing on Taiwan, Korea, and US futures overnight** (see `../260908/taiwan.md`, `korea.md`, `usa.md`) fed directly into Tokyo's own risk-off tone, with the stronger yen amplifying rather than replacing that pressure.

## Money flow — 外国人 (foreign investors)

Same-day foreign-investor flow data via JPX's weekly investor-type statistics was not available at writing time (JPX publishes on a weekly, not daily, cadence — a recurring limitation for this file). Tuesday's price action — a sharp reversal concentrated in export-sensitive names on yen strength — is consistent with a session driven by currency-hedging and safe-haven flows into the yen rather than a broad-based foreign-investor exit from Japanese equities; this remains a read on sentiment rather than a confirmed flow figure.

## Bottom line

A sharp, broad reversal that ended a brief two-session winning streak — Nikkei −1.70% to 65,269.33, driven overwhelmingly by a rapid yen appreciation toward 152 on overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz safe-haven flows, with export-oriented names bearing the brunt of the move. Unlike Monday's narrow, mega-cap-concentrated rally, Tuesday's decline reflects a genuinely macro (currency-driven) story rather than a single-stock or sector-specific one. Whether the yen's strength persists — and what it does to the broader region's own risk appetite — is the clearest thing to watch heading into Wednesday's session.
