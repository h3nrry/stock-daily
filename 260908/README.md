# Daily Market Briefing — September 8, 2026 (Tuesday)

Global snapshot: US markets reopened after Labor Day into a genuine, broad-but-orderly risk-off session as the **Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz conflict escalated further overnight** — fresh tanker strikes traded in both directions — pushing **Brent crude to an intraday peak of $99.46** and reviving safe-haven and defensive flows across nearly every market this project covers. The **Dow fell 1.18% to 52,786.07** (its steepest one-day drop in weeks), with healthcare and financials leading losses while energy and utilities held up; the **S&P 500 fell 0.58%** and the **Nasdaq just 0.32%**, technology genuinely decoupling on fresh AI-chip partnership news (an Intel-Amazon custom-chip deal). Asia-Pacific was similarly split: **Japan's Nikkei was the region's biggest loser, falling 1.70%** as a sharp yen appreciation toward 152 (on the same Iran-driven safe-haven flows) hit export names hard; **Taiwan's TAIEX eased 0.47%**, giving back a slice of Monday's record institutional-buying rally but with foreign investors still buying for a third straight day; **Korea's KOSPI round-tripped from an intraday high of 7,171.52 to close down 0.58%**; and **Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell a milder 0.38%**, with commodity-linked names (Luoyang Molybdenum, Sinopec) rallying even as its own Tech sub-index dropped 1.61%. Southeast Asia stayed split for a third straight session, with **Indonesia's IHSG the region's standout, rallying 1.01%** on broad raw-materials and energy strength — a direct beneficiary of the same oil spike that pressured almost everyone else — while Singapore fell 0.43% and Malaysia/Thailand each finished essentially flat. Europe was quiet and modestly lower, tracking the same global tone: **FTSE −0.10%, DAX roughly flat, CAC −0.52%, STOXX 600 −0.05%**, with the region's own upcoming ECB meeting and a hot August inflation print (3.3% y/y) adding a genuine, oil-price-driven inflation concern of its own. Gold was mixed by instrument (COMEX futures −0.74%, spot −0.03%) in a continued consolidation, and bitcoin slipped roughly 1.6% on the same geopolitical risk-off tone layered onto this week's Fed "rate watch."

**Reading the table:** Tuesday's clearest story is a single, shared shock — the overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation — landing very differently across markets depending on each one's own sector composition. Japan, with its heavy export weighting, took the hardest hit as the yen strengthened on safe-haven flows. Indonesia, with its heavier commodity/resource weighting, was the one market to actually benefit from the same oil-price spike that hurt almost everyone else. Taiwan and Korea both show a reassuring pattern beneath their negative headline numbers — foreign/institutional buying continued in Taiwan, and Korea's round-trip left SK Hynix still higher on the day — suggesting Tuesday reads more as a geopolitical pause than a fundamental repricing of this month's dominant AI/semiconductor rally. Korea's KOSPI remains the most extreme number in the table on a 20-day view (+10.40%), a reminder of just how sharp this month's regional whipsaw has been even after Tuesday's pullback.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** a genuine but selective risk-off reopening — Dow −1.18% (its worst single-day drop in weeks) on healthcare (−2.52%) and financials (−1.38%) weakness, while energy (+1.11%) and utilities (+0.86%) held up and technology actually rose (+0.32%). Fresh Intel-Amazon AI-chip partnership news drove some of the day's sharpest single-stock gains (Intel +7.5–9%, Qualcomm +3.2%) even as Amgen (−9.1%) and Ionis (−10.5%) led healthcare's sharp, idiosyncratic declines. Treasury yields (10Y 4.796%) and credit spreads (HY OAS 2.67%, IG OAS 0.81%) stayed calm, suggesting fixed-income markets see this as a geopolitical/commodity shock rather than a credit-quality scare. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** an orderly pullback with a constructive undertone — TAIEX −0.47% to 47,105.78, giving back a slice of Monday's record institutional-buying day, but foreign investors extended their net-buying streak to a third straight session even as investment trusts and dealers took profits. Leadership rotated cleanly into probe-card/testing (WinWay Technology +10% limit-up), memory semiconductors, and financials (Yuanta Financial +4.59% to a historic high), away from Monday's MediaTek/TSMC-led rally. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** a genuine intraday round-trip — KOSPI touched 7,171.52 before geopolitical risk and profit-taking pulled it back to close −0.58% at 6,954.52. SK Hynix still closed higher (+0.56%), and foreign/institutional investors together bought a net ₩1.3 trillion even as individuals sold ₩3.33 trillion — a much smaller reversal in institutional conviction than the headline swing suggests. See `korea.md`.
- **Japan:** the region's clearest casualty of the overnight Iran escalation — Nikkei −1.70%, driven overwhelmingly by a sharp yen appreciation toward 152 on safe-haven flows, which hit export-oriented names hard and ended a brief two-session winning streak. A genuinely macro, currency-driven story rather than a stock-specific one. See `japan.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a milder, more selective decline — Hang Seng −0.38%, with commodity/resource names (Luoyang Molybdenum, Sinopec) rallying on the same oil-price spike that hurt most other markets, even as the Hang Seng Tech Index fell a much sharper −1.61% on broad technology/internet weakness (Lenovo, BYD Electronics, SMIC). See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** split for a third straight session — Indonesia's IHSG was the region's standout, +1.01% on broad raw-materials/energy strength directly benefiting from the oil spike, while Singapore fell −0.43% in line with the region's risk-off tone and Malaysia/Thailand both finished essentially flat, each absorbing the same headwind without a clear directional resolution. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** a quiet, modestly negative session tracking the same global tone — FTSE −0.10%, DAX roughly flat, CAC −0.52% (continuing its persistent underperformance on every timeframe tracked here), STOXX 600 −0.05% — with oil's push higher a genuine fresh inflation risk for the region just ahead of the ECB's own policy meeting (markets pricing a 25bp hike) against an already-hot August inflation print (3.3% y/y). See `uk_europe.md`.

## Oil

**Brent crude rose to $97.92 (+0.92%), with an intraday peak of $99.46** — the highest intraday level of the current escalation — as Iran and the US traded fresh strikes in the Strait of Hormuz overnight, a direct continuation of the weekend's tanker-strike-and-retaliation pattern. **WTI traded above $90/barrel.** Iranian military officials additionally warned of potential retaliation specifically targeting energy infrastructure in the Persian Gulf, keeping the supply-risk premium fully live heading into Wednesday. Oil was the clearest single cross-market driver of Tuesday's session, acting as a headwind for export-sensitive and energy-import-dependent markets (Japan, Europe, the broad US market) while directly benefiting commodity/resource-heavy ones (Indonesia, parts of Hong Kong, Thailand's own energy holdings). See `usa.md`.

## Gold

Gold's move depended on instrument: **COMEX gold futures fell to $4,443.30 (−0.74%)**, while **spot gold (per goldprice.org) eased more modestly to $4,422.54 (−0.03%)** from Friday's $4,479.85 reference point — both consistent with a continued consolidation after last week's sharp rally rather than a reversal, even as oil and other risk-sensitive commodities moved higher on the same Iran-conflict backdrop that might normally be expected to lift the metal's own safe-haven bid.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin opened Tuesday at $79,093.85 and slipped to around $78,370.62 by 7:20am ET, down roughly 1.6% from Monday's open**, with **Ethereum opening at $2,489.84 and easing to about $2,473.66 (down roughly 1% from Monday's open)**. Crypto commentary framed the move as geopolitical risk-off selling compounding this week's dominant "rate watch" narrative — markets are pricing roughly a 60% probability of a Fed rate hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting, with the higher-for-longer rate backdrop continuing to pressure yield-free assets alongside Tuesday's fresh Iran-driven risk aversion.
