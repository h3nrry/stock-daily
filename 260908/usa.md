# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 8, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

US markets reopened after the Labor Day holiday to a genuine risk-off session as the Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz conflict escalated further overnight and oil pushed toward the $100/barrel threshold. The **Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 628.18 points (−1.18%) to 52,786.07**, the **S&P 500 fell 45.08 points (−0.58%) to 7,673.52**, and the **Nasdaq Composite fell 85.58 points (−0.32%) to 26,421.41** — a broad but uneven decline, with the **Russell 2000 down 0.5% to 2,960.79**. Both closes reconcile arithmetically against Friday, September 4's confirmed prior close (Dow 53,414.25, S&P 7,718.60, Nasdaq 26,506.99 — see `../260907/usa.md`), since Monday was a full market holiday.

## What drove the market

- **Iran and the US traded fresh strikes in the Strait of Hormuz overnight**, a direct continuation of the weekend escalation flagged in Monday's outlook — CNBC reported Iran struck US vessels and oil tankers in the strait in retaliation for US strikes on Iranian tankers. With no offsetting catalyst, the conflict pushed **oil toward $100/barrel** and revived the same hawkish-Fed, elevated-yield backdrop that has defined much of this month's trading.
- **The Dow's decline was disproportionately sharp relative to the S&P and Nasdaq**, consistent with **healthcare (−2.52%) and financials (−1.38%)** — both heavily represented in the Dow — leading sector losses, while **energy (+1.11%) and utilities (+0.86%)** were the session's only gainers, a classic defensive/commodity rotation on a geopolitical-risk-driven selloff. **Technology actually rose modestly (+0.32%)**, the reason the Nasdaq's decline was far milder than the Dow's.
- **Healthcare was hit hard by single-stock news**: **Amgen fell 9.1%** and **Ionis Pharmaceuticals dropped 10.5%**, among the session's steepest declines; **Howmet Aerospace fell 7.2%** and **Expedia dropped 5.8%** on their own idiosyncratic pressures.
- **AI-chip partnership news drove the day's biggest single-stock rallies**: **Intel surged 7.5–9%** and **Qualcomm rose 3.2%** on reports of an **Intel-Amazon custom AI chip deal**, while **Roivant Sciences jumped 18.7%**, **Lumentum Holdings gained 8%**, and **CoreWeave rose 6%**. On the other side, **Salesforce, Intuit, and ServiceNow each fell roughly 4–5%** on renewed concern about AI-driven disruption to their own software businesses.
- **Fed rate-hike odds firmed further**: markets were pricing roughly a **60% probability of a rate increase at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting**, per CME Group's FedWatch tool, building on August's stronger-than-expected jobs report (+162,000). Reporting also flagged an escalating **US-Canada trade dispute** as an additional, secondary headwind for cross-border-exposed names.

## Treasury yields

Yields stayed near their recent multi-year highs as the Iran-driven oil spike reinforced inflation concerns: the **2-year yield stood at 4.39%**, the **10-year yield at 4.796%** (holding just under its highest levels in roughly two decades), the **20-year at 5.253%**, and the **30-year at 5.256%** — all dated September 8. The 10-year has been effectively pinned in the high-4.7%/low-4.8% range since late last week, with this week's incoming CPI/PPI data the next real catalyst for a directional move.

## Credit spreads

The **ICE BofA US High Yield OAS stood at 2.67%** and the **ICE BofA US Investment-Grade (Corporate Index) OAS at 0.81%**, both dated September 8 — both still historically tight and essentially unchanged from Friday's readings, indicating credit markets are not yet pricing meaningful stress from the equity selloff, the elevated yield backdrop, or the Iran/oil shock.

## Oil, gold, bitcoin

**Brent crude rose to $97.92 (+0.92%), with an intraday peak of $99.46** — its highest intraday level of the current escalation — as the fresh Strait of Hormuz tanker exchanges kept supply risk fully live; **WTI traded above $90/barrel**. **Gold was mixed depending on instrument**: COMEX gold futures fell to **$4,443.30 (−0.74%)**, while spot gold (per goldprice.org) eased more modestly to **$4,422.54 (−0.03%)** from Friday's $4,479.85 reference — both consistent with a continued consolidation after last week's sharp rally rather than a reversal, even as oil and other risk-sensitive commodities pushed higher on the same Iran-conflict backdrop. **Bitcoin opened at $79,093.85 and slipped to around $78,370.62 by 7:20am ET (−1.6% from Monday's open)**, with **Ethereum opening at $2,489.84 and easing to about $2,473.66 (−1% from Monday's open)** — crypto commentary framed the move as geopolitical risk-off selling compounding this week's "rate watch" ahead of the September 15–16 FOMC meeting, where higher-for-longer rates continue to pressure yield-free assets.

## Bottom line

A genuine risk-off reopening after the long weekend — the fresh Strait of Hormuz escalation and oil's push toward $100/barrel drove the Dow to its worst single-day percentage decline in several sessions, with healthcare and financials leading losses and only energy and utilities holding up. The Nasdaq's far milder decline, alongside QQQ's near-flat close, shows technology genuinely decoupling from the broader market's risk-off tone — helped by fresh AI-chip partnership news (Intel-Amazon) that drove some of the day's sharpest individual gainers even as the health-care sector saw its own sharp, idiosyncratic single-stock losses (Amgen, Ionis). Treasury yields and credit spreads were comparatively calm, suggesting fixed-income markets are treating this as a geopolitical/commodity shock rather than a broader growth or credit-quality scare. This week's CPI/PPI prints and the ongoing Iran conflict are the two clearest catalysts to watch into the September 15–16 FOMC meeting.
