# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 8, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

European markets fell modestly, tracking the same overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation and oil-price spike weighing on risk appetite in the US and Asia. **FTSE 100 closed at 10,811.66 (−0.10%)**, **DAX 40 at 26,007.63 (essentially flat, +0.004%)**, **CAC 40 at 8,262.80 (−0.52%)**, and the pan-European **STOXX 600 at 649.60 (−0.05%)** — a genuinely quiet session in aggregate, with the region's major benchmarks finishing within roughly half a percent of Monday's close in either direction.

## What drove the session

- **Crude oil extended its gains for a third consecutive session**, with Brent crude holding above $90/barrel (and pushing toward $99 intraday elsewhere in global trading — see `../260908/usa.md`) as the fresh Strait of Hormuz tanker exchanges kept supply risk fully live. **Iranian military officials warned of potential further retaliation against US or allied strikes, specifically threatening energy infrastructure in the Persian Gulf** — a direct, live threat to global energy supply that weighed on sentiment across energy-import-dependent European economies.
- **Higher oil prices pose a genuine inflation risk for Europe specifically**: August Eurozone headline inflation came in at **3.3% year-on-year, with energy prices up 14.3%** — a backdrop that complicates the European Central Bank's policy calculus heading into its own upcoming meeting, where markets are pricing in a likely **25-basis-point rate increase**.
- **Germany's DAX was the region's relative outperformer**, finishing essentially flat even as the broader region leaned lower — consistent with a market weighing the same oil/inflation headwind without a fresh domestic catalyst either way.
- **France's CAC 40 was the region's laggard**, falling roughly half a percent — a continuation of the index's persistent underperformance on every timeframe this project has tracked this month (see the 20-day comparison below), reflecting its own ongoing domestic political overhang layered on top of Tuesday's shared oil-driven headwind.

## Bottom line

A genuinely quiet, digestive session that mirrors the muted-but-negative tone seen across most of Asia and the US on the same overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation — Europe's major indices finished within roughly half a percent of flat, with Germany's DAX the relative outperformer and France's CAC 40 continuing its now-familiar role as the region's laggard on every timeframe measured here. With oil prices directly threatening a fresh round of European inflation (August's headline print already at 3.3% year-on-year) just ahead of the ECB's own policy meeting, the region's next catalyst is squarely a monetary-policy one, layered on top of whatever the Iran conflict does next to energy prices.
