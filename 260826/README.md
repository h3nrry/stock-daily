# Daily Market Briefing — August 26, 2026 (Wednesday)

Global snapshot: markets across Asia, Europe, and the US spent the entire session **positioning ahead of Nvidia's earnings**, released after the US close — the single dominant catalyst of the day everywhere. Taiwan (+1.47%), Korea (+0.97%), Hong Kong (+0.56%), and Japan (+0.5–0.6%) all rose on genuine anticipation, and in Taiwan's and Korea's cases, on real underlying support (a decisive institutional-flow reversal in Taiwan, massive Samsung/SK Hynix buyback commitments in Korea) rather than momentum alone. Europe stayed essentially flat across all four major benchmarks, the calmest region again. US indices were little changed into the close — and then Nvidia delivered a genuine beat-and-raise (revenue $96.2B vs. $91.9B expected, Q3 guidance of $108B, a 70%-fiscal-2028-growth forecast from Jensen Huang), triggering an initial ~1.5% after-hours dip on margin concerns that reversed into a ~6–7% gain by Thursday's US open. Elsewhere, Indonesia's IHSG fell 1.48% on a distinctly local catalyst (caution ahead of an August 27 Jakarta demonstration), oil eased further (WTI/Brent both modestly lower after Tuesday's sharp drop), and gold and bitcoin both continued their multi-week dollar-weakness-driven rallies. See `usa.md`, `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, and `uk_europe.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

**Reading the table:** the split today is between markets with a same-day AI-anticipation story and everyone else. Taiwan and Korea posted the region's biggest gains, and both have real substance behind them — Taiwan's three institutional-investor categories flipped from a combined net sell yesterday to a **+NT$59.39B net buy** today, and Korea's gain rested on Samsung and SK Hynix's own **90–110 trillion won and 40 trillion won buyback commitments**, respectively (see `taiwan.md`, `korea.md`). Zoom out to the 20-day column and Taiwan (+14.47%) and Korea (+20.22%) are both still working through the same well-documented recovery from their late-July corrections flagged repeatedly in this folder — today's gains are one more chapter in that story, not a new signal on their own. Indonesia is the clear outlier on the 1-day column, and for a genuinely different reason: political caution ahead of an August 27 demonstration, not the Nvidia-anticipation theme driving every other market (see `southeast_asia.md`). Europe stayed the calmest region again, with every benchmark inside a third of a percentage point of flat. The US was similarly quiet in the cash session — the real story arrived after the bell.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** the most recent published ETF flow data (Monday, Aug 24) showed a real defensive rotation — **SPY (−$1.26B), IVV (−$1.49B), QQQ (−$1.6B), and SOXX (−$653M) all saw outflows, while gold's GLD took in +$1.19B**, the largest inflow of the day. That's a sharper "risk-off within equities, into gold" signal than the diversification-not-exit read from the prior week. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** all three institutional investor categories reversed hard into net buying — **foreign +NT$36.60B, dealers +NT$17.93B, investment trusts +NT$4.86B, combined +NT$59.39B** — a sharp turn from yesterday's combined ≈−NT$5.34B net sell. Foreign investors also trimmed their TAIEX futures net-short position for a second straight session. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** a genuinely unusual flow picture — **retail net-sold 2.25 trillion won and foreign investors net-sold a modest 105.2 billion won, yet the index still rose 0.97%**, powered by **1.6 trillion won of corporate buyback activity** (Samsung and SK Hynix's own newly announced programs) plus 764.9 billion won of institutional buying. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** no daily flow print available; the day's gain was led by healthcare/biotech (Innovent Biologics +10.5%) rather than a broad flow signal. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Japan:** no fresh weekly JPX flow data covering today was available (JPX publishes on a weekly lag); the most recent published figure still shows foreign investors net-selling ¥198.8B the prior week, framed as a pause rather than a reversal. See `japan.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Indonesia's selloff was driven by political-event caution, not a flow story; Malaysia's ringgit hit a two-month high (4.0240/USD, +0.5% on the day) alongside its equity gain — a rare case of currency and equity strength moving together in the region today. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** no daily flow signal; the region's calm reflects a genuine "wait for Nvidia" posture rather than a rotation. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** the dollar-weakness trade that has dominated the month continued — **DXY sat around 99.18**, still close to its multi-month low despite a small daily uptick — while **gold traded near $4,675/oz (+8.7% over the past week, +15.9% over the past month)** and **bitcoin held near $79,000 (+21.4% week, +22.1% month)**. Oil eased further (WTI −0.67%, Brent −0.38%) after Tuesday's sharp drop, extending the pullback even as Iran–Oman talks over a Strait of Hormuz shipping corridor reduce supply-risk premia. The 10-year Treasury yield ticked up marginally to 4.65%.

**Net read:** today was a genuine "calm before the catalyst" day almost everywhere — but not a passive one. Taiwan's and Korea's gains were backed by real institutional and corporate capital, not just sentiment, which matters for how Thursday's reaction to Nvidia's actual results plays out. Nvidia's beat-and-raise, its initial ~1.5% after-hours stumble, and its reversal into a ~6–7% gain by Thursday's US open (see `usa.md`) is now the single most important input for every market in this folder — the real test of whether today's anticipation-driven buying across Asia was justified.

## United States

US indices closed little changed (Dow −0.21%, S&P 500 −0.02%, Nasdaq −0.08%) in a quiet session dominated by single-stock news — Meta's $16.7B social-media-addiction settlement, Abercrombie's 35% guidance-driven surge, Salesforce's 13% jump — while July's core PCE inflation held steady at 3.3% YoY. The real story arrived after the bell: Nvidia beat on revenue ($96.2B) and EPS ($2.22), guided Q3 to $108B, and forecast 70% FY2028 growth, triggering an initial ~1.5% after-hours dip that reversed into a sharp ~6–7% gain by Thursday's open. Full write-up — including Treasury yields, credit spreads, and US ETF flows — in **`usa.md`**.

## Japan (Nikkei 225)

Nikkei 225 rose for a second straight session (~+0.5–0.6%, to roughly 66,220–66,260), reversing an intraday loss of over 1%, as chip-related shares tracked South Korea's rebound and real estate/banking/textile stocks led broadly. Advantest, a major Nvidia supplier, stayed defensive into the print (−3.33%). Full write-up in **`japan.md`**.

## Korea (KOSPI)

**KOSPI** closed at **6,808.21**, up **0.97%**, on a genuinely unusual flow picture: retail and foreign investors were both net sellers, but Samsung Electronics' 90–110 trillion won buyback plan and SK Hynix's 40 trillion won buyback-and-retirement program provided enough corporate buying to push the index higher regardless. **KOSDAQ** was essentially flat (826.87, −0.03%). Full write-up in **`korea.md`**.

## Hong Kong (Hang Seng)

**Hang Seng Index** rose **0.56% to 25,652.97**, a second straight gain led by healthcare/biotech — Innovent Biologics jumped 10.5% — alongside modest tech-sector strength tracking the region's pre-Nvidia optimism. Full write-up in **`hongkong.md`**.

## Southeast Asia (Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand)

A genuinely split day. **Indonesia's IHSG** fell **1.48% to 6,405.69** on caution ahead of an August 27 Jakarta demonstration — a local political driver distinct from the region's Nvidia-anticipation theme. **Malaysia's FBM KLCI** snapped a two-day slide, up **0.70% to 1,748.54**, as the ringgit hit a two-month high. **Thailand's SET** rose **0.37% to 1,601.92** on easing Middle East tensions. **Singapore's STI** eased **0.25% to 5,721.59** in an otherwise quiet session. Full write-up, including the 1-week/1-month comparison table, in **`southeast_asia.md`**.

## Taiwan (TAIEX / 加權指數)

Surged **663.16 points (+1.47%) to close at 45,832.62**, its strongest session in weeks, on turnover of **NT$804.25B** — up 11% from the prior day. AI/semiconductor and AI-adjacent names (Global Unichip, Largan Precision, MediaTek) hit or neared daily limits, while TSMC recovered to close positive. ## How to read the 5-day/20-day columns in this report

The 5-day (~1 trading week) and 20-day (~1 trading month) comparisons in this report are measured against each market's own closing level from 5 and 20 trading days before today, sourced from each index's own historical data series (linked in the References section of each file). Bitcoin and Ethereum, which trade 24/7, use "week" and "month" figures reported directly by the cited source rather than a 5-/20-trading-day calculation. Today's Nikkei 225 row uses a percentage-based range rather than a single point value, since available sources diverged materially on the exact closing level while agreeing closely on the percentage move — see `japan.md` for detail.
