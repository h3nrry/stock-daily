# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — August 26, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Singapore (STI)

**Straits Times Index** closed at **5,721.59, down 0.25%**, a modest pullback after Tuesday's 0.97% gain. **Sembcorp Industries** was the top-performing blue chip, up **1.16%**. No broader single catalyst stood out in coverage — this reads as a routine consolidation day rather than a reaction to any specific news, consistent with STI's multi-week pattern of trading near record territory (+33.45% YoY as of late August).

## Indonesia (IHSG / JCI)

**IHSG** fell sharply, down **1.48% to 6,405.69**, with breadth heavily negative — only **136 stocks gained** against **599 decliners**. Unlike the regional "waiting for Nvidia" theme elsewhere, Indonesia's weakness had a distinct **local political driver**: markets grew cautious ahead of a **planned demonstration at Indonesia's Parliament building on August 27**, with reports of provincial (Pati-area) residents mobilizing to join. All major sub-indices fell in tandem — LQ45 (−1.46%), IDX30 (−1.28%), IDX80 (−1.69%) — pointing to broad, index-wide selling rather than a single-sector story. Notably, a handful of small-caps bucked the trend hard: **TMPO rose 34.76%**, with **NICE** and **SAFE** each gaining roughly 25%, underscoring that today's weakness was concentrated in larger, more liquid names.

## Malaysia (FBM KLCI)

**FBM KLCI** snapped a two-day losing streak, closing at **1,748.54, up 0.70% (+12.21 points)**, as trading resumed after Tuesday's public holiday. **Banking counters and YTL-linked stocks led the gains**, with breadth positive (777 advancers vs. 466 decliners). The **ringgit** was a standout mover in its own right, strengthening to **4.0240 per US dollar — its highest level in about two months** (+0.5% on the day, +0.88% year-to-date).

## Thailand (SET)

**SET Index** rose **0.37% to 1,601.92**, on trading value of **THB 71.66 billion**. The rebound was attributed to **easing Middle East tensions, which pulled down both bond yields and oil prices**, plus positive sentiment from the **Thailand Focus 2026** investor symposium and ongoing dividend distributions from listed companies. Analysts flagged a **lack of fresh supporting catalysts** beyond that macro relief, suggesting today's gain was more a relief bounce than the start of a new trend.

**Reading the table:** the region split cleanly into two stories today. Indonesia stands out as the region's clear laggard on the day (−1.48%) on a genuinely local catalyst — pre-demonstration political caution — even as its 20-day trend remains the strongest in the region (+3.56%), a reminder that today's move looks like a discrete, event-driven dip rather than a change in the underlying month-long recovery. Malaysia is the mirror image: today's +0.70% bounce (helped by a public-holiday-shortened week and a stronger ringgit) continues a steady, unspectacular positive grind across all three horizons. Singapore and Thailand both stayed inside a tight band at every horizon, reinforcing Southeast Asia's role as the calmer corner of Asia relative to the chip-driven swings in Taiwan, Korea, and Japan today.
