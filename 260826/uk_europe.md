# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — August 26, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

Europe stayed calm and essentially flat, holding near its recent record-adjacent levels ahead of Nvidia's earnings. **FTSE 100** closed at **10,879.04, down 0.07%** — a rounding-error pullback after Tuesday's 0.29% gain. **DAX** was flat-to-slightly-higher at **26,285.96 (+0.08%)**. **CAC 40** edged up **+0.27% to 8,462.39**. The pan-European **STOXX 600** was dead flat at **656.41 (−0.01%)**. This is the same "calm zone" pattern flagged in recent days — Europe's moves stayed inside a tight band while Asia and the US positioned more actively around the Nvidia print.

## What's driving the calm

- **Nvidia's earnings, released after Europe's close**: European markets, like every market in this folder, spent the session waiting rather than reacting — one analyst quoted in coverage noted "simply beating the $91bn guidance will not be enough," with a Q3 outlook below the roughly $104bn Wall Street target seen as the real risk. In the event, Nvidia's guide came in well above even that bar (see `usa.md`), a genuinely bullish surprise for tomorrow's session.
- **US economic data**: July's PCE inflation print (the Fed's preferred gauge) came in line with expectations at 3.3% YoY, alongside GDP and durable-goods data — inputs into the same Friday Jackson Hole read that's shaping sentiment everywhere.
- **Geopolitical relief continued**: reports of a "temporary corridor through the Strait of Hormuz" being negotiated between Iran and Oman continued to ease oil-supply-disruption fears, a tailwind for inflation expectations that has supported European equities for several sessions now.

## Bottom line

A genuinely quiet session across the UK and continental Europe — all four major benchmarks closed within a third of a percentage point of unchanged, holding steady rather than pre-positioning hard for Nvidia's after-the-close report. That restraint looks well-judged in hindsight: Nvidia's results beat even the higher end of the bar analysts had set, with an initial post-earnings wobble reversing into a sharp gain by Thursday's US open (see `usa.md`). With that uncertainty now resolved to the upside, and Jackson Hole still two days out, Thursday's European open is worth watching for whether the region's AI/semiconductor-adjacent names (ASML and peers) catch a direct bid from Nvidia's guidance, breaking Europe's recent pattern of trading more on its own macro calendar than on US tech-earnings spillover.
