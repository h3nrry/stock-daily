# Korea Stock Market Analysis — August 26, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

**KOSPI** closed at **6,808.21**, up **0.97%**, a second straight day of gains as the index continued recovering from Monday's Samsung-driven scare. **KOSDAQ** closed essentially flat at **826.87 (−0.03%)**. The headline move masks a genuinely interesting divergence underneath: **retail and foreign investors were both net sellers today**, and the index still rose — powered almost entirely by **corporate share buybacks**, not by the domestic institutional/retail buying that drove Tuesday's V-shaped recovery.

## What drove the gain — corporate buybacks, not investor flows

**Samsung Electronics** and **SK Hynix** both announced major shareholder-return programs: Samsung unveiled **a buyback plan worth 90–110 trillion won for this year**, while SK Hynix said it would **repurchase and fully retire 40 trillion won worth of its own shares**. These are extraordinarily large commitments even by the standards of Korea's two largest companies, and they provided enough direct support to push the benchmark higher despite both retail and foreign investors selling on net. One analyst noted "investors should pay attention to the improving resilience within the market" — a reference to the index's ability to absorb outside selling pressure when its largest constituents are actively buying back their own stock.

## Money flow — investor activity

| Investor group | Net flow (Aug 26) |
|---|---|
| Retail/individual investors | **−2.25 trillion won (net sell)** |
| Foreign investors | **−105.2 billion won (net sell, modest)** |
| Institutional investors | **+764.9 billion won (net buy)** |
| Corporate investors (buybacks) | **+1.6 trillion won (net buy)** |

This is a markedly different picture from Tuesday, when institutional (+1.17T won) and retail (+1.05T won) buying together absorbed heavy foreign selling (−3.82T won) to drive the V-shaped reversal. Today, retail flipped to a large net seller, foreign selling narrowed to a rounding error by comparison, and it was specifically **corporate buyback activity** — Samsung and SK Hynix's own announced programs — that provided the marginal buying power. The won also strengthened modestly, to **1,384.8 per dollar (+1.3 won)**.

## Bottom line

A second consecutive up day for KOSPI, but a very different mechanism from Tuesday's broad-based domestic dip-buying: today's gain rested on two of Korea's largest companies putting real capital behind their own stock, even as retail investors took profits and foreign investors stayed net sellers (albeit only modestly). That's a genuinely different — and in some ways more fragile — kind of support than broad-based institutional conviction, since it's concentrated in exactly two names rather than reflecting market-wide buying interest. With Nvidia's results out after Korea's close (see `usa.md`) showing a beat-and-raise that reversed an initial after-hours dip into a sharp gain, Thursday's session — and whether Samsung and SK Hynix's own buyback-driven strength can hold alongside a genuine Nvidia-driven chip-sector move — is the next real test.
