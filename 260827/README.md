# Daily Market Briefing — August 27, 2026 (Thursday)

Global snapshot: markets spent the day delivering their verdict on **Nvidia's Wednesday-evening beat-and-raise** — and the verdict was decisively positive almost everywhere it mattered most. The **Nasdaq (+1.57%)** led US indices as enterprise-software names (Salesforce +22.58%, CrowdStrike +20.50%) piled a second AI-adjacent growth story on top of Nvidia's own 8.74% gain. Korea's **KOSPI (+1.53%)** and Taiwan's **TAIEX (+0.31%, after briefly spiking 550+ points)** both rode the same optimism, though Taiwan gave back most of its opening pop to profit-taking. Japan's **Nikkei (−0.20%)** and Hong Kong's **Hang Seng (−0.34%)** both posted small declines despite the same catalyst — Japan on a single stock's (Advantest) intraday round-trip, Hong Kong on a rotation of preference toward mainland China listings. The day's genuine outlier was **France**, where a fresh political-fiscal crisis (PM Bayrou's confidence-vote gambit) sent the **CAC 40 down ~1.7%** and French bank stocks down 5-7%, while Germany's DAX gained regardless. Indonesia's **IHSG jumped 1.81%** even with a planned political demonstration on the calendar. See `usa.md`, `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, and `uk_europe.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

**Reading the table:** the split today ran along a clear line — genuine, earnings-confirmed AI/tech enthusiasm (Nasdaq, Korea, Taiwan) versus markets where a local story overrode the regional theme (France's political crisis, Indonesia's demonstration-day relief rally, Japan's single-stock drag). Korea's 20-day column (+22.05%) and Taiwan's (+15.13%) both remain the standout multi-week recovery stories in this folder, extending the well-documented rebound from their late-July corrections. France's CAC 40 is the day's clearest negative outlier, and — unlike most of the "calm Europe" days flagged in recent reports — this one has a real, ongoing catalyst (a September 8 confidence vote) rather than just short-term noise.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** ETF flows showed a decisive rotation *into* AI/growth and *out of* broad-market and gold exposure — **QQQ (+$3.67B) and SOXX (+$379.8M) led inflows, while SPY (−$3.82B), IVV (−$920.5M), and even GLD (−$171.1M) saw outflows** — a sharp reversal from the "into gold, out of tech" pattern flagged earlier in the week. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** foreign investors led a second straight day of institutional buying — **foreign +NT$49.09B, dealers +NT$15.03B, investment trusts +NT$0.18B, combined +NT$64.31B** — even as the index itself gave back most of an early 550-point spike. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** the index rallied 1.53%, but retail investors net-sold ₩2.25 trillion, investor cash deposits fell below ₩100 trillion for the first time in nine sessions, and margin loans rose for a seventh straight day — a genuine "borrowed-money" caution flag underneath the gain. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** no dominant flow story; the session's modest decline reflected relative preference for mainland Shanghai listings (SSE Composite +1.13%) over Hong Kong ones. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Indonesia's rebound was broad-based across every sector (healthcare +6.26% led) despite a planned demonstration; Malaysia's pullback came specifically from financials and plantations even as tech-adjacent names were expected to benefit from Nvidia's results. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** French bank stocks (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole) fell 5-7% on a fresh political-fiscal risk story — PM Bayrou's September 8 confidence-vote gambit over a €43.8B fiscal package — the day's most concentrated flow-relevant move in the region. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** the dollar held roughly flat (**DXY ~99.16**), gold ticked up modestly to **~$4,657.69/oz (+1.9% week, +11.95% month)**, and **bitcoin rose to ~$80,254 (+14.1% week, +20.9% month)** while **ethereum traded near $2,499 (+11.3% week, +28.3% month)**. Oil rose alongside the broader risk-on tone (WTI +1.58% to $83.53, Brent +1.82% to $88.52), a reversal from the week's earlier pullback. The 10-year Treasury yield held steady near 4.66%.

**Net read:** today confirmed that Wednesday's Nvidia earnings anticipation across Asia was justified — a real, broadening AI/tech rally (helped by a surprise second engine in enterprise software) carried through the US session and most of Asia. But two genuine new storylines emerged that deserve tracking into Friday and beyond: Korea's rally is increasingly financed by margin debt rather than fresh cash, and France's political-fiscal risk is back on the table with a hard September 8 deadline. Both are worth watching independent of Friday's Jackson Hole speech, which remains the week's single biggest remaining catalyst (see `usa.md`).

## United States

US indices delivered a strong, earnings-confirmed rally: **S&P 500 7,730.99 (+0.72%)**, **Nasdaq 26,541.35 (+1.57%)**, **Dow 53,569.44 (+0.20%)**. Nvidia rose 8.74% on its 13th straight quarter of guidance beats, while enterprise-software names Salesforce (+22.58%, on a new Anthropic partnership), CrowdStrike (+20.50%), and Veeva (+15.20%) delivered their own blowout results — broadening the AI trade beyond chips. ETF flows confirmed real rotation into QQQ/SOXX and out of SPY/IVV/GLD. Full write-up — including Treasury yields, credit spreads, and ETF flows — in **`usa.md`**.

## Taiwan (TAIEX / 加權指數)

Gained **142.60 points (+0.31%) to close at 45,975.22**, after an early 550+ point spike to above 46,400 was largely given back to profit-taking. **Largan Precision hit limit-up to a new record (~NT$6,915)** on CPO (co-packaged optics) supply-chain speculation — a genuine new bullish narrative, and the first of two straight limit-up sessions (see `../260828/taiwan.md`). Institutional investors added a second straight day of solid net buying (**+NT$64.31B**, foreign-led). Full breakdown in **`taiwan.md`**.

## Japan (Nikkei 225)

Closed at **66,123.50, down 132.96 points (−0.20%)**, in a session "lacking clear direction" — the entire decline traced to Advantest's intraday round-trip (a ~203-yen index drag at its worst, before partial recovery), while breadth stayed close to even and TOPIX kept extending its own separate winning streak. Full write-up in **`japan.md`**.

## Korea (KOSPI)

**KOSPI** rose **1.53% to 6,912.37**, riding the region's AI-optimism wave, but with a genuine caution flag underneath: retail investors sold ₩2.25 trillion net, investor cash deposits fell below ₩100 trillion for the first time in nine sessions, and margin debt rose for a seventh consecutive day. **KOSDAQ** rose 1.30% to 837.65. Full write-up in **`korea.md`**.

## Hong Kong (Hang Seng)

**Hang Seng Index** eased **~0.3-0.34% to 25,565.74**, diverging from the region's broader optimism as capital showed relative preference for mainland Shanghai listings (SSE Composite +1.13%) over Hong Kong ones. Full write-up in **`hongkong.md`**.

## Southeast Asia (Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand)

**Indonesia's IHSG surged 1.81% to 6,521.75** — a broad, all-sector rebound (healthcare +6.26% led) that came despite, not because of, a planned Jakarta demonstration on the calendar. **Singapore's STI** eased 0.65% to 5,684.12, **Thailand's SET** was essentially flat (−0.08% to 1,600.70), and **Malaysia's FBM KLCI** slipped at midday (~−0.35%) on financial-sector weakness even as tech names were expected to benefit from Nvidia's results. Full write-up in **`southeast_asia.md`**.

## UK & Europe

A genuinely eventful session broke Europe's recent calm pattern. **CAC 40 fell ~1.68% to 8,319.87** as French bank stocks (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole) dropped 5-7% on PM Bayrou's confidence-vote gambit over a €43.8B fiscal package, due September 8. **DAX rose 0.31% to 26,367.24**, bucking the French-driven weakness. **FTSE 100 fell 0.79% to 10,792.54**. Full write-up in **`uk_europe.md`**.
