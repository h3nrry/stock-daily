# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 9, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

Japan's Nikkei 225 declined for a second straight session, though far more mildly than Tuesday's sharp drop. The index **closed at 65,142.78, down 126.55 points (−0.19%)**, holding the psychologically important 65,000 level after opening lower (touching 65,076 early) on weak overnight US trading, then stabilizing through the session. The close reconciles exactly against Tuesday's confirmed 65,269.33 (see `../260908/japan.md`).

## What drove the session

- **The market opened weak, tracking overnight US futures softness, but found support near 65,000** on two specific catalysts: **US semiconductor strength** (Qualcomm's newly reported AI-data-center chip partnership with Amazon lifted sentiment for Japan's own chip-supply-chain names) and **a pause in Tuesday's sharp yen appreciation** — USD/JPY eased back toward the **153 level**, a partial retracement of Tuesday's safe-haven-driven yen rally toward 152, which took some pressure off export-oriented names.
- **Non-ferrous metals led all 33 TSE sector indices, surging 6.88%**, as LME 3-month copper futures hit record highs — a genuine commodity story layered on top of the broader Iran/oil-driven macro backdrop. **Oil & coal products (+5.01%) and mining (+2.17%)** also ranked among the day's strongest sectors, tracking Brent crude's push above $100/barrel overnight (see `../260909/usa.md`).
- **Optical-fiber and cable makers found support on a fresh demand story**: Corning's newly reported long-term supply contract with Verizon boosted sentiment for Japanese cable makers **Fujikura and Furukawa Electric**.
- **Two large-cap decliners did most of the index-level damage**: **Advantest and Fast Retailing together dragged the Nikkei down roughly 382 points**, while **SoftBank Group contributed about +204 points** on the upside — a reminder of how concentrated the Nikkei 225's price-weighted mechanics can make single-stock moves.
- **Sector weakness was concentrated in securities/brokerage, services, and retail names**, while the broader session stayed in a narrow, cautious range ahead of the **September 11 major settlement date (メジャーSQ)** and next week's Japanese and US monetary-policy decisions.

## Money flow — 外国人 (foreign investors)

Same-day foreign-investor flow data via JPX's weekly investor-type statistics was not available at writing time (JPX publishes on a weekly, not daily, cadence — a recurring limitation for this file). Wednesday's price action — a broad-based commodity/resource-sector rally (non-ferrous metals, oil & coal, mining) alongside a stabilizing yen — is consistent with a session driven more by global commodity and currency cross-currents than a directional shift in foreign-investor positioning; this remains a read on sentiment rather than a confirmed flow figure.

## Stocks/sectors to watch

| Stock/Sector | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Non-ferrous metals sector | +6.88%, the day's clear sector leader on record LME copper prices — a genuine commodity story rather than a Japan-specific one | A pullback in copper prices, or a de-escalation of the Iran conflict removing the broader commodity bid, would quickly cool this sector's momentum |
| Advantest & Fast Retailing | Together dragged the index down ~382 points — worth watching whether this is idiosyncratic profit-taking or the start of renewed weakness in two of the Nikkei's most influential single names | Continued weakness here could offset any broader commodity/yen-driven tailwind for the headline index |
| USD/JPY (153 level) | A pause in Tuesday's sharp yen-appreciation move — whether the yen resumes strengthening (renewed Iran-driven safe-haven flows) or continues easing is the clearest cross-asset signal to watch into Thursday | A snap-back toward 152 or below would revive Tuesday's export-sector pressure |
| Fujikura / Furukawa Electric | Optical-fiber/cable names supported by Corning's new long-term Verizon supply deal — a fresh, company-specific demand catalyst distinct from the day's broader macro themes | Depends on confirmation of order volumes flowing through from the US contract, which is not yet visible in Japanese data |

## Bottom line

A mild, broadly stabilizing session after Tuesday's sharp reversal — Nikkei −0.19% to 65,142.78, holding the 65,000 level as a pause in the yen's rally and US semiconductor strength offset overnight weakness. The clearest genuine story beneath the modest headline move was a sharp commodity-sector rotation — non-ferrous metals up nearly 7% on record copper prices — layered on top of the same Iran/oil-driven backdrop weighing on markets globally. With Thursday's US inflation data, the September 11 settlement date, and next week's BOJ/Fed decisions all approaching, the market's cautious, narrow-range posture looks deliberate rather than directionless.
