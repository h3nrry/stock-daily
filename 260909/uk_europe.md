# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 9, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

European markets fell sharply, tracking the same overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation and oil's break above $100/barrel that hit US markets the same day. **FTSE 100 closed at 10,698.87 (−1.04%)**, **DAX 40 at 25,576.45 (−1.66%)**, **CAC 40 at 8,156.67 (−1.28%, see note below)**, and the pan-European **STOXX 600 at 640.41 (−1.41%)** — a genuinely broad, one-directional selloff, in sharp contrast to Tuesday's quiet, near-flat session.

## What drove the session

- **The US military destroyed five Iranian oil tankers overnight, in retaliation for Iran's ballistic-missile strikes on American naval vessels** — the second such strike exchange in two days. This drove **Brent crude above $100/barrel for the first time since July**, with **WTI up over 2%**, directly reviving the same inflation concerns that have periodically hit European markets throughout this conflict.
- **Rising oil prices pushed the region's own bond yields higher in sympathy with the US**, where the **10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.808–4.843%**, its highest level in roughly two decades — a genuine cross-asset headwind for European equities just ahead of the **European Central Bank's own policy meeting**, where markets are pricing a likely **25-basis-point rate increase**, and against an already-hot August Eurozone inflation backdrop (3.3% year-on-year, per Tuesday's reporting).
- **Germany's DAX and France's CAC 40 led the region's decline**, both falling more than 1.6%, while the **FTSE 100's more modest 1.04% decline** made it the region's relative outperformer for the session — a reversal of Tuesday's pattern, when the FTSE and DAX both held up better than the region's other benchmarks.
- **The selloff was genuinely broad rather than concentrated in any single sector or story**: unlike some recent sessions where a specific catalyst (a rate decision, a single large-cap earnings report) drove the move, Wednesday's decline tracked the shared, global Iran/oil-driven risk-off tone that also hit US equities sharply the same day (see `../260909/usa.md`).

## Bottom line

A genuinely sharp, broad-based selloff that reversed Tuesday's quiet, near-flat session — Europe's major indices fell between roughly 1% and 1.7% on the same overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation and oil spike that hit US markets hard the same day. Germany's DAX and France's CAC 40 led the decline, while the FTSE 100's smaller loss made it the region's relative outperformer. With Brent above $100/barrel directly threatening a fresh round of European inflation just ahead of the ECB's own policy meeting, and Treasury-yield-driven pressure spilling over from the US, the region's next catalysts are squarely the ECB's rate decision and the ongoing trajectory of the Iran conflict — both due imminently.
