# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 9, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

US markets fell for a third consecutive session as the Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz conflict escalated sharply overnight and oil broke above the $100/barrel threshold for the first time since July. The **Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 405.41 points (−0.77%) to 52,380.66**, the **S&P 500 fell 37.16 points (−0.48%) to 7,636.36**, and the **Nasdaq Composite fell 168.07 points (−0.64%) to 26,253.34**, with the small-cap **Russell 2000 the session's worst performer, down 1.36% to 2,919.86**. Nearly 70% of S&P 500 constituents declined. All figures reconcile arithmetically against Tuesday's confirmed close (see `../260908/usa.md`).

## What drove the market

- **A serious overnight escalation between the US and Iran**: the US military destroyed five Iranian oil tankers in retaliation for Iran launching ballistic missiles at American naval vessels — the second such strike exchange in two days. With no de-escalation in sight, **Brent crude surpassed $100/barrel for the first time since July, settling around $101**, while **WTI crude jumped 2.5–3.25% to roughly $95–96/barrel**.
- **Treasury yields hit fresh 52-week highs** as the oil spike reignited inflation concerns just as markets await Friday's PPI/CPI data: the **2-year yield rose to 4.427% (+2.9bp)**, the **10-year yield jumped to 4.843% (+3.9bp) — its highest level since October 2023**, and the **30-year yield rose to 5.294% (+3bp)**. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced plans to **triple the next long-term bond buyback operation from $2 billion to $6 billion** in an attempt to curb rising borrowing costs, a move that initially drew a negative market reaction.
- **Sentiment deteriorated sharply**: the CNN/CNBC-style Fear & Greed Index dropped to **42 ("Fear")**, down from 52 ("Neutral") and 59 ("Greed") in recent prior readings, as investors weighed the energy shock against an already-hawkish Fed backdrop. Odds of a rate hike at the **September 15–16 FOMC meeting held near 60%** per CME Group's FedWatch tool.
- **Apple unveiled its first foldable iPhone Duo (starting at $1,999), new iPhone 18 Pro models, and an upgraded Apple Watch Series** at its autumn hardware event — but Apple's own stock edged lower despite the launch, a muted market reaction to a major product-cycle event.
- **Meta Platforms rose roughly 5–6%** after launching **Muse**, a new AI personal-assistant/chatbot, which analysts framed as potentially "the beginning of a substantial product cycle." **Marvell Technology jumped 6.4%** after its CEO raised revenue targets substantially. On the downside, **Casey's General Stores fell 16.5%** on disappointing same-store sales, and **Fox and Roku both fell roughly 2%** after the Justice Department requested additional information on Fox's proposed $22 billion Roku acquisition, extending the regulatory review timeline.

## Treasury yields

Yields across the curve set fresh multi-year highs on the oil-driven inflation scare: **2-year 4.427% (+2.9bp)**, **10-year 4.843% (+3.9bp, highest since October 2023)**, **30-year 5.294% (+3bp)**. The move came despite the Treasury's announced tripling of its long-term buyback program, underscoring how directly the Iran/oil shock is now feeding into rate expectations ahead of this week's inflation data and the September 15–16 FOMC meeting.

## Credit spreads

FRED's most recently posted readings — **ICE BofA US High Yield OAS at 2.67%** and **ICE BofA US Investment-Grade (Corporate Index) OAS at 0.81%** — remain dated September 8 (FRED's typical ~1-business-day publication lag). Both readings are unchanged from Tuesday and still historically tight, suggesting credit markets have not yet repriced meaningfully for the equity selloff or the sharp rise in Treasury yields.

## Oil, gold, bitcoin

**Brent crude surpassed $100/barrel for the first time since July, settling near $101**, while **WTI rose 2.5–3.25% to roughly $95.39–96.05** — the US military's destruction of five Iranian oil tankers, in retaliation for Iran's ballistic-missile strikes on American naval vessels, kept the supply-risk premium fully live. **Gold's move diverged sharply by source and moment in the session**: December COMEX gold futures were reported up 0.49% to $4,460.70 by one recap, while another described gold futures opening at $4,399 (−0.9% from Tuesday's close) before recovering to about $4,438.20 by mid-morning; **spot gold per goldprice.org fell more sharply, down 1.67% to $4,349.97** — a genuinely mixed, dip-then-partial-recovery session rather than a clean move in either direction, with the higher-for-longer rate repricing evidently working against gold's usual safe-haven bid even as oil spiked on the same conflict. **Bitcoin opened at $78,446.18 (−0.8% from Tuesday) and traded around $78,824.54 by mid-morning**, while **Ethereum opened at $2,484.93 (−0.2%) and traded near $2,486.04** — crypto commentary described the Middle East escalation as "keeping a lid" on prices, with a potential "double squeeze" flagged if the conflict persists alongside an expected 25bp Fed hike, since non-yielding risk assets face pressure from both directions at once.

## Bottom line

A genuine, broad-based risk-off session driven almost entirely by a serious overnight US-Iran escalation — the destruction of five Iranian oil tankers pushed Brent above $100/barrel for the first time since July and sent Treasury yields to fresh multi-year highs, with the 10-year at its highest level since October 2023. The Dow's steeper decline reflects broader market weakness (nearly 70% of stocks fell, Russell 2000 down 1.36%) even as pockets of genuine strength persisted — Meta's Muse AI launch and Marvell's raised guidance drove sharp single-stock gains, while Apple's major iPhone Duo foldable unveiling drew a surprisingly muted market reaction. Credit spreads stayed calm, suggesting fixed-income markets still read this as a geopolitical/commodity shock rather than a credit-quality event, even as the Fear & Greed Index's slide to 42 shows sentiment deteriorating meaningfully. Friday's PPI/CPI data and the trajectory of the Iran conflict remain the two clearest catalysts into the September 15–16 FOMC meeting.
