# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 9, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia traded broadly lower, aligning with the region-wide and global risk-off tone driven by the overnight Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz escalation and oil's push above $100/barrel. Singapore, Indonesia, and Thailand all closed modestly lower, while Malaysia was essentially flat — a far more uniform picture than the sharp Singapore/Indonesia divergence seen in recent sessions.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,729.63, down roughly 37.8 points (−0.66%)** from Tuesday's 5,767.45, opening weak (**down 26.03 points, −0.45%, to 5,741.42 at 9:19am**) and extending losses through the session. **Financial heavyweights DBS (−0.35%), OCBC Bank, and UOB all declined**, and the **REIT sector was mostly weaker**, with **CapitaLand Ascendas REIT down 0.42%**; **Singtel was among the few gainers**. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−0.25% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **+0.16% over 20 trading days (~1 month)**, per Investing.com's historical-data series — Wednesday's decline erased the week's earlier modest gain but left the longer-term trend still marginally positive.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,678.20, down 8.21 points (−0.12%)** — a much milder pullback than the region's initial risk-off tone might have suggested, reversing only a small part of Tuesday's +1.01% rally. Market breadth was slightly negative, with **317 declining stocks versus 308 advancing** (169 unchanged) on turnover of **Rp16.17 trillion** across 34.51 billion shares. **Non-primary consumer, healthcare, and financial sectors saw the deepest corrections**, with **Bank Central Asia (BBCA) the single largest drag**, contributing roughly 14 points to the index's decline. Drivers cited alongside the Iran/US tension included a live **US-Canada trade dispute** and oil's push toward $100 (Brent above $99, WTI above $94), with investors also watching **China's same-day CPI/PPI release** (see `../260909/hongkong.md`) and Indonesia's own consumer-confidence data. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+1.25% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **+4.78% over 20 trading days (~1 month)**, per Investing.com's historical-data series — both still solidly positive despite Wednesday's small pullback.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,714.34, down just 0.06 points** — essentially unchanged, holding steady even as Brent crude topped $100/barrel overnight. Cautious sentiment dominated trading despite the muted headline move, with **Zetrix AI Bhd the most actively traded counter (659.66 million shares), declining 3.5 sen to 27 sen**, while **Gamuda gained 12 sen to RM4.92** (touching RM5 intraday), **JAKS Resources rose 1 sen to 13 sen**, and **Ni Hsin Group and AHB Holdings each added 1 sen**. Market volume fell to 3.66 billion units even as transaction value rose to RM3.49 billion. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+0.33% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **−1.57% over 20 trading days (~1 month)**, per Investing.com's historical-data series.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,617.89, down 4.00 points (−0.25%)** on trading value of **THB 81.33 billion**, a genuinely two-sided session per Kaohoon: selling pressure came from the **electronics and banking sectors** (with some banking names trading ex-dividend), while **higher crude prices directly benefited local energy companies**, cushioning the broader decline — the same oil-price spike acting simultaneously as headwind and tailwind within a single market, echoing the pattern seen in Tuesday's session. The market **"moved sideways-down within a limited range"** rather than showing a clear directional break. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+2.72% over 5 trading days (~1 week)** and **+0.33% over 20 trading days (~1 month, using Aug 11, 2026 as the closest available baseline)**.

## Bottom line

A far more uniform session than recent days' Singapore/Indonesia divergence — all four markets either declined modestly or held flat, tracking the same overnight Iran/oil-driven global risk-off tone that hit US and European markets sharply the same day (see `../260909/usa.md`, `uk_europe.md`). Indonesia's pullback was notably shallow given it was reversing part of Tuesday's rally, while Thailand's energy-sector strength again partly offset broader weakness from its own oil exposure. Malaysia's essentially flat close, despite Brent crossing $100, stands out as the region's most resilient print of the day. Every market's 5-day and 20-day figures remain positive or only mildly negative, suggesting Wednesday's softness reads as a pause within otherwise-constructive medium-term trends rather than a reversal.
