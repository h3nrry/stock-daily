# Daily Market Briefing — September 9, 2026 (Wednesday)

Global snapshot: markets split sharply along an East/West line as a serious overnight **US-Iran escalation** — the US military destroyed five Iranian oil tankers in retaliation for Iran launching ballistic missiles at American naval vessels — pushed **Brent crude above $100/barrel for the first time since July** and drove **US Treasury yields to fresh 52-week highs** (10-year 4.843%, highest since October 2023). **US markets fell for a third straight session** (Dow −0.77%, S&P −0.48%, Nasdaq −0.64%), and **Europe fell sharply across the board** (DAX −1.66%, CAC 40 −1.28%, STOXX 600 −1.41%, FTSE −1.04%) on the same oil/yield shock, just ahead of the ECB's own policy meeting. Asia told a genuinely different story: **Korea's KOSPI surged 1.40% to reclaim 7,000 for the first time in 33 trading days** on strong foreign-and-institutional buying into semiconductors, **Taiwan's TAIEX edged up 0.16%** despite TSMC reversing to a loss, and **Japan's Nikkei fell just 0.19%**, stabilizing near 65,000 as a pause in the yen's rally and US chip-sector strength offset overnight weakness. **Hong Kong slipped 0.17%** on a consumer-stock selloff (Haidilao −9%+) even as China's same-day CPI/PPI data came in largely as expected. Southeast Asia was broadly, mildly negative (Singapore −0.66%, Indonesia −0.12%, Thailand −0.25%) with Malaysia essentially flat. Gold's reaction was genuinely mixed by source and moment — an initial dip on the Iran news partially recovered through the morning — while bitcoin and ethereum stayed pinned near recent lows as the conflict and looming Fed decision both weigh on risk assets without yield.

**Reading the table:** Wednesday's clearest story is a genuine East/West split, not a single global shock landing uniformly. The overnight US-Iran escalation and its knock-on effect on oil and Treasury yields hit the US and Europe hard and uniformly — every Western index fell, several by more than 1%. Asia, by contrast, largely shrugged it off: Korea's chip-driven rally (KOSPI +1.40%, reclaiming 7,000 for the first time in 33 sessions) and Taiwan's own institutional-buying-driven gain (+0.16% despite TSMC's reversal) both show the region's dominant AI/semiconductor demand narrative currently outweighing the same geopolitical risk that dragged Western markets lower on the identical calendar day. Japan's mild decline (−0.19%) reflects a market caught between the two forces — some overnight weakness from the West, offset by the same chip-sector strength lifting Korea. Korea's KOSPI remains the standout number in the table on both the 5-day (+7.45%) and 20-day (+7.18%) views, a reminder of just how sharp this month's rally has been. Europe's across-the-board declines, in contrast to Tuesday's quiet session, show just how directly the region's markets are exposed to the same oil/yield shock hitting the US, layered on top of the region's own upcoming ECB decision.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** a genuine, broad risk-off session — Dow −0.77%, S&P −0.48%, Nasdaq −0.64% (its relative resilience continuing) — driven almost entirely by the overnight Iran/oil escalation and fresh 52-week-high Treasury yields (10Y 4.843%, highest since Oct 2023). Nearly 70% of S&P 500 stocks fell and the Fear & Greed Index dropped to 42 ("Fear"). Meta's new Muse AI-agent launch (+5-6%) and Marvell's raised guidance (+6.4%) drove the sharpest single-stock gains even as Apple's major iPhone Duo foldable unveiling drew a surprisingly muted stock reaction. Credit spreads stayed calm (HY OAS 2.67%, IG OAS 0.81%), suggesting fixed income still reads this as a commodity/geopolitical shock rather than a credit event. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** a genuinely constructive session beneath a modest headline number — TAIEX +0.16% to 47,183.36 despite a 490-point intraday swing and TSMC reversing to a NT$5 loss. The real story is NT$220.81 billion in net institutional buying (foreign +NT$209.24bn, trust +NT$30.40bn), the largest since Monday's record, concentrated in a clean "dual boards" (panel/substrate) and Apple-supply-chain-optics rotation that offset both TSMC's weakness and a broadly lower financial sector. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** the region's standout — KOSPI +1.40% to 7,051.64, reclaiming 7,000 for the first time in 33 trading days on a "twin pincer" of foreign (+₩382bn) and institutional (+₩294bn) buying concentrated in semiconductors, with SK Hynix (+3.31%) the clearest single-stock engine on continued HBM-dominance expectations. The won strengthened in step, reinforcing rather than fighting the equity rally. See `korea.md`.
- **Japan:** a mild, stabilizing session after Tuesday's sharp reversal — Nikkei −0.19% to 65,142.78, holding 65,000 as a pause in the yen's rally and US semiconductor strength offset overnight weakness. Non-ferrous metals (+6.88%) led all 33 sectors on record LME copper prices, a genuine commodity story layered on the same global oil/Iran backdrop. See `japan.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a mild, selectively-driven decline — Hang Seng −0.17%, with a genuine consumer/dining-stock selloff (Haidilao −9%+) the clearest sector story, even as technology names traded in genuinely mixed fashion (Baidu up, Meituan/NetEase down) and optical-communications names rallied sharply (+7-19%+). China's same-day August CPI (+0.8% y/y) and PPI (+3.8% y/y) data came in largely as expected and does not appear to have been the session's dominant driver. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** broadly, mildly negative and far more uniform than recent sessions' Singapore/Indonesia divergence — Singapore −0.66%, Indonesia −0.12% (a shallow pullback given it reversed only part of Tuesday's rally), Thailand −0.25% (with energy names cushioning the decline on the same oil spike), and Malaysia essentially flat despite Brent crossing $100. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** a sharp, broad-based reversal of Tuesday's quiet session — FTSE −1.04%, DAX −1.66%, CAC 40 −1.28% (recomputed; see note above), STOXX 600 −1.41% — tracking the same overnight Iran/oil shock and the spillover into US Treasury yields, just ahead of the ECB's own policy meeting where a 25bp hike is priced in against an already-hot August inflation backdrop. See `uk_europe.md`.

## Oil

**Brent crude surpassed $100/barrel for the first time since July, settling around $101**, while **WTI rose 2.5–3.25% to roughly $95.39–96.05/barrel** — the US military's destruction of five Iranian oil tankers, in retaliation for Iran's ballistic-missile strikes on American naval vessels, marked the second such strike exchange in two days and kept the supply-risk premium fully live. Oil was the single clearest cross-market driver of Wednesday's session, acting as a direct headwind for the broad US market and for energy-import-dependent Europe, a mixed force for Southeast Asia (a headwind for most sectors but a tailwind for Thailand's and Indonesia's own energy-linked names), and a genuine commodity tailwind for Japan's non-ferrous-metals sector even as it fed the same yield/inflation concerns pressuring equities generally. See `usa.md`.

## Gold

Gold's reaction was genuinely mixed by source and by moment within the session. One recap described **December COMEX gold futures up 0.49% to $4,460.70**; another described **gold futures opening at $4,399 (−0.9% from Tuesday's close) before recovering to about $4,438.20 by mid-morning**; and **spot gold per goldprice.org fell more sharply, down 1.67% to $4,349.97**. Rather than the metal's usual safe-haven bid responding cleanly to the overnight Iran escalation, gold appears to have initially dipped — likely on the same higher-for-longer rate repricing pressuring other assets — before partially recovering through the session, a genuinely unsettled, dip-then-partial-recovery pattern rather than a clean move in either direction.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin opened Wednesday at $78,446.18, down 0.8% from Tuesday, and traded around $78,824.54 by mid-morning**, while **Ethereum opened at $2,484.93 (−0.2%) and traded near $2,486.04**. Crypto commentary described the ongoing Middle East escalation as "keeping a lid" on prices, flagging a potential "double squeeze" if the conflict persists alongside an expected 25-basis-point Fed rate hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting — a scenario in which non-yielding risk assets face pressure from both a geopolitical risk-off flow and a higher-rate environment simultaneously.
