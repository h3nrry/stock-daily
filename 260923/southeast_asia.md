# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 23, 2026 (Wednesday)

**Summary:** Mixed again, but Wednesday's leader was **Indonesia (+1.56%)**, which rebounded from a multi-week low after **Bank Indonesia held its policy rate at 5.75%**. **Thailand (+0.39%)** gained a third straight day, led by tech names. **Singapore (−0.24%)** and **Malaysia (−0.42%)** slipped on profit-taking. Every regional close was confirmed from same-day sources, including Singapore, which is often a gap in this project.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

| Item | Reading |
|---|---|
| IHSG close (Sept 23) | **6,374.91, +97.87 points (+1.56%)** (CNBC Indonesia, Bisnis, IDN Times, Okezone) |
| Turnover | Rp12.57 trillion; 361.71 million lots; 1.91 million transactions |
| Leading sectors | Consumer +4.6%, energy +3.4%, basic materials +3.1% |
| Key movers | Bank Central Asia (BBCA), Bayan Resources (BYAN), Dian Swastatika Sentosa (DSSA), Merdeka Copper Gold (MDKA), Amman Mineral (AMMN) |

**Driver: Bank Indonesia held the BI-Rate at 5.75%** for a third straight meeting (deposit facility 4.75%, lending facility 6.50%). Coming after two straight losing sessions, including Tuesday's −1.69% drop to a multi-week low, the hold set off a broad rebound led by commodity and consumer stocks. **Data note:** Media Keuangan reported a slightly different close (6,379.66, +1.64%). CNBC Indonesia's end-of-day figure, which four other outlets also reported, is used here.

## Thailand — SET

| Item | Reading |
|---|---|

| Value traded | THB 64.41 billion |

**Driver:** tech stocks, led by **Delta Electronics (Thailand) (DELTA)**, plus lower oil prices and US yields during Asian hours, which helped tourism and power stocks. Kaohoon also cited hopes for Middle East de-escalation and the US–China summit. The government separately presented investment priorities (data centers, energy transition) to institutional investors. Kaohoon's analyst expected a sideways session next.

## Bottom line

Southeast Asia split again. **Indonesia jumped 1.56% after the BI rate hold** and Thailand edged up on tech strength, while Singapore (−0.24%) and Malaysia (−0.42%) took profits. All four closes came before the US PMI surprise sent the 10-year Treasury yield to 5.11%, a 2007 high. Thursday showed how rate-sensitive these markets are: all four fell (see `../260924/southeast_asia.md`).
