# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 23, 2026 (Wednesday)

**European stocks closed lower as strong PMI data pushed euro-area government bond yields to multi-decade highs.** Traders now expect both the ECB and the Fed to keep raising rates. **DAX −0.66% to 25,410.63, CAC 40 −0.39% to 8,123.41, STOXX 600 −0.44% to 639.92, FTSE 100 essentially flat (−0.03%) at 10,705.26.** Energy was the only sector that rose. Insurers, banks and German automakers led the decline.

## What drove the market

- **Bond yields jumped.** Strong September flash PMIs for both services and manufacturing, with higher energy costs being passed into output prices, raised expectations that the **ECB will keep hiking this year** (Trading Economics). **Germany's 10-year Bund yield rose 11.6bp to 3.560%**, and **France's 10-year OAT rose 16.8bp to 4.660%**, widening the French–German spread again (investingLive). The US PMI surprise later in the day (composite 58.4 vs 55.3 expected) pushed the **US 10-year up 11.6bp to about 5.08–5.11%**, a 2007 high (see `usa.md`).
- **Insurers and banks fell with bond prices.** **Allianz −3.8% to −4.0%** (Trading Economics, Saxo), **Deutsche Bank −2.6%**, **BNP Paribas −1%+**.
- **German automakers fell.** **Mercedes-Benz and BMW each lost about 3%.**
- **Energy was the only sector to rise** (Saxo). Brent rose 3.86% to $103.08 after Iran's president said Tehran would "never surrender" to the US, reversing five straight days of declines (see `README.md`).
- **London held up best.** The FTSE 100 was flat (−0.03%), supported by its heavy weighting in oil majors, which offset rate-sensitive losses elsewhere.

## Money-flow analysis

This was a **rates-driven selloff, not a growth scare**. Money came out of long-duration and rate-sensitive stocks (insurers that hold large bond portfolios, autos, growth names) and went into energy. The data behind it were strong: activity and prices were both running hot. That is the kind of day when good economic news is bad for stocks. France's larger yield jump (+16.8bp vs Germany's +11.6bp) is a reminder that French fiscal risk, flagged repeatedly in this project, is still an issue. Rising yields on both sides of the Atlantic also pushed the dollar index back above 100 (USAGOLD), which hurt gold.

## Bottom line

Europe **fell on a rates shock, not bad news**: strong PMIs sent euro-area yields to multi-decade highs (Bund 3.56%, OAT 4.66%), increasing expectations of more ECB hikes. The DAX (−0.66%), CAC 40 (−0.39%) and STOXX 600 (−0.44%) all fell, with insurers and autos leading. Only the energy-heavy FTSE 100 held flat (−0.03%). The US PMI surprise later in the day made the rate move bigger on both sides of the Atlantic.
