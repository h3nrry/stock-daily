# Daily Market Briefing — September 23, 2026 (Wednesday)

**Asia went up; the West sold off on rates.** Asian markets closed before the US data and mostly rose. **Taiwan's TAIEX closed at another record (48,157.29, +0.75%)**, led by TSMC's return to NT$2,500 and a memory-stock rally. **Korea's KOSPI rose 0.90% to 7,080.92** on its last session before Chuseok, driven by Samsung Electronics (+3.07%). **Indonesia jumped 1.56%** after Bank Indonesia held rates. **Hong Kong was the exception (−1.01%)**, as AI-model and internet stocks gave back Tuesday's gains ahead of the Trump–Xi summit. **Japan was closed** (Autumnal Equinox Day), with Osaka holiday futures settling 1,801 yen above the last cash close. Later in the day, **September flash PMIs came in much stronger than expected on both sides of the Atlantic** (US composite 58.4 vs 55.3 expected, a five-year high). **The US 10-year yield jumped to ~5.1%, its highest since 2007**, the Bund hit 3.56% and the OAT 4.66%. Stocks fell: **DAX −0.66%, STOXX 600 −0.44%, S&P 500 −0.75%, Nasdaq −1.13%, Russell 2000 −1.77%**. **Brent rose 3.86% to $103.08** after Iran's president vowed never to "surrender", ending a five-day decline and adding to inflation worries.

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** foreign investors bought for a **fifth straight day (+NT$37.313bn** of a +NT$38.922bn institutional total), concentrated in TSMC and memory (Nanya +3.62%). **Nearly 1,000 listed/OTC stocks fell**, so the record was carried by a few heavyweights. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** closed. Holiday futures settled at **66,820** (+1,720 yen over three days). The yen weakened into the **158 range** as the US 10-year hit 5.1%. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** individuals sold **₩1.44 trillion** and foreigners **₩511 billion** before the holiday. Institutions (+₩319bn) and other corporations bought, mostly Samsung. KRX closed Sept 24–25. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** southbound buyers stayed net positive (**+HK$3.449bn**) and bought Tencent and Alibaba on the dip, while selling into Kingboard Laminates' +11% jump. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Indonesia rebounded on the BI hold (consumer +4.6%, energy +3.4%); Malaysia and Singapore took profits; Thailand rose on tech (DELTA). See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **UK/Europe:** money moved **out of rate-sensitive stocks (insurers like Allianz −4%, autos −3%, banks) into energy**. French yields rose most (+16.8bp). See `uk_europe.md`.
- **United States:** a bear-flattening bond selloff pushed money out of small caps (−1.77%), housing and AI-disruption-exposed financials and travel stocks (Schwab, JPM, Booking, Expedia). It went into energy, cybersecurity and Micron (+5% ahead of Sept 30 earnings). See `usa.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** the dollar index moved back above 100; gold (−1.2%), silver (−3.1%) and bitcoin (−2.8%) all fell as yields jumped.

## Oil

**Oil reversed a five-day slide.** **Brent settled at $103.08 (+$3.83, +3.86%)** and **WTI at $92.16 (+$1.64, +1.81%)** (Reuters via GV Wire). Drivers: **Iranian President Pezeshkian said Tehran would "never surrender" to the US** after Trump threatened to "annihilate" Iran, while talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz continue. Reports of a possible **90-day US diesel export ban** knocked ULSD futures down about 5% before the White House denied it. Supply eased on the other side: **Saudi Arabia restarted its East-West pipeline** to the Red Sea (shut since Sept 11), and Iraq is exporting more than 3 million bpd. **US crude inventories unexpectedly rose 3.0M barrels to 426.4M.**

## Gold

**Gold fell to about $4,304/oz (−$53.75, −1.23%)** and **silver fell 3.09% to $65.06** (USAGOLD). A firmer dollar (DXY above 100) and hawkish Fed comments (Musalem, Goolsbee, Barr) raised rate-hike expectations. Yahoo's late read put gold near $4,321.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin fell to about $84,230 (−2.84%)** by the US close (Yahoo Finance), from ~$85,700 at 10am ET (Fortune). Higher expected rates hit crypto along with other risk assets. It is still up about 10% on the week after the Sept 21 short squeeze (see `../260921/README.md`).
