# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 23, 2026 (Wednesday) — MARKET CLOSED (HOLIDAY, DAY 3 OF 3)

**The Tokyo Stock Exchange was closed on Wednesday, Sept 23, for Autumnal Equinox Day (秋分の日), the last day of the three-day Silver Week closure (Sept 21 敬老の日, Sept 22 国民の休日, Sept 23 秋分の日). Cash trading resumed Thursday, Sept 24** (see `../260924/japan.md`). There is no Nikkei 225 or TOPIX cash close for Sept 23. The last confirmed cash close is still **Friday Sept 18: Nikkei 65,018.95 (+1.38%), TOPIX 4,091.14 (−0.07%)**.

## Holiday-session derivatives (Osaka Exchange) — day 3

- **Nikkei 225 futures (December contract) settled the Sept 23 holiday session at 66,820**: **+1,720 yen** over the three holiday days (vs the Sept 18 settlement) and **+420 yen** vs Tuesday's holiday settlement (implying ≈66,400 for Sept 22). The intraday range was 600 yen, narrower than 1,050 yen on Monday and continuing to shrink. (tbladvisory.com)
- **The futures ended 1,801 yen above the Nikkei's last cash close.** That gap can only close when cash trading reopens on Thursday.
- **Driver:** the US market. The Nasdaq Composite set back-to-back record closes on Sept 21–22 and the SOX rose for a sixth straight session, which kept buyers in holiday futures. Holiday-session volume is thin, so this is an indicative level, not a market-clearing price.

## Yen and rates

- **The yen weakened sharply into the 158 range** (円急落158円台), per Nikkei — back to the level before the Ministry of Finance's earlier "rate check". It was pushed there by the **US 10-year yield rising to about 5.1%** after strong US PMIs (see `../260923/usa.md`). Tokyo FX commentary on Sept 24 (gaitame.com, ZAi) described USD/JPY as pulled between high US yields and the risk of intervention.
- The BOJ's 25bp hike to **1.25%** (decided Sept 18, 7–2 vote) takes effect **Sept 24**, the day the market reopens.

## What this means for Thursday's reopening

| Factor | Why it matters | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Futures 1,801 yen above the last cash close | Cash stocks have to absorb three days of global news at once | Holiday volume is thin, so the gap may overstate real demand. **On Sept 24 the Nikkei opened sharply higher, touched 66,249, then closed only +495 yen** — only about a quarter of the gap was held (see `../260924/japan.md`) |
| US yields at a 2007 high | Higher global rates weigh on valuations | Wednesday's US selloff (Nasdaq −1.13%) happened *after* most of this holiday futures session |
| Yen at 158 | A weak yen helps exporters, but intervention risk is back | A sudden yen spike could hit exporters on reopening day |
| Trump–Xi summit (Sept 24) | Export-control and AI decisions matter for chip-equipment makers | Headline risk on the reopening day itself |

## Bottom line

Japan was closed for the third straight day. Osaka holiday futures settled at **66,820**, up 1,720 yen over the three-day break and 1,801 yen above the last cash close, riding the US AI/semiconductor rally. But Wednesday's US session reversed after the futures had closed: yields hit a 2007 high and the Nasdaq fell 1.13%. So Thursday's reopening looked less strong than the futures implied, and that is what happened (see `../260924/japan.md`).
