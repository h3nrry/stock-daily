# Taiwan Stock Market Analysis — August 24, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

TAIEX (加權指數) closed at **44,762.32**, down **461.97 points (−1.02%)**, breaking below the psychologically important 45,000 level on the **lowest trading volume in over four months** (NT$629.43B, ~$19.8B). This was a classic "index down, market not really down" session: the headline loss was concentrated almost entirely in a handful of heavyweight tech and petrochemical names, while memory, silicon-wafer, and shipping stocks rallied hard underneath. Thin volume on the way down is itself a signal — it points to profit-taking and index-weight mechanics rather than broad-based panic selling.

## What pushed the index down

- **TSMC / 台積電 (2330)** fell 1.45% to NT$2,375, hitting a session low and acting as the single biggest drag on the index given its ~30%+ index weight.
- **MediaTek / 聯發科 (2454)** slipped 0.66% to NT$3,765.
- **Petrochemicals (塑化股) were the worst sector**, down 5.45% as a group, with **Formosa Petrochemical / 台塑石化 (6505)** plunging 7.78–8%+ — the sharpest single-stock move of the day, likely tied to margin/pricing concerns rather than anything Taiwan-specific.
- **Financials** dropped 1.34% and **semiconductors** as a group slipped 1.17%, consistent with broad profit-taking after a strong run and investors "cutting losses on shares bought at higher prices," per local commentary.
- The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index's 0.51% decline on Friday added external pressure to Taiwan's chip complex overnight.

## What rallied — the real story beneath the index

Four pockets of strength stood out, and they're worth watching going into the rest of the week:

**Silicon wafers (矽晶圓)** — the standout sector. Reports that wafer prices across 6", 8", and 12" specifications are rising **more than 10%**, the first such increase in three years, sent the whole group higher: GlobalWafers / 環球晶圓 (6488) +6.8% to NT$1,005 (back above the NT$1,000 mark), Taiwan Sil-Tech / 台勝科 (3532) hit **limit-up (漲停)** at NT$376.50, Formosa Crystals / 中美晶 (5483) +2.84%, and Heraeus Shin-Etsu / 合晶 (6182) +6%+. This isn't a short-term shortage story — it's existing long-term contracts expiring into a market where 12" capacity is running near-full utilization and 6" is at full capacity, so suppliers are pushing for longer commitments or upfront payment before adding capacity. That dynamic tends to persist for multiple quarters once it starts.

**Memory / DRAM (記憶體)** — the second engine. Reports of DRAM prices rising as much as 70% triggered broad buying: Nanya Technology / 南亞科 hit a new high, and Phison / 群聯 (8299), Adata / 威剛 (3260), GUC / 廣穎 (8271), ESMT / 鈺創 (5351), and Innodisk / 宜鼎 (5289) all rose around 4%+. PSMC / 力積電 (6770) ranked third in market trading volume, and Winbond / 華邦電 (2344) stayed in the top-10 by volume. AI server demand is the underlying driver.

**Semiconductors ex-TSMC** — **UMC / 聯電 (2303)** surged 6–8% to NT$123.5 on heavy foreign buying, the second-highest volume stock of the day — notable given foreign investors were net sellers of the market overall (see below), meaning UMC was a specific accumulation target, not just beta.

**Shipping/dry bulk (航運)** — Yang Ming / 陽明海運 (2609), Wan Hai / 萬海航運 (2615), and Evergreen / 長榮海運 (2603) all hit new highs as container and dry-bulk freight indices strengthened.

**Other notables:** King Slide / 川湖 (2059) hit limit-up (漲停) at NT$14,720, closing in on Silicon King / 信驊 (5274, NT$15,500) for the title of Taiwan's highest-priced stock. Lite-On / 光寶科 hit limit-up in computer peripherals (電腦周邊, sector +1.44%). Lite-On subsidiary Liteon Technology / 光寶科技 (2301) hit an all-time high of NT$287 on AI server power-supply demand. Biotech / 生技 (+2.08%) also outperformed on licensing-deal news.

## Money flow (資金流向) — is money leaving Taiwan or coming in?

**Today, on net, money left the market — but the composition matters more than the headline.**

| Investor group | Net flow (Aug 24) |
|---|---|
| Foreign investors (外資) | **−NT$15.75B (賣超)** (net sell) |
| Investment trusts (投信) | +NT$3.71B (買超) (net buy) |
| Dealers (自營商) | −NT$4.33B (賣超) (net sell) |
| **Three institutions combined (三大法人)** | **−NT$16.38B (net sell)** |

So today was a foreign-investor-led sell-off, partially offset by domestic investment trusts buying the dip — a fairly typical pattern on down days and not, by itself, a red flag.

**The bigger trend is more important than one day**, though, and it's genuinely two-sided:

- Foreign investors have net-sold a cumulative **NT$2.28 trillion (~US$70.7B)** in Taiwan stocks year-to-date 2026 — a large, persistent outflow.
- Despite that, foreigners still hold roughly **48.7% of total Taiwan market cap**, meaning the selling has been a gradual reduction of an overweight position, not a stampede for the exits.
- Institutional strategists (e.g., Allianz Global Investors Taiwan) argue the underlying bull case is still intact: they cite the AI supply chain's fundamentals as "robust" and project **~65.4% YoY earnings growth for 2026**, which they see as continuing to provide downside support for the index even as valuation multiples get stretched.
- The catch: TAIEX now trades around **29.75x forward earnings**, about **two standard deviations above its 10-year average** — expensive by Taiwan's own historical standards. That's why the institutional advice has shifted from "buy the index" to "be selective": favor names with high earnings visibility and upward estimate revisions, concentrated in AI servers, advanced semiconductors, ASICs, and high-speed transmission components.

**Read on money flow:** this is not a "money is fleeing Taiwan" story — it's a "foreign investors are trimming a large, profitable, years-long overweight position while still holding half the market" story, layered under a market that domestic institutions keep buying on dips. The near-term risk is technical (crowded positioning, low volume, elevated valuation) rather than fundamental.

## Taiwan ETFs (台股ETF) — a different story than the headline index

Even as TAIEX itself closed down 1.02%, several of the island's largest and most widely held ETFs moved *against* the broader market today:

- **0050 (元大台灣50 / Yuanta Taiwan Top 50 ETF)** — Taiwan's original and largest broad-market ETF, tracking the 50 largest TWSE-listed companies (heavily weighted toward TSMC). Reported to have risen against the market trend (逆勢上揚) today.
- **00919 (群益台灣精選高息 / Capital Taiwan Select High Dividend ETF)** — a high-dividend-focused ETF from Capital Investment Trust (群益投信), one of the most popular income-focused ETFs among Taiwan's retail "buy-and-hold dividend" (存股) investor base — also reported higher against the falling market today.
- **00878 (國泰永續高股息 / Cathay Sustainable High Dividend ETF)** — Cathay Investment Trust's sustainability-screened high-dividend ETF, another of the "big three" retail dividend ETFs alongside 0050 and 00919, widely tracked in the same daily rankings.

**What's notable:** local reporting attributed today's 0050/00919 strength in part to **"八大公股" (the eight major state-owned/government-linked financial institutions)** stepping in as buyers — a pattern that recurs during down days on the TAIEX, where government-linked funds have historically provided a stabilizing bid under large, liquid, broad-market ETFs during broader-market selloffs. This lines up with the money-flow read above: foreign investors sold, but retail and state-linked buying interest in the flagship ETFs suggests confidence in the underlying large-cap/dividend names even as index heavyweights like TSMC and MediaTek dragged the headline number down. Worth tracking into month-end whether this state-fund support continues, as a signal of official confidence in current large-cap valuations. (For US-listed ETFs, see `usa.md`.)

## Bottom line

Down day, but a healthy one under the hood: the sell-off was concentrated in index heavyweights and petrochemicals on thin volume, while memory, silicon wafers, semiconductors ex-TSMC, and shipping all pushed higher. Foreign investors sold today and have been net sellers all year, but they're trimming from a still-large ~49% ownership stake, not exiting, and domestic institutions bought today's dip. With TAIEX valuations stretched (~29.75x forward earnings, 2 std. dev. above the 10-year average), the market looks set to stay in a stock-picker's environment — earnings-driven names in AI servers, advanced semis, and the wafer/memory upcycle look better-positioned than a passive index bet into Nvidia's earnings and the Jackson Hole Fed symposium later this week.
