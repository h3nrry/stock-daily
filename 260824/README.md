# Daily Market Briefing — August 24, 2026 (Monday)

Global snapshot: US stocks are drifting lower in Monday trading — blue chips holding up, tech under pressure — as markets digest new Iran sanctions and a collapsed US-Canada trade negotiation, with Nvidia earnings (Wed) and the Fed's Jackson Hole symposium (Fri) as the week's real catalysts. The dollar has slid to a three-month low as Treasury buybacks and a split Fed weigh on it, which is a big part of why gold and bitcoin are both extending strong rallies and why the yuan is at its strongest since January 2023. Asia had a rougher session than the US: Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Southeast Asia all fell on the same rising-yields, pre-Nvidia caution, while Korea's KOSPI plunged over 3% on a Samsung-specific shareholder-return disappointment rather than a broad selloff. Europe was the calm spot, holding near this month's record highs. Oil is pulling back slightly even as the Iran conflict keeps a geopolitical premium in the price. See `usa.md` (now including US Treasury/corporate bonds and US ETFs), `taiwan.md` (now including Taiwan ETFs), `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, and `uk_europe.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

Southeast Asia and Europe were the calmest zones, with moves mostly inside ±0.4%. Japan sits in between — a genuine, if modest, region-wide pullback tied to yields and pre-Nvidia caution rather than a single-stock story.

## Where the money is flowing

Pulling together the money-flow data across every market covered in this folder, the clearest pattern is **rotation within markets, not a broad flight between them**:

- **United States:** $6.38B flowed into S&P 500 ETFs (SPY+VOO) while Nasdaq-100 funds (QQQ+QQQM combined) were roughly flat — money moving from the most AI-concentrated exposure into diversified large-caps, not leaving US equities. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** Foreign investors net-sold NT$15.75B today and have sold a cumulative NT$2.28 trillion (~US$70.7B) year-to-date — but still hold ~48.7% of the market. Domestic investment trusts and state-linked funds (八大公股) bought the dip, including in flagship ETFs (0050, 00919). Read as trimming an overweight position, not an exit. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** Foreign and institutional investors net-sold a combined ~4.97 trillion won, almost entirely concentrated in the Samsung shock; individual investors net-bought 3.32 trillion won into the dip. Market breadth stayed positive. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** No broad outflow signal — the pullback was Alibaba-specific (dilution from a share placement), and financials (Ping An, AIA) actually gained. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Japan:** Foreign investors net-sold ¥198.8 billion last week, ending a two-week buying streak, but local coverage explicitly framed this as a pause in an intact bullish stance, not a reversal. See `japan.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Mixed — Malaysia saw a continued foreign outflow (−RM549 million, part of a multi-day streak, the clearest sustained outflow signal in this region), while Singapore, Indonesia, and Thailand showed no comparable broad exit signal today. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** No daily outflow signal available, and the region is holding near this month's record highs — the qualitative read is capital staying invested. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** The one truly directional, one-way flow today is out of the US dollar — DXY near a three-month low — and into gold, bitcoin, and the offshore yuan, all driven by the same story: rising Treasury issuance, a doubled Fed buyback program, and a split, uncertain Fed ahead of Friday's Jackson Hole speech. That's less "region A to region B" and more "away from dollar-denominated safety, into hard assets and higher-yielding/appreciating currencies" — a hedge against US fiscal and monetary uncertainty rather than a regional equity rotation.

**Net read:** almost nowhere today does the data show investors broadly exiting a market — Taiwan, Korea, and Malaysia all show real foreign selling, but each is either concentrated in one name/sector, offset by domestic buying, or explicitly framed by the market itself as a pause rather than a reversal. The one unambiguous directional flow is the dollar-weakness trade benefiting gold, bitcoin, and the yuan — worth watching whether Fed Chair Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole speech extends or reverses it, since that single event is the common thread hanging over every market in this folder.

The dollar is the common thread behind today's moves everywhere else. DXY has given back almost its entire late-July rally after the Treasury said it would at least double its long-bond buyback program, and after the Fed's unusually split 9-3 vote to hold rates (three dissents wanted a hike) left traders uncertain what Fed Chair Kevin Warsh will signal at his first Jackson Hole keynote this Friday. The **yen** has strengthened as odds build (~82%) for a Bank of Japan rate hike in September. The **yuan** (offshore) is at its strongest level since January 2023, benefiting purely from dollar weakness — the PBOC held its 1-year loan prime rate steady at 3%.

## Gold

Gold futures traded around **$4,712.60/oz** this morning, essentially flat to slightly softer (−0.2%) versus Friday's close, but the bigger picture is a powerful rally: +6.3% over the past week, +14.9% over the past month, and +39.5% year-over-year. Three forces are driving it: the Treasury's bond-buyback expansion (up to $4B/session) fueling demand for hard assets, the ongoing Iran war keeping safe-haven demand elevated, and persistent global inflation. Analysts expect gold to "stay hot through the end of the month."
