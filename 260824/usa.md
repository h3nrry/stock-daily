# USA Stock Market Analysis — August 24, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

US markets are drifting in a classic "rotation, not rout" session. As of midday: **Dow +0.27% to 53,419.17**, **S&P 500 −0.23% to 7,656.50**, **Nasdaq −0.44% to 26,066.56**. Blue chips are holding up while tech gives back ground — money is rotating out of richly-valued AI/semiconductor names and into communication services and financials ahead of a genuinely high-stakes week: Nvidia earnings Wednesday and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote Friday.

## What pushed the market down

- **Nvidia** fell ~1.9% ahead of Wednesday's earnings — the single most important print of the week for the AI trade, and traders are trimming into it rather than adding.
- **Semiconductors broadly** weighed on the Nasdaq; the article flow (and the read-through into UMC's outsized foreign buying in Taiwan) suggests this is a rotation *within* semis — money moving toward specific names with a fresh catalyst (legacy nodes, memory) and away from the most crowded AI-capex names — rather than a broad chip sell-off.
- **New Iran sanctions**: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled fresh economic measures against Iran, describing them as "an economic D-Day — the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary." That kind of rhetoric raises geopolitical-risk pricing even as headline oil eased.
- **US-Canada trade talks collapsed** over the weekend. The US imposed new tariffs on Canadian goods; Canadian PM Mark Carney suspended talks and pledged retaliatory tariffs of equal size. This is a fresh, unpriced risk that wasn't on the table last week.
- **VIX up over 5%** — a real, if modest, pickup in hedging demand, consistent with a market bracing for Wednesday and Friday rather than panicking today.

## What's holding up / leading

- **Communication services and financials** are the day's leaders, the classic destination for money rotating out of high-multiple tech.
- **Dow outperforming Nasdaq** by roughly 70 basis points intraday is itself the clearest read: this is a valuation/duration rotation, not broad risk-off. If it were broad risk-off, financials (rate-sensitive, cyclical) wouldn't be leading.
- Bitcoin (+0.66%) and gold (+0.27%) both firm at the same time equities wobble — consistent with the dollar-weakness/rate-relief trade dominating cross-asset positioning more than equity-specific fear.

## Money flow and positioning

There's no single clean daily "foreign vs. domestic" flow print for US equities the way Taiwan or Japan publish, but the available signals point the same direction: this is a **rotation, not an exit**. The VIX move is modest (+5% on the index, not a spike into the 20s+), Dow money isn't leaving the market, it's moving inside it, and cross-asset behavior (gold + bitcoin + Dow all firm, Nasdaq soft) reads as a duration/valuation trade — investors trimming the most expensive, highest-beta AI names into two major catalysts, while staying invested through cheaper, more defensive sectors. The bigger structural question is what the **Treasury's expanded long-bond buyback** and the Fed's unusually split 9-3 vote (three dissents wanting an immediate hike) mean for liquidity into year-end — that's arguably a bigger driver of the "risk stays on, just rotates" behavior than anything company-specific today.

## US Treasury yields

| Maturity | Yield | Change (day) |
|---|---|---|
| 2-Year | 4.24% | +0.004 pts |
| 10-Year | 4.71% | −0.026 pts |
| 30-Year | 5.24% | −0.038 pts |

The **10-year** slipped to 4.71% today, retreating after two straight sessions of gains as investors positioned ahead of Fed communications this week. Zoom out, though, and the trend is still upward: the 10-year is up 0.06 points over the past month and a full 0.43 points higher than a year ago. The **30-year** at 5.24% remains close to levels not seen in nearly two decades.

**Why yields have been rising since July:** increased Treasury debt issuance and higher federal deficit spending have been pushing longer-dated yields up. The Treasury's response — announcing it would **double its buyback program for longer-dated securities** (from roughly $2B to $4B per session) — has provided only temporary relief; it pulled 30-year yields off their recent highs but hasn't reversed the underlying trend. This is the direct read-through into gold's rally (bond-market intervention boosting demand for hard assets) and the dollar's weakness (foreign investors reassessing US fiscal sustainability) covered in `README.md`.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote (Friday) is the week's central catalyst for the yield curve. The FOMC's unusually split 9-3 vote to hold rates (three dissents wanting an immediate hike) means the market has real uncertainty about the Fed's reaction function heading into this speech — a hawkish tilt would likely push yields back up and reverse this month's dollar-weakness trade; a dovish tilt would likely extend it.

## US corporate bonds — investment grade and high yield

| Segment | OAS spread | As of | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investment Grade (IG) | **82 bps** | Aug 20, 2026 | Tight — "risk-on, strong credit confidence" |
| High Yield (HY) | **275 bps** | Aug 20, 2026 | Tight — "risk-on, limited compensation for default risk" |

Both investment-grade and high-yield credit spreads are sitting near the tight (low-risk-premium) end of their historical range — IG's 82bps compares to a 350bps reading in the March 2020 COVID shock and 230bps in the October 2022 rate-shock period; HY's 275bps is well below the 600bps "stress" threshold and far below the 800bps recession threshold. In plain terms: bond investors are not demanding much extra compensation to hold corporate credit over Treasuries right now, which reflects confidence in corporate balance sheets and low near-term default risk.

**The catch:** tight spreads cut both ways. There's minimal room for spreads to compress further, but real room for them to widen if economic conditions deteriorate — the asymmetry favors caution even though nothing in current data points to imminent stress. For IG, the bigger risk to bond *prices* right now is duration (interest-rate sensitivity from the yield moves above), not credit quality. For HY, worth watching the energy sector (~14% of the HY index) given ongoing Iran-conflict oil volatility, the BB/CCC quality mix, and September's new-issuance calendar.

**ETF proxies:** iShares iBoxx $ Investment Grade Corporate Bond ETF (LQD) for IG exposure, and comparable high-yield ETFs (e.g., HYG) for the HY segment — both move inversely with the spread/yield dynamics above.

But QQQM tracks the same Nasdaq-100 index as QQQ at a meaningfully lower expense ratio, and it pulled in $383M the same day — meaning a chunk of the "QQQ outflow" may just be investors switching wrappers for the cheaper fund rather than genuinely reducing Nasdaq-100 exposure. The cleaner signal is the unambiguous **$6.38B rush into S&P 500 exposure**, lining up with the rotation into more diversified, less AI-concentrated large-cap exposure ahead of Nvidia's earnings. ## What to watch this week

1. **Nvidia earnings, Wednesday** — the real test of whether the AI capex/monetization narrative still supports current multiples. A weak print or cautious guidance likely extends today's rotation into a deeper correction in AI-adjacent names (chips, power/cooling infrastructure, hyperscaler capex plays); a strong beat-and-raise could snap the rotation back the other way fast.
2. **Jackson Hole, Friday** — Fed Chair Warsh's first major keynote in the role. Given the unusually split FOMC vote, markets will parse this hard for a signal on the path of rates into 2027. A dovish tilt is a green light for the current gold/bitcoin/risk-asset rally to continue; a hawkish tilt could hit the dollar-weakness trade that's been supporting nearly everything else this month.
3. **July PCE inflation and Q2 GDP data**, also due this week — inputs directly into the Warsh speech's credibility.
4. **US-Canada tariff escalation** — a genuinely new geopolitical/trade risk that wasn't priced a week ago; watch for retaliation specifics and sector exposure (autos, energy, agriculture typically most exposed to Canada-US trade friction).

## Bottom line

Today's weakness is a rotation out of the most crowded, highest-multiple AI/semiconductor names and into cheaper, more defensive sectors — not a broad risk-off move. The dollar's slide to a three-month low, plus firm gold and bitcoin, both point to a market still comfortable holding risk, just repositioning ahead of two genuinely pivotal events: Nvidia's earnings Wednesday and Fed Chair Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech Friday. Both could easily reverse today's sector leadership in either direction — this is a week to expect volatility, not to read too much into any single day's sector rotation.
