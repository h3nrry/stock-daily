# UK & Europe Stock Markets Analysis — August 24, 2026 (Monday)

## Overview

European markets are little changed to modestly higher today, standing in contrast to the sharper pullbacks in Korea and Hong Kong and the cautious rotation seen in the US. The region enters the week coming off a genuinely strong run — the STOXX 600 and several national indices (FTSE 100, CAC 40, DAX, FTSE MIB, Euro Stoxx 50) hit record or near-record highs earlier this month. Today's session looks like consolidation near those highs rather than a reversal: gold's safe-haven bid is offsetting oil-linked weakness in the UK, Germany's DAX is extending Friday's bounce, and France's CAC 40 is essentially flat as investors stay cautious ahead of geopolitical announcements and the Nvidia/Jackson Hole catalysts hitting every other market this week.

## United Kingdom (FTSE 100)

**FTSE 100** stood at **10,842**, up **25.04 points (+0.23%)**. The move was a tug-of-war between two commodity-linked forces: **gold miners rallied** on precious metals' safe-haven bid (the same gold strength lifting bullion prices globally today), providing a cushion for the index, while **oil majors and AstraZeneca slid** — oil majors on the pullback in crude prices, AstraZeneca on pharmaceutical-sector-specific weakness. Net result: a modest gain, with gold's strength outweighing energy and healthcare softness.

**What to watch:** the FTSE's gold-miner sensitivity makes it one of the more direct equity plays on this month's gold rally (see `README.md` for the gold section) — worth watching whether that leadership persists if gold's rally cools once Jackson Hole passes. Oil majors (exposed to ongoing Iran-conflict-related volatility) are the other swing factor: today's dip tracks the broader ~2% pullback in WTI/Brent, but the underlying geopolitical premium in oil remains intact.

## Germany (DAX)

**DAX** traded around **26,175**, up roughly **0.15%** on the day, extending Friday's 0.59% bounce that snapped a four-day losing streak. Analysts (Helaba) describe the market as still "realigning portfolios" after the recent bond-market volatility, with expectations that improving global industrial cycles will continue to support German equities — a relevant signal given the DAX's heavy weighting toward industrials and export-oriented manufacturers that are sensitive to both global growth and the euro's exchange rate.

## France (CAC 40)

**CAC 40** was little changed, trading around **8,467–8,486**, reflecting cautious positioning amid geopolitical tensions (the same Iran-sanctions and US-Canada tariff headlines weighing on sentiment globally) and anticipated policy announcements later this week (Jackson Hole).

## Broader Europe (STOXX 600)

The pan-European **STOXX 600** closed Friday at **654.18** (+0.59%), with the **Euro STOXX 50** at 6,462.22 (+0.63%) — both supported that session by heavyweight banks and luxury names. Today's national-index moves (FTSE flat-to-up, DAX modestly higher, CAC roughly flat) suggest the STOXX 600 is holding near those levels rather than giving back much ground, consistent with a market in consolidation after a strong 2026 that has seen repeated record highs across the region's major benchmarks.

## Reading the money flow

There's no single clean daily flow print across European equities the way Taiwan or Korea publish, but the qualitative picture is consistent: **capital broadly stayed invested in Europe today rather than exiting**. Gold miners (a direct beneficiary of safe-haven flows) led the UK; German and French indices held steady rather than selling off with Asia; and the region's 2026 story — record highs across FTSE 100, CAC 40, DAX, FTSE MIB, and Euro Stoxx 50 earlier this month — reflects sustained institutional interest rather than a rotation out of the region. The main risk to watch is the same one hanging over every market in this folder: a hawkish surprise from Fed Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole Friday could pressure global bond yields further, which would be a headwind for European equities just as it has been for US and Asian markets.

## Bottom line

Europe is the region's calm spot today — the FTSE, DAX, and CAC are all essentially flat to modestly higher, holding near the record highs set earlier this month, while Korea and Hong Kong sold off harder and the US/Japan/Taiwan saw more clear-cut rotation. Gold's safe-haven bid is doing real work for the UK specifically via its miners. With no major region-specific shock today, Europe's path this week likely tracks the same global catalysts as everywhere else — Nvidia's Wednesday earnings and Fed Chair Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole speech — rather than anything Europe-specific.
