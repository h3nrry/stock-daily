# Southeast Asia Stock Markets Analysis — August 24, 2026 (Monday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia's major markets mostly drifted lower today, but far more gently than Northeast Asia — no market in this region saw anything close to Korea's Samsung-driven 3.12% plunge or even Hong Kong's 1.3% drop. The common threads across the four markets below: elevated US Treasury yields dampening global risk appetite, Middle East/Iran-related oil-price volatility, and regional semiconductor weakness bleeding into Thailand specifically. Singapore was the most resilient by far, essentially flat and still up over 33% year-over-year — one of the best-performing markets globally in 2026.

## Singapore (Straits Times Index / STI)

**STI** stood at **5,680.46**, down just **8.50 points (−0.15%)** — a mild pullback that barely dented what's been an exceptional year: **+1.07% over the past month and +33.45% year-over-year**. Singapore's relative calm today, even as Korea and Hong Kong sold off harder, reflects the index's composition: it's dominated by the big three domestic banks — **DBS Group / 星展銀行 (D05.SI)**, **OCBC / 華僑銀行 (O39.SI)**, and **UOB / 大華銀行 (U11.SI)** — which have been the primary engine of the 2026 rally on the back of record profits and rising dividend payouts, and which carry far less direct AI/semiconductor-capex exposure than the tech-heavy indices elsewhere in the region.

**Stocks to watch:** DBS, OCBC, and UOB remain the core of the Singapore market's strength — all three have been setting records through 2026 on strong earnings and shareholder returns, and today's mild pullback looks more like broad regional caution than any bank-specific concern. Their resilience today, while Samsung-driven Korea and Alibaba-driven Hong Kong both sold off harder, is itself a signal of Singapore's defensive characteristics within the region.

## Indonesia (IHSG / JCI)

The **IHSG (Indeks Harga Saham Gabungan)** closed at **6,501.67**, down **0.37%**. The decline was broad — 10 of 11 sectors fell — but shallow: **property (−1.67%)** led the losers, followed by healthcare (−1.59%) and transportation/logistics (−0.91%), while **basic materials was the lone gainer, +1.38%**. Market breadth: 367 decliners versus 259 advancers, on trading value of roughly Rp14.29 trillion.

Small-cap speculation was active regardless of the broader dip — **PICO (+34.31%)**, **ELIT (+34.03%)**, and **SAFE (+24.87%)** were the day's biggest movers, though moves of that size in small, thinly-traded names should be treated as speculative rather than a market signal.

**Stocks to watch:** the basic materials sector's outperformance today (+1.38% against a falling market) lines up with the mining/commodity-linked names local commentary flagged for the session — **Bank Rakyat Indonesia / BBRI (BBRI.JK)**, **Bumi Resources / BUMI (BUMI.JK)**, and **Bumi Resources Minerals / BRMS (BRMS.JK)** — worth a closer look given they're swimming against a red tape on the broader index, though as always with commodity names, they carry direct exposure to volatile global commodity prices.

## Malaysia (FBM KLCI)

The **FBM KLCI** closed essentially flat at **1,736.33**, down just **0.15 points (−0.01%)**, though the headline number masks more negative breadth underneath: **714 decliners versus 482 gainers**. Trading was thin ahead of the Maulidur Rasul holiday, with a market dealer describing conditions as "relatively subdued" as investors stayed on the sidelines. **Foreign investors extended their selling streak, withdrawing RM549 million** from the market — a continuation of an outflow trend worth watching alongside earnings season (Malaysia's corporate results season is entering its final week). The ringgit weakened slightly, down 0.09% against the US dollar to 4.0420.

**Gainers:** Petronas Dagangan (+RM0.38 to RM20.00), MISC (+RM0.35 to RM8.58), Kuala Lumpur Kepong / KLK (+RM0.28 to RM21.92) — all ahead of, or on, quarterly results.
**Decliners:** Malaysian Pacific Industries (−RM1.70 to RM41.50), Allianz Malaysia (−RM0.58 to RM21.70), KPJ Healthcare (−RM0.32 to RM2.80).

**Stocks to watch:** Petronas Dagangan, MISC, and KLK all rose into their earnings releases — worth watching whether the results validate the pre-earnings buying. The persistent foreign outflow (RM549 million today, part of a broader streak) is the more important underlying signal here and worth tracking over the coming weeks rather than reading too much into any single day's flat index print.

## Thailand (SET Index)

The **SET Index** closed at **1,601.05**, down **12.73 points (−0.79%)**, on trading value of THB 60.9 billion. Thailand was the region's clearest read-through from the global semiconductor rotation: **DELTA Electronics Thailand / เดลต้า (DELTA.BK)** and **Hana Microelectronics / ฮานา (HANA.BK)** both faced selling pressure directly tied to the broader semiconductor weakness rippling through Asian markets ahead of Nvidia's Wednesday earnings — the same theme hitting chip names in the US, Japan, Korea, and Taiwan today. Elevated US bond yields and Middle East tensions were cited as additional dampeners, with local analysts noting a lack of positive catalysts to support the market. One analyst flagged a **potential rebound on August 25**.

**Stocks to watch:** DELTA and HANA are Thailand's two most direct semiconductor plays and both are effectively hostage to the same Nvidia-earnings catalyst driving chip-sector caution across the whole region this week — a strong Nvidia print Wednesday would likely be the single biggest catalyst for a bounce in both names.

## Bottom line

Southeast Asia held up notably better than Korea and Hong Kong today, with Singapore essentially flat and still one of 2026's best-performing global markets, while Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand all posted only mild declines. The regional throughline is the same one showing up everywhere in this folder today: caution ahead of Nvidia's Wednesday earnings and the Fed's Friday Jackson Hole symposium, layered on top of elevated US Treasury yields. Malaysia's persistent foreign outflow and Thailand's direct semiconductor-linked weakness (DELTA, HANA) are the two clearest country-specific threads worth following into the rest of the week; Singapore's bank-driven resilience and Indonesia's basic-materials strength are the two bright spots.
