# Japan Stock Market Analysis — August 24, 2026 (Monday)

## Headline

**Nikkei 225 / 日経平均株価** closed at **65,528.09**, down **488.27 points (−0.74%)**, a second straight losing session (東証大引け「日経平均は続落」). The move tracked a broader Asia-wide pullback tied to rising US and Japanese bond yields, alongside a sharp selloff in Korean equities. Underneath the index, this looks like a rotation story similar to the US and Taiwan: AI and semiconductor names took a breather ahead of Nvidia's Wednesday earnings, while investors rotated into "content stocks" (コンテンツ株) and other names less directly exposed to AI-capex valuation risk.

## What pushed the index down

- **Rising US and Japanese interest rates (日米金利上昇)** — the same Treasury-yield pressure hitting US and Taiwan markets weighed on Japanese equities, with rate-sensitive and growth names hit hardest.
- **Korean stock weakness (韓国株安)** spilled over into regional sentiment: South Korea's KOSPI fell sharply in the same session (reported between −1.2% and −3.5% depending on the source and timing), with **Samsung Electronics** down over 4% while **SK Hynix bucked the trend, up ~3.6%** — a semiconductor divergence worth noting since it mirrors the "not all chip names move together" pattern seen in Taiwan's memory-stock rally today.
- **AI/semiconductor names paused (AI小休止)** ahead of Nvidia's earnings this Wednesday — the single most important catalyst for the entire regional tech/AI trade this week.
- **SoftBank** (a major AI/tech bellwether, heavily exposed via its investment portfolio) slipped about 1%; **Kioxia** (memory/NAND) was roughly flat-to-down, in contrast to the memory-stock strength seen in Taiwan today — a reminder that "memory" isn't a single uniform trade across countries and depends on company-specific exposure (NAND vs. DRAM, contract vs. spot pricing).

## What's rotating in

Local commentary specifically flagged **content stocks (コンテンツ株)** as "狙い目" (the place to look) as investors rotate away from AI/semiconductor names that have run hard into an uncertain earnings catalyst. This is a domestic-flavored version of the same rotation seen in the US (into financials/communication services) and points to money staying in Japanese equities broadly rather than leaving the market — just repositioning within it.

## Money flow — foreign investors in Japan

The most recent published weekly data (Japan Exchange Group's investor-type statistics, week of August 17–21, i.e. the week just completed) shows **foreign investors (海外投資家) net-sold approximately ¥198.8 billion (1,988億円)** in Japanese equities — ending a two-week buying streak. Importantly, financial press coverage of that same data explicitly framed it as **"強気姿勢は崩さず"** — foreign investors' bullish stance remains intact despite the one-week net sell. That's a very similar pattern to what's showing up in Taiwan today (foreign selling, but read by strategists as trimming rather than an exit) and worth watching in next week's JPX release to see whether it's a one-off pause or the start of a longer reduction.

**Read on money flow:** net-sell for the week, but described by the market itself as a pause in an otherwise bullish foreign stance, and it's happening for the same macro reason hitting US and Taiwan markets today — a bond-yield-driven pause ahead of Nvidia's earnings and Jackson Hole — rather than anything Japan-specific.

## Currency context — the yen matters here more than almost anywhere else

**USD/JPY is trading around 158.95**, down from an August 18 high of 159.60, with the yen up 2.88% over the past month as expectations build (~82% odds, per market pricing) for a **Bank of Japan rate hike in September**. This is the single biggest swing factor for Japanese equities right now: a weaker yen has historically been a tailwind for Nikkei (boosting exporters' reported earnings), so a strengthening yen into a September BOJ hike is a genuine headwind for the export-heavy names that make up a large share of the index, even as it's currently a modest net positive for real purchasing power and imported-inflation dynamics domestically.

## Bottom line

Nikkei's −0.74% session today is part of a regional pattern — the US, Taiwan, and Japan all pulled back on the same rising-yields, pre-Nvidia-earnings caution, with money rotating within each market rather than leaving it. Foreign investors turned net sellers of Japanese equities last week after two weeks of buying, but market commentary reads that as a pause in an intact bullish stance, not a reversal. The bigger multi-week theme to watch is the yen: strengthening on rising BOJ rate-hike odds, which is a genuine headwind for Japan's export-heavy index constituents even as it reflects a healthier domestic rate and inflation picture. Like the US and Taiwan, this week's real catalysts — Nvidia's earnings Wednesday and the Jackson Hole Fed symposium Friday — will likely matter far more than today's single-session move.
