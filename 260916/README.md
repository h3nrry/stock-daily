# Daily Market Briefing — September 16, 2026 (Wednesday)

**The Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75%-4.00%, but a hawkish dot plot (16 of 18 officials now see at least one more 2026 hike) sent US stocks lower and reversed a pre-decision gold/silver rally, even as Asia and Europe had already closed higher earlier the same day.** Asia/Pacific markets rebounded Wednesday after a rough Monday-Tuesday, led by a genuine cross-regional semiconductor story — reports of a potential **SK Hynix–Intel US chip-manufacturing partnership** lifted chip names from Seoul to Taipei to Tokyo to Hong Kong. **Korea's KOSPI surged 1.37% to 6,717.97** (SK Hynix +4.08%, Samsung Electronics +2.01%), snapping a four-day losing streak, though on negative market breadth and foreign/retail selling. **Taiwan's TAIEX rose 0.74% to 45,848.9**, also snapping a four-day skid, even as foreign investors extended their own selling streak to a fifth day. **Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 0.69% to 63,923.00**, its first rebound in four sessions, with genuine breadth (TOPIX +0.61%). **Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.19% to 24,713.78**, masking a divergence between rallying semiconductors and declining megacap internet stocks. **Southeast Asia was the outlier**: **Malaysia's Bursa Malaysia was closed for Malaysia Day**, **Thailand's SET fell 1.01%** on domestic energy-policy news, and **Indonesia (−0.38%) and Singapore (≈−0.45% recomputed)** posted modest, macro-driven declines. **Europe closed broadly higher** — **STOXX 600 +0.46% to 637.10**, **DAX +0.53% to 25,537.75**, **CAC 40 +0.62% to 8,140.59**, **FTSE 100 +0.28% to 10,688.47** — a genuine recovery from Tuesday's three-month low, but Europe's close came hours *before* the Fed's decision, so the region has not yet had a chance to react to the hawkish dot-plot surprise. **The US, by contrast, was open through the announcement**: the **Dow fell 1.21% to 51,462.55**, the **S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81**, and the **Nasdaq Composite closed essentially flat, −0.01% at 25,978.43** — the Dow's underperformance reflecting a rotation out of rate-sensitive sectors (financials, homebuilders, energy) rather than a broad flight from risk. **Oil reversed sharply lower** for a second straight session — Brent closed at $105.83/barrel (−2.69%) and WTI at $102.43/barrel (−3.21%) — after the US said Saudi Arabia's damaged pipeline would resume operations within days. **Gold and silver spiked pre-decision, then reversed on the hawkish dot plot**: spot gold settled at $4,310.10/oz (+0.38%, off a much larger pre-decision spike), while silver held on to more of its gain at $64.92/oz (+1.66%). **Bitcoin held the $75,000-$76,000 range**, roughly flat, despite ~$746 million in spot ETF outflows over September 15-16.

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** the TAIEX's +0.74% rebound snapped a four-day losing streak, but foreign investors extended their own selling streak to a fifth day (≈−NT$213.8 billion total institutional net selling despite the rally) — a genuine divergence suggesting the rebound was short-covering and domestic (investment-trust) buying rather than a return of foreign conviction. TSMC's flat, ex-dividend-day close and selective foreign buying in AU Optronics were the session's more constructive signals. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** a concentrated, institutionally-driven rally (institutions +₩1.21T net buy) powered almost entirely by SK Hynix (+4.08%) and Samsung Electronics (+2.01%) on SK Hynix–Intel US chip-manufacturing partnership talk — even as foreign investors (−₩1.68T) and individuals (−₩1.19T) were both net sellers and market breadth stayed negative (578 decliners vs. 304 advancers). See `korea.md`.
- **Japan:** a genuinely broad rebound (Nikkei +0.69%, TOPIX +0.61% — closely matched, unlike Tuesday's single-stock-driven divergence) after a weak morning gave way to a second-half reversal into a high-of-day close. See `japan.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a sector-rotation session — semiconductor/optical-communication names surged (Cambridge Technology +10%+, Shanghai Fudan +8%+) on the same cross-regional chip-partnership story, even as megacap internet names (Alibaba, Tencent, Xiaomi, Bilibili) all declined. Southbound Stock Connect logged its eighth consecutive day of net buying (+HK$2.099B). See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** the day's headline finding was a market that didn't trade at all — Bursa Malaysia was closed for Malaysia Day. Thailand's decline was almost entirely domestic-driven (an energy-price policy change, a tourism-stimulus delay), diverging sharply from the North Asian chip rally the same day. Indonesia and Singapore posted more modest, macro-driven (Treasury-yield-linked) declines. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **UK/Europe:** a genuine, broad-based recovery from Tuesday's three-month low — every major index (FTSE, DAX, CAC, STOXX 600) closed higher on easing oil prices and pre-Fed positioning. The gains were sector-rotational: Germany split between an AI/semiconductor tailwind (BofA's raised 2030 chip forecast) and auto-sector weakness (Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz both −2.6% on Berenberg's overcapacity/China-competition warning), while the UK's advance concentrated in miners, banks, and housebuilders (Barratt Redrow). Crucially, Europe's close preceded the Fed's actual decision by several hours, so Wednesday's gains reflect a "hike now priced in" positioning bet rather than a reaction to the dot plot's hawkish surprise — Thursday's European session is the one to watch for how that surprise gets priced in. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **United States:** a "sell the (hawkish) news" session layered on a rotation away from rate-sensitive sectors. The Fed's 25bp hike was fully priced in, but the dot plot's hawkish surprise (16 of 18 officials seeing at least one more 2026 hike, up from just 6 in June) drove the actual selling — energy (XLE −2.8%) and financials (XLF −1.5%) led sector declines, homebuilders tumbled as mortgage rates topped 7%, and the Dow's 1.21% drop outpaced the Nasdaq's essentially flat close. The Intel/SK Hynix chip-partnership trade remained the market's most durable bullish thread (Intel +4.34%), even as the broader semiconductor complex diverged sharply (ON Semiconductor −8.5%, −$2B market value). Gold and silver's pre-decision rally reversed on the hawkish surprise, and bitcoin held $75-76K despite ETF outflows. See `usa.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** oil's reversal deepened into the US close (Brent −2.69% to $105.83, WTI −3.21% to $102.43) on the Saudi pipeline de-escalation, while gold and silver's pre-FOMC hedging rally was cut short by the hawkish dot plot — a clean confirmation that the risk this project flagged in the pre-decision snapshot ("if the Fed frames today's hike as dovish, gold/silver extend their rally") materialized in the opposite direction. Credit spreads (FRED: HY OAS 2.76%, IG OAS 0.80%, both as of Sept 15, the latest FRED had posted) stayed tight, showing no sign of credit-market stress despite the hawkish surprise and the 10-year yield holding near its 5% multi-decade high.

## Oil

**Brent crude closed at $105.83/barrel (−2.69%) and WTI closed at $102.43/barrel (−3.21%)**, extending Wednesday's earlier reversal after the **US said Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — damaged in recent strikes — would resume operations within days**. This is a genuine, now-confirmed-through-the-close de-escalation in the Middle East supply-disruption story that dominated this project's oil coverage for over a week, though ongoing Strait of Hormuz tensions (Houthi shipping threats) mean the situation remains fluid.

## Gold

**Spot gold settled at $4,310.10/oz, up a modest 0.38% on the day**, after December gold futures had spiked as high as $4,387.50 (+1.26%) ahead of the Fed decision — a clean reversal once the dot plot's hawkish surprise landed. **Silver held on to more of its gain, finishing at $64.92/oz (+1.66%)**. Ongoing Strait of Hormuz tensions continue to support some defensive demand for both metals, but the hawkish Fed outcome capped what had been a larger pre-decision rally.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin held the $75,000-$76,000 range**, roughly flat versus Tuesday's confirmed close (~$75,894.44), showing resilience despite the Fed's hawkish surprise — though **spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $746 million in net outflows across September 15-16**, an added headwind that bitcoin's price action absorbed without a deeper breakdown. Commentary attributed the muted reaction partly to the 25bp hike itself being widely priced in beforehand.
