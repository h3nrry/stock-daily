# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 16, 2026 (Wednesday)

**The Fed hiked, the dot plot signaled more to come, and stocks sold off before paring losses into the close.** The FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to **3.75%–4.00%** — a unanimous **12-0 vote**, and the Fed's **first rate increase since 2023**. The real surprise was the dot plot: **16 of the 18 policymakers submitting 2026 projections now see at least one more quarter-point hike before year-end**, up sharply from just 6 who held that view at the June meeting. Chair **Kevin Warsh** (confirmed across multiple sources, resolving the placeholder file's earlier uncertainty) struck a hawkish tone in the press conference, saying "this summer's inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved" and that he was "hard-pressed to call financial conditions restrictive" — while attributing the summer's rising bond yields to "economic strength, competition for capital/surge in capex, and geopolitics." Stronger-than-expected data reinforced the hawkish case: **August retail sales rose 1.2%**, beating estimates.

## What drove the market

- **The Dow underperformed the S&P and Nasdaq by a wide margin**, reflecting "concentrated pressure in the old-economy blue chips" (per one vendor's own framing) rather than a broad-based sell-off — **energy (XLE −2.8%)** and **financials (XLF −1.5%)** led sector declines, with cautious commentary from Goldman Sachs weighing on banks even as Goldman's own stock was among the session's laggards.
- **Homebuilders tumbled** as mortgage rates climbed above 7% in the wake of the hike; the NAHB homebuilder sentiment index fell to 32, a one-year low, and **Truist downgraded M/I Homes (MTH)**.
- **Semiconductors were mixed but the Intel–SK Hynix chip-partnership story continued to dominate single-stock flows**: **Intel (INTC) closed up 4.34%** and **SK Hynix's ADR (SKHY) rose 1.31%** on continuing reports the two are in talks over Intel-based US manufacturing operations — a story that carried across Wednesday's Asian sessions as well (see `../260916/korea.md`, `../260916/taiwan.md`, `../260916/japan.md`). Bank of America separately raised its 2030 global semiconductor market forecast to $3.2 trillion, citing stronger memory/data-center growth — a tailwind referenced in Wednesday's European session too (see `uk_europe.md`).
- **ON Semiconductor (ON) fell roughly 8.5% to $68.11**, wiping out about $2 billion of market value — a reminder that the chip trade's gains were concentrated in the Intel/SK Hynix names specifically, not sector-wide.
- **Boeing (BA) fell 3.75%** on 737 MAX production-delay concerns.
- Smaller-cap movers: **Cipher Mining (CIFR) +12%** on new ERCOT data-center-capacity designations, **Alvotech (ALVO) +8%** on a Barclays upgrade tied to upcoming product approvals; **J.B. Hunt (JBHT) −12%** on guidance for Q3 earnings to drop 5-10% on higher operating costs, **Cellectis (CLLS) −13%** on a downgrade after concerning preclinical data, and **Expedia (EXPE) −2%** after Morgan Stanley initiated coverage citing consumer weakness.
- **The 10-year Treasury yield closed back around 5.00-5.02%**, essentially flat to marginally higher versus Tuesday's already-confirmed 5.002% close, after dipping intraday to roughly 4.97% ahead of the decision and then climbing back above 5% on the hawkish dot plot (CNBC/briefs.co: "climbs back to 5%," 5.016%; Investrade: 5.003%, +8bp intraday) — sources differ on the exact size of the day's move (2bp vs 8bp) though they agree the level closed essentially at the 5% threshold, a genuine multi-decade high last seen in 2007. The 2-year yield rose to roughly 4.72% (+6.5bp), flattening the 2s10s spread to about 30bp. Credit spreads stayed tight and little-changed: FRED's ICE BofA High Yield OAS was last posted at **2.76% (Sept 15)** and Investment Grade OAS at **0.80% (Sept 15)** — FRED had not yet posted a Sept 16 print for either series at writing time, consistent with this series' typical one-day reporting lag; not fabricated as a Sept 16 value.
- **Oil fell for a second straight session**, extending Wednesday's earlier reversal documented in the placeholder file: **Brent crude closed at $105.83/barrel (−2.69%)** and **WTI closed at $102.43/barrel (−3.21%)**, continuing to unwind after the **US said Saudi Arabia's damaged East-West pipeline would resume operations within days** — a meaningful de-escalation from the pipeline-disruption story that had driven oil above $108-113/barrel across Monday and Tuesday.
- **Gold and silver spiked pre-decision, then reversed on the hawkish hike/dot-plot combination** — a clean confirmation of the dynamic the placeholder file had flagged as a risk ("if the Fed frames today's expected hike as a limited response to oil-driven inflation, gold and silver can extend the relief move" — instead, the dot plot's hawkish surprise cut the rally short). December gold futures had spiked as high as $4,387.50 (+1.26%) ahead of the announcement before **spot gold settled around $4,310.10/oz, up a more modest 0.38% on the day** (Kitco PM report), while **silver futures still finished higher at $64.92/oz (+1.66%)**, holding on to more of its pre-decision gains than gold did.
- **Bitcoin held in the $75,000–$76,000 range**, roughly flat versus Tuesday's confirmed close (~$75,894.44), showing resilience despite the hawkish outcome — though **spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded roughly $746 million in net outflows across September 15-16**, an added headwind. Commentary noted the 25bp hike was widely priced in beforehand, limiting the incremental downside from confirmation alone.

## Money-flow analysis

Wednesday's session was a genuine "sell the (hawkish) news" event layered on top of a rotation away from rate-sensitive, old-economy sectors (financials, homebuilders, energy) and into large-cap tech/semis, which is why the Dow underperformed the Nasdaq by more than a full percentage point despite both facing the same macro shock. The chip-partnership story (Intel/SK Hynix) kept flowing capital into a narrow set of semiconductor names even as the broader chip complex (ON Semiconductor) sold off sharply — a bifurcation, not a sector-wide rally, that echoes the same pattern seen in Wednesday's Korea and Hong Kong sessions. Gold and silver's late-session reversal, and bitcoin's ETF outflows, both point to a hawkish-surprise unwind of pre-FOMC hedging positions rather than a rotation into genuinely defensive assets — consistent with yields holding near 5% rather than falling on a "hike now priced in" relief.

## Bottom line

The Fed delivered the expected 25bp hike but paired it with an unexpectedly hawkish dot plot (16 of 18 officials seeing at least one more 2026 hike), which is what actually moved markets — equities sold off broadly, yields held near a multi-decade 5% high, and gold/silver's pre-decision rally reversed sharply. The Dow's underperformance versus the Nasdaq reflects a rotation out of rate-sensitive sectors (financials, homebuilders, energy) rather than a broad flight from risk, and the Intel/SK Hynix chip story remained the market's most durable bullish thread even as the broader semiconductor complex (ON Semiconductor) diverged sharply lower.
