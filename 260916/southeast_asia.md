# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 16, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia was mixed on Wednesday, with **Malaysia's Bursa Malaysia closed for the Malaysia Day (Hari Malaysia) public holiday** — confirmed via a holiday-calendar check ahead of building this folder (see "Malaysia — market holiday" below), the first regional holiday closure this project has flagged all week. Among the three markets that traded, **Thailand's SET fell sharply (−1.01%)** on domestic energy-policy and tourism-stimulus news, while **Indonesia's IHSG slipped a more modest −0.38%** and **Singapore's STI was roughly flat to slightly lower**, both ahead of Wednesday night's FOMC decision.

## Malaysia — market holiday

**Bursa Malaysia was closed Wednesday, September 16, 2026 for Malaysia Day (Hari Malaysia)**, a nationwide public holiday marking the 1963 formation of the Malaysian federation. This was confirmed via a holiday-calendar check (officeholidays.com, awarenessdays.com, nationaltoday.com all list September 16 as a fixed-date Malaysia Day holiday) rather than assumed — consistent with this project's practice of verifying rather than guessing near a possible holiday, especially in FOMC week when getting every market's status right matters. No FBM KLCI close exists for today; see `../260915/southeast_asia.md` for the last confirmed close (1,679.21) as the reference point for Thursday's session.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,635.41, down 3.23 points (−0.06%)** per Trading Economics' own internally-consistent series. However, this figure does not reconcile against this project's own confirmed Tuesday close (5,660.75; see `../260915/southeast_asia.md`) — a vendor-prior-close divergence of the same kind this project has repeatedly documented for European indices (CAC 40, FTSE, STOXX 600). Recomputed against this project's own confirmed prior close, Wednesday's move is closer to **−0.45%** (5,635.41 vs. 5,660.75). No same-day Singapore news wrap with sector/stock-level detail was found from sources checked — a persistent, recurring gap for Singapore coverage this project has flagged nearly every session.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,436.85, down 24.30 points (−0.38%)**, per CNBC Indonesia's own closing report — reversing an earlier intraday rebound (the index had briefly turned up as much as 1% during the session per an earlier CNBC Indonesia "Breaking News" piece) as renewed pressure from global bond yields took hold into the close. **Utilities (−1.84%) and technology (−1.36%) led sector declines.** Market breadth was negative, with **456 declining issues against 213 advancers**. The session's pressure traced directly to the same global macro backdrop hitting every market this project covers pre-FOMC: the US 10-year Treasury yield reaching **5.041% — its highest level since 2007** — and elevated energy prices compounding inflation concerns, with the Fed decision (due Sept 17 Jakarta time) priced at a **92.4% probability of a 25-basis-point hike** per CME FedWatch.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index fell 15.93 points (−1.01%) to close at 1,562.73**, on trading value of **฿75.54 billion** — a figure that reconciles cleanly against this project's own confirmed Tuesday close (1,578.66; see `../260915/southeast_asia.md`). The decline traced to several domestic-specific headwinds rather than the broader regional pre-FOMC caution alone: **an Energy Policy Administration Committee resolution to reduce the ex-refinery price for diesel** weighed on energy-sector names, **reports of a delay in the Thai Travel Plus tourism-stimulus program rollout** pressured tourism-related equities, and the **telecommunications sector also faced selling pressure**. An analyst quoted in Kaohoon's market roundup noted that "the Thai stock market plunged and underperformed the northern Asian peers" — a notable contrast with Wednesday's semiconductor-led rallies in Korea, Taiwan, and Hong Kong.

## Bottom line

Wednesday's dominant Southeast Asia story is actually a non-market one: **Malaysia's Bursa Malaysia was closed for the Malaysia Day public holiday**, the first confirmed regional market closure this project has tracked all week — a reminder to verify holiday calendars proactively rather than assume every session is a normal trading day, especially in a week as newsworthy as FOMC week. Among the markets that did trade, Thailand's session was driven almost entirely by domestic catalysts (an energy-price policy change, a tourism-stimulus delay) rather than the global pre-FOMC caution affecting the rest of the region, while Indonesia and Singapore both posted more modest, macro-driven declines tied to the US 10-year Treasury yield's continued proximity to 5% (its highest since 2007) ahead of the Fed's decision. Notably, Southeast Asia's mixed, mostly-negative session stands in contrast to the semiconductor-led rallies seen in Korea, Taiwan, and Hong Kong the same day (see those files) — a reminder that the SK Hynix–Intel partnership story lifting North Asian chip names had little direct read-through for this region.
