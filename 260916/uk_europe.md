**European equities closed higher for the first session in three, extending the pre-close intraday recovery documented in this project's earlier snapshot**, as easing oil prices and a widely-anticipated (if hawkish-flavored) Fed hike gave markets room to bounce off Tuesday's three-month low. ## What drove the market

- **Germany — a genuinely bifurcated session.** Ariva.de's Frankfurt closing report described the session as balancing an **AI/semiconductor tailwind** (Bank of America raised its 2030 global semiconductor-market forecast to $3.2 trillion, citing "stronger growth in memory and data center segments plus clearer recovery in industrial markets" — a story that also lifted the Intel/SK Hynix trade in the US and Asia this same session, see `usa.md`) against **auto-sector weakness**: Berenberg analyst Romain Gourvil warned of "weak earnings outlooks, persistent overcapacity, and rising competition" from China facing the sector, and **Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz both fell 2.6%**. The **MDax rose 0.45% to 31,276.77** and the **EuroStoxx 50 gained 0.48% to 6,266.50**, both broadly in line with the DAX's own gain.
- **UK — miners, banks, and housebuilders led gains.** London's advance was concentrated in **mining shares, which firmed on stronger commodity prices**, alongside **banking stocks** and **housebuilders** — **Barratt Redrow shares advanced after reporting improved reservation rates, a solid order book, and profit ahead of forecasts**. UK inflation had landed at 3.1% year-on-year ahead of the open (per Sunday Guardian Live's preview, already noted in the earlier snapshot), a modest beat the market digested without derailing the day's risk-on tone.
- **Oil's continued reversal provided a genuine tailwind into the close.** The earlier snapshot flagged a stale "$113/barrel" reference for Brent from an intraday European wire; by Wednesday's close, Brent had fallen further still (see `usa.md`'s $105.83, −2.69% US-session close) after the **US said Saudi Arabia's damaged East-West pipeline would resume operations within days** — the same de-escalation story that lifted US and Asian markets this session.
- **The Fed decision landed after European markets had already closed** (2pm ET / roughly 2am Taipei Thursday, several hours past Europe's ~23:30 Taipei close), so Wednesday's European session reflects pre-decision positioning rather than a reaction to the actual 25bp hike and hawkish dot plot — European markets will only get to react to Wednesday's FOMC outcome in Thursday's session. A pre-close Zonebourse piece ("Acheter la rumeur, vendre la Fed?" — "buy the rumor, sell the Fed?") captured this dynamic directly, describing investors "positioning with a slight positive bias" into the decision after five declines in six sessions, with the CAC 40 trading as high as 8,195.74 (+0.68%) intraday before giving back some of that gain into the 8,140.59 close.
- **Fed-hike odds were priced at roughly 92-93% for a 25bp move** ahead of the European close, consistent with the odds cited across US/Asia sources this same session (see `usa.md`) — and, as it turned out, an accurate read on the hike itself, if not on the hawkish dot-plot surprise that followed hours later.

## Money-flow analysis

Wednesday's European session was a genuine, broad-based recovery from Tuesday's three-month low — every major index (FTSE, DAX, CAC, STOXX 600) closed higher, a reversal of Tuesday's region-wide decline. The gains were sector-rotational rather than uniform: Germany's session split between AI/semiconductor optimism and auto-sector weakness (Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz), while the UK's advance concentrated in cyclical, commodity- and rate-sensitive names (miners, banks, housebuilders) rather than defensives — consistent with a market positioning for a "hike now priced in, no surprise" outcome ahead of the close. Because Europe's close preceded the actual Fed announcement by several hours, Wednesday's gains reflect that positioning bet rather than a reaction to the dot plot's hawkish surprise; Thursday's European session is the one to watch for how (or whether) that surprise gets priced in a market that had already rallied into it.

## Bottom line

European markets closed broadly higher Wednesday — FTSE +0.28%, DAX +0.53%, CAC 40 +0.62%, STOXX 600 +0.46% — recovering from Tuesday's three-month low on easing oil prices and pre-Fed positioning, but the region's close came hours before the actual FOMC decision and its hawkish dot-plot surprise (see `usa.md`). Thursday's European session, not Wednesday's, is where the market gets its first chance to price in 16 of 18 Fed officials expecting at least one more 2026 hike. Today's DAX closing level also saw a genuine, documented vendor discrepancy (25,537.75 vs. 25,493.14) worth tracking into the next session.
