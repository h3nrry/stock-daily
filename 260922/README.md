# Daily Market Briefing — September 22, 2026 (Tuesday)

**Tuesday was a "pause and rotate" day almost everywhere after Monday's broad AI-led surge — index-level moves were small in the US and Europe even as sector-level rotation (banks out, tech in) and country-specific catalysts (a substrate/PCB rally across Taiwan, Korea and briefly Hong Kong; Indonesia's sharper decline; Malaysia's bargain-hunting rally) did most of the day's real work.** **Taiwan's TAIEX hit a fresh closing record (47,800.17, +0.17%) only after an 801-point intraday round-trip driven by pre-holiday profit-taking**, with foreign investors still net-buying for a fourth straight day. **Korea's KOSPI (+0.15% to 7,017.91) gave back a 2%+ morning surge as individual investors sold heavily**, even as substrate/PCB stocks (Korea Circuit +28.68%) staged the region's most dramatic single-stock rally on AI-server-substrate demand. **Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose a third straight day (+0.18% to 25,087.75)** on Tencent's +5.02% move, with southbound Stock Connect buying extending to a 12th straight session. **Japan stayed closed for a second of three straight holiday days**, with Osaka Exchange Nikkei futures continuing to grind higher (to a "66,000-yen range") on the same US AI optimism. **Southeast Asia split again**: Malaysia +0.99% and Thailand +0.40% gained, Indonesia fell a sharper -1.69% to a multi-week low, and Singapore rose ≈+0.85% (derived later from Wednesday's close). **Europe was muted**: the DAX closed essentially flat (+0.02%, confirmed), while FTSE 100 slipped 0.29% and the CAC 40 rose 0.20% (both confirmed on a Sept 24 follow-up pass). **The US closed effectively flat to mixed**: the S&P 500 (-0.05%) barely moved, the Dow fell 0.36% on a rotation out of bank stocks (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan) into technology, and the Nasdaq Composite closed at a second straight record (27,244.28, +0.45%) on continued AI optimism. *(Correction: an earlier version said Micron reported after Tuesday's close — Micron actually reports on Sept 30.)*

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** a fresh closing record (47,800.17, +0.17%) that hid an 801-point intraday round-trip — MediaTek, TSMC and AUO all faded hard from morning highs on pre-holiday profit-taking, even as ABF/substrate names (Global Unichip, Aspeed Technology — Taiwan's first NT$20,000 stock) kept setting fresh records. Institutions stayed net buyers for a fourth straight day (**+NT$60.821 billion**, ~74% from foreign investors alone). See `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** still closed (day 2 of 3). Osaka Exchange Nikkei futures extended Monday's gain, rising as much as 1,880 yen intraday before paring back roughly 930 yen, settling in a "66,000-yen range" — tracking Monday's US AI rally and a 4%+ overnight SOX surge. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** a 2%+ morning surge (KOSPI as high as 7,171.44) was almost entirely erased by heavy individual-investor selling, leaving the index "barely holding 7,000" (+0.15% to 7,017.91). The real story was rotation: **substrate/PCB stocks (Korea Circuit +28.68%, TLB +19.82%, Simtec +17.98%) rallied hard on AI-server-substrate demand**, while Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both reversed from steep morning gains. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a third straight gain (+0.18% to 25,087.75) led narrowly by Tencent (+5.02%), with southbound Stock Connect buying extending to a **12th consecutive day** (>HK$12 billion). Monday's thematic winners — shipping, PCB — both reversed sharply, a reminder that several of this week's Asia-wide rallies are running on fast-rotating money. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Malaysia (+0.99%) led on bargain-hunting ahead of the Trump-Xi summit and easing US yields; Thailand (+0.40%) extended its Fitch-upgrade-driven gain; Indonesia fell a sharper −1.69% to a fresh multi-week low on broad-based selling (only 139 of ~790 stocks advanced); Singapore rose ≈+0.85% (derived on the Sept 24 follow-up). See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **UK/Europe:** a pause-and-digest session after Monday's broad rebound — the DAX's confirmed +0.02% close came with resistance at its 50-day moving average, and sector rotation (retail up on Kingfisher's guidance, insurance down) was the clearest available signal. Confirmed later: FTSE 100 −0.29%, CAC 40 +0.20%, STOXX 600 +0.13%. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **United States:** a rotation day — money moved out of banks (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab −5.70%) into technology, leaving the S&P 500 unchanged (7,764.64) and the Dow down 0.36%, even as the Nasdaq Composite closed at a second straight record (27,244.28, +0.45%) and the SOX added 2.06%. Oil's fifth straight losing session supported risk appetite. (Micron reports Sept 30, not Sept 22 as originally stated.) See `usa.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** oil extended its decline to a fifth straight session (WTI ≈$89–91, Brent briefly below $100 before rebounding to $101.88 by Wednesday morning) on Iran's Hormuz-reopening signal; gold was little changed (~$4,336); bitcoin pulled back modestly from Monday's ~$87,000 close to trade near $86,000 as US equities posted only small gains.

## Oil

**Oil extended its decline to a fifth straight session.** Vendor snapshots converged on **WTI crude at roughly $89–91/barrel (down about 2.5–3.2% on the day)** — Invezz read $89.42 (−3.19%), Investing.com read $90.97 (−1.52%) — and **Brent crude at roughly $97.6–99.6/barrel**, with Brent **briefly dipping below the psychologically significant $100 mark during European trading hours** (see `uk_europe.md`) before rebounding to **$101.88 (+1.53%) by Wednesday morning**, a notably fast reversal. Drivers: **Iran signaled it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days** under certain conditions, and President Trump's UN General Assembly remarks continued to feed hopes for US–Iran diplomatic de-escalation. Falling oil weighed on Chevron (the Dow's only energy component) but supported broader risk sentiment and lower-oil-beneficiary markets (Malaysia, Thailand — see `southeast_asia.md`).

## Gold

Hong Kong gold-mining names extended Monday's weakness (see `hongkong.md`) on continued US-dollar strength and Fed rate-hike expectations.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin traded near $86,000**, pulling back modestly from Monday's confirmed ~$87,000 close (CoinDesk's live-updates page for the session was headlined "Bitcoin trades near $86,000 as U.S. stocks post small gains"). The session's range spanned an intraday high near **$87,300** to snapshots as low as **$85,766**, consistent with a genuine, if modest, pullback after Monday's short-squeeze-driven surge (see `../260921/README.md`) rather than a reversal of the broader trend.
