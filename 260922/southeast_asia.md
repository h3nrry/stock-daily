# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 22, 2026 (Tuesday)

**Summary:** A split session again, but with Indonesia's decline much sharper this time. **Malaysia (+0.99%) led** on bargain-hunting ahead of the Trump-Xi summit, **Thailand (+0.40%) extended its gain** on lower oil/yields, **Singapore rose ≈+0.85%** (derived on the Sept 24 follow-up from Wednesday's confirmed close), while **Indonesia fell a sharp 1.69% to a fresh multi-week low**, its second straight decline.

## Singapore — Straits Times Index (STI)

**Updated Sept 24 (derived close):** no direct Sept 22 closing print was ever found for Singapore, but the next day's confirmed close allows a clean back-calculation: headtopics' Daily Markets Briefing reported the **STI closed Wednesday Sept 23 at 5,709.91, down 0.24%**, which implies a **Sept 22 close of ≈5,723.6 — up ≈+0.85% from Monday's 5,675.23**. Trading Economics' Sept 23 row (≈5,710, −0.24%) corroborates the same implied level. This is presented as a **derived** figure, not an independently sourced print — but it is arithmetically tight and means Singapore joined Malaysia and Thailand on the positive side on Tuesday. (The original pass found every source still stuck on Monday's 5,675.23.)

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

| Breadth | 139 advancers / 533 decliners / 118 unchanged |

**Drivers:** Coverage flagged the decline as broad-based (only 139 of ~790 listed stocks advanced), extending Monday's weakness (see `../260921/southeast_asia.md`, which cited Bayan Resources and rupiah softness as the prior day's drag). Indonesia's own reporting (CNBC Indonesia, TVOne, Liputan6) described the session with headlines ranging from "turun 1% lebih" (intraday) to "anjlok 1,69%" (final), consistent with a decline that worsened through the day rather than stabilizing. **Indonesia was again the region's laggard**, bucking gains in Malaysia and Thailand. Longer-window context from the same data source: month-to-date −3.45%, week-to-date −2.85%.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

**Drivers:** Malay Mail described the session as driven by **"bargain hunting following the recent heavy sell-off"** and positive regional sentiment **ahead of the Trump–Xi meeting**, with **"renewed strength in technology shares and easing US Treasury yields"** cited as easing macro concerns. **Hong Leong Bank +48 sen to RM23.38** and **Malaysian Pacific Industries +40 sen to RM43.20** led gainers; **Nestlé −54 sen to RM89.16** and **Allianz Malaysia −20 sen to RM21.10** were among the laggards. This is Malaysia's best single-day % gain logged in this project in several sessions (see `../260921/southeast_asia.md` for the prior day's +0.08%).

**Drivers:** Kaohoon International attributed the gain to **"positive momentum in overseas markets,"** with **declining oil prices and lower US bond yields** easing investor concerns and **"buying interest in the technology sector"** supporting domestic sentiment — a continuation of Monday's Fitch-upgrade-driven advance (see `../260921/southeast_asia.md`). The outlook called for the market to **"move sideways within range"** the following session.

## Money-flow read-through

Tuesday split the region along the same broad lines as Monday — **oil-relief and tech-rally beneficiaries (Malaysia, Thailand) versus Indonesia's commodity/rupiah-driven weakness** — but Indonesia's decline accelerated (from −0.88% Monday to −1.69% Tuesday) rather than stabilizing, a genuine deterioration worth flagging heading into this week's Bank Indonesia rate decision. Malaysia's participation in the global "tech read-through" trade (explicitly cited by Malay Mail) is notable given the substrate/PCB rallies playing out the same day in Taiwan, Korea and (initially) Hong Kong.

## Bottom line

Tuesday extended the region's now-familiar pattern: **country-specific catalysts (Malaysia's bargain-hunting rally, Thailand's continued Fitch-driven strength) outweighed the global tape for the gainers, while Indonesia's commodity exposure and rupiah softness drove a second straight, and sharper, decline (−1.69% to 6,277, a multi-week low)**. Singapore's close was later derived at ≈5,723.6 (≈+0.85%) from Wednesday's confirmed print — see the Singapore section.
