# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 4, 2026 (Friday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia had a genuinely mixed session on Friday — a contrast to Thursday's broadly positive tone across the region (see `../260903/southeast_asia.md`). Singapore pushed to a fresh record high, crossing 5,800 for the first time, while Indonesia reversed an early gain into a decline on renewed Iran/oil-related risk aversion, Malaysia pared back after two days of gains on profit-taking and foreign selling, and Thailand extended its own rally on the same dovish Fed signal lifting markets elsewhere.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index crossed the 5,800 level for the first time**, climbing as much as **1.0% to a fresh intraday record high of approximately 5,803 points** around midday Friday, extending Thursday's own intraday record (5,807.16, which had faded to a flat close — see `../260903/southeast_asia.md`). For the week, the index was on track for a **1.8% gain**. The advance was driven by the same catalyst lifting markets globally: continued momentum from Wall Street's Thursday rally and easing US Treasury yields, though rising oil prices remained a partial headwind by intensifying inflation concerns. **Sector leadership was concentrated in communication services, non-energy minerals, and financials**: **Hong Kong Land rose +4.2%**, **Yangzijiang Shipbuilding gained +3.6%**, **City Developments advanced +2.4%**, and banks **OCBC (+1.2%) and UOB (+1.1%)** extended their own multi-day strength. *A confirmed final closing level was not available from sources checked at writing time — this section reflects the midday record-high level and the week-to-date gain figure explicitly reported by wire sources; see note in the README.*

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,636.47, down 31.41 points (−0.47%)** — a genuine reversal from an opening gain of +0.43% to 6,695, which faded through the session on renewed concern about **escalation in the US-Iran conflict and tensions in the Strait of Hormuz**, threatening to keep oil prices elevated. The reversal came alongside a notable piece of house-view news: **JPMorgan cut its year-end 2026 IHSG target to 7,000**, down sharply from an initial 10,000 projection, citing high interest rates, rising oil prices, and potential food-inflation risk — the bank noted the index has already corrected some **23% since January 2026** on these external pressures. Market breadth was negative, with **325 declining stocks versus 266 advancing** (196 unchanged) on turnover of **Rp8.3 trillion**. **Healthcare (−1.04%), transportation (−0.68%), and non-cyclical consumer stocks (−0.54%)** led the declines across seven pressured sectors. Top losers included **ESSA (−2.78%)**, **ASII/Astra International (−2.57%)**, and **HRTA (−2.16%)**; top gainers were **CUAN (+2.20%)**, **BUMI (+0.97%)**, and **AADI (+0.89%)**.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,708.10, down 7.03 points (−0.41%)**, snapping a two-session winning streak that had run through Thursday's rate-hold-driven gain (see `../260903/southeast_asia.md`). The pullback reflected **profit-taking after two days of gains**, compounded by **continued foreign selling (a net outflow of RM75 million)** and **softer crude oil prices** weighing on plantation and energy-linked names. Market breadth was negative (523 gainers vs. 568 decliners, 596 unchanged) on trading value of **RM2.98 billion**. Notable decliners included **United Plantations (−96 sen to RM32.06)**, **Carlsberg (−84 sen to RM13.02)**, **Heineken Malaysia (−80 sen to RM14.84)**, and **PETRONAS Gas (−36 sen to RM17.26)**; **F&N (+38 sen to RM24.76)** and **Nestlé Malaysia (+36 sen to RM94.98)** were among the session's few notable gainers.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,595.58, up 18.66 points (+1.18%)**, on trading value of **THB 63.42 billion** — extending Thursday's modest rebound with a stronger follow-through gain. The rally tracked the same catalyst lifting markets across this project's coverage: a **Federal Reserve official (Governor Waller) signaling the central bank is likely to hold its policy rate steady should inflation stabilize**, which pulled bond yields lower and encouraged buying in large-cap names. **DELTA (Delta Electronics Thailand) and GULF (Gulf Energy Development)** led the gains, while **PTT (−1.20%)** carried the day's highest trading value (THB 4.05 billion) despite declining — a reminder that Thailand's energy-linked names don't always track the broader index's direction.

## Bottom line

A genuinely split session across the four Southeast Asian markets covered here: Singapore's fresh record high (crossing 5,800 for the first time) and Thailand's extended rally both tracked the same dovish-Fed, falling-yields tone lifting markets globally, while Indonesia's reversal from an opening gain into a −0.47% decline reflected renewed, geography-specific concern about Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz oil-supply risk — a genuinely different driver from the region's shared "yields easing" narrative. Malaysia's pullback after two days of gains reads as ordinary profit-taking rather than a change in trend. JPMorgan's sharp cut to its IHSG year-end target (10,000 → 7,000) is the standout piece of news from the session, a reminder that Indonesia's own 23% year-to-date correction has been driven by real, external macro pressures rather than sentiment alone.
