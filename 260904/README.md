# Daily Market Briefing — September 4, 2026 (Friday)

Global snapshot: Friday was a genuine story of Asia-Pacific catching up to Thursday's US-led relief rally, followed by a much quieter, more ambivalent handoff to Europe and the US as a stronger-than-expected American jobs report complicated the dovish-Fed narrative that had driven Thursday's gains in the first place. **Every fully-closed Asia-Pacific market covered here rallied**: Japan's Nikkei snapped a four-session losing streak to close **+1.26%** (though the gain was unusually concentrated in just two stocks, SoftBank Group and Advantest), Korea's KOSPI surged **+1.64%** on record August semiconductor exports, Hong Kong's Hang Seng jumped **+1.74%** on an AI/internet-driven rally plus a quarterly index rebalancing, and Taiwan's TAIEX rebounded **+1.51%**, fully reversing Thursday's IC-substrate-driven selloff. Southeast Asia was more mixed: **Singapore closed at a fresh record 5,801.96 (+0.94%)**, crossing 5,800 for the first time, on continued bank/property strength, and **Thailand extended its own rally +1.18%** on the same dovish-Fed tailwind, while **Indonesia reversed an opening gain into a −0.47% decline** on renewed Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz oil-risk concerns (alongside a sharp JPMorgan target cut, 10,000 → 7,000), and **Malaysia pulled back −0.41%** on ordinary profit-taking after two days of gains. By the time Europe and the US opened, the picture had genuinely shifted: **US nonfarm payrolls came in far stronger than expected** (+162,000 vs. ~55,000 consensus), pushing September rate-hike odds back up and complicating Thursday's rate-cut-driven rally — **European markets closed the week essentially flat** (FTSE −0.07%, DAX +0.17%, CAC −0.31%, STOXX 600 +0.17%), digesting the data mid-session, and **US equities closed modestly lower** (Dow −0.51%, S&P 500 −0.38%, Nasdaq −0.29%), with the small-cap Russell 2000 the lone gainer (+0.25%) — a mild, broad pullback rather than a rout, though Lululemon's 17% guidance-cut-driven plunge was the day's most dramatic single-stock move. Oil eased further from the week's Iran-driven highs (Brent ~$96.90, −2.49%), gold pulled back from Thursday's sharp rally (~$4,468.60, −1.57%) as yields ticked modestly higher, and bitcoin held roughly steady near $81,200–81,300 on continued strong ETF inflows.

**Reading the table:** today's clearest story is Asia-Pacific finishing what Thursday started — every fully-closed Asian market except Indonesia and Malaysia posted a solid gain, several (Korea, Hong Kong, Taiwan) on genuinely fundamental catalysts (record semiconductor exports, an AI/internet rally, a full reversal of a sector-specific selloff) rather than pure carry-over sentiment, and Singapore's record close now confirms what had been an intraday-only milestone. By the time trading moved west, though, the mood had genuinely shifted: a stronger-than-expected US jobs report arrived mid-Europe-session and left European markets essentially flat, and the same data pulled the US modestly lower by the close — a mild, broad pullback (only 3 of 11 S&P sectors advanced) rather than a sharp reversal, with credit spreads barely moving even as short-end Treasury yields hit fresh 52-week highs. The 20-day column remains dominated by France's CAC 40 (−5.21%), still the region's standout underperformer on its own domestic political overhang, while Korea's KOSPI's extreme +6.21% 20-day figure reflects just how sharp this month's whipsaw has been in both directions.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** a much-stronger-than-expected August jobs report (+162,000 vs. ~55,000 consensus) directly undid much of Thursday's dovish-Fed-driven rally, pushing September rate-hike odds back up and lifting short-end Treasury yields to fresh 52-week highs (2Y 4.374%, 5Y 4.545%), while the 10-year (4.78%) and 30-year (~5.25%) moved only modestly — a sign markets are treating one data point with some caution. Equities closed mixed-to-lower (Dow −0.51%, S&P −0.38%, Nasdaq −0.29%), with only Technology, Industrials, and Utilities among S&P sectors advancing; credit spreads (IG 0.81%, HY 2.65%) barely budged. Lululemon's 17.4% guidance-cut-driven plunge to $100.61 was the session's standout single-stock story, unrelated to the macro narrative. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** a clean, broad reversal of Thursday's ABF-substrate-driven selloff — TAIEX +1.51% to 46,551.13, led by electronics contract manufacturers (Foxconn +3.43%, Quanta +2.99%) and a sharp rebound in passive components (Walton, AMAX both hit limit-up), tracking Thursday's strong US session directly. Same-day 三大法人 institutional flow data was not yet available at writing time. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** the region's most fundamentally-driven rally — KOSPI +1.64% on record August semiconductor exports ($46.65B, +209% YoY), with both foreign and institutional investors returning as net buyers (a genuine reversal from Thursday's catalyst-less selloff), even as individual investors took profits and financials lagged the broader gain. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** a broad, AI/internet-led rally (Hang Seng +1.74%, Hang Seng Tech +2.27%) — Meituan, Baidu, Xiaomi, and Ark Health all surged on AI/cloud-computing enthusiasm, even as semiconductor names notably bucked the trend. The session also carried a structural tailwind from the Hang Seng Index's quarterly rebalancing (534 → 580 constituents), estimated by Goldman Sachs to generate over $7.2B in passive flows. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Japan:** a decisive but genuinely narrow rebound — Nikkei +1.26%, snapping a four-session losing streak, but with SoftBank Group and Advantest alone accounting for roughly 83% of the index's point gain, even as more Tokyo Prime Market stocks declined than advanced. See `japan.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** a genuinely split session — Singapore closed at a fresh record 5,801.96 (+0.94%, and now confirmed +1.79%/+2.89% over 5/20 trading days) and Thailand extended its rally (+1.18%) on the same dovish-Fed tailwind lifting markets elsewhere, while Indonesia reversed into a −0.47% decline on renewed Iran/oil-risk concerns (and a sharp JPMorgan year-end target cut, 10,000 → 7,000) and Malaysia pulled back −0.41% on ordinary post-rally profit-taking. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** closed the week essentially flat (FTSE −0.07%, DAX +0.17%, CAC −0.31%, STOXX 600 +0.17%) as markets digested the stronger US jobs report mid-session — a genuine step back from Thursday's decisive relief rally, with Volkswagen's company-specific strength helping Germany outperform and France's own September 8 confidence-vote overhang keeping the CAC 40 the region's clear laggard on every timeframe. See `uk_europe.md`.

## Oil

**Brent crude eased further to around $96.90/barrel, down roughly −2.49%** from Thursday's level near $99.38, continuing its pullback from the week's Iran-driven intraday spike toward $99. **WTI traded near $89.24, down roughly −2.26%.** Both benchmarks remain elevated relative to pre-Iran-strike levels but retreated for a second straight session as the immediate escalation risk that dominated earlier in the week did not materialize further — even as Indonesia's own market reversal today showed that Strait-of-Hormuz/Iran-conflict risk remains a live, market-moving concern for oil-import-dependent economies specifically.

## Gold

**Spot gold pulled back to around $4,468.60, down roughly −1.57%** from Thursday's $4,477.66 close — a partial reversal of Thursday's sharp +2.06% yields-relief rally, consistent with Friday's rise in Treasury yields following the stronger-than-expected US jobs data. Gold's pullback is a useful real-time signal of how markets digested the tension between Thursday's dovish Fed commentary and Friday's more hawkish labor-market surprise.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin held roughly steady near $81,200–81,300**, essentially flat from Thursday's sharp +5.07% close near $81,272, and closed out a **+1.3% weekly gain**. Crypto markets reportedly saw their **largest ETF inflow in nine months** following Thursday's dovish Fed commentary — a tailwind that persisted through Friday even as the stronger jobs report complicated the broader rate-cut narrative elsewhere. Ethereum traded near $2,522, also roughly flat on the day.
