# Daily Market Briefing — September 4, 2026 (Friday)

Global snapshot: Friday was a genuine story of Asia-Pacific catching up to Thursday's US-led relief rally, followed by a much quieter, more ambivalent handoff to Europe and the US as a stronger-than-expected American jobs report complicated the dovish-Fed narrative that had driven Thursday's gains in the first place. **Every fully-closed Asia-Pacific market covered here rallied**: Japan's Nikkei snapped a four-session losing streak to close **+1.26%** (though the gain was unusually concentrated in just two stocks, SoftBank Group and Advantest), Korea's KOSPI surged **+1.64%** on record August semiconductor exports, Hong Kong's Hang Seng jumped **+1.74%** on an AI/internet-driven rally plus a quarterly index rebalancing, and Taiwan's TAIEX rebounded **+1.51%**, fully reversing Thursday's IC-substrate-driven selloff. Southeast Asia was more mixed: **Singapore crossed 5,800 for the first time** on continued bank/property strength, and **Thailand extended its own rally +1.18%** on the same dovish-Fed tailwind, while **Indonesia reversed an opening gain into a −0.47% decline** on renewed Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz oil-risk concerns (alongside a sharp JPMorgan target cut, 10,000 → 7,000), and **Malaysia pulled back −0.41%** on ordinary profit-taking after two days of gains. By the time Europe and the US opened, the picture had genuinely shifted: **US nonfarm payrolls came in far stronger than expected** (+162,000 vs. ~55,000 consensus), pushing September rate-hike odds back up toward 59% and complicating Thursday's rate-cut-driven rally — **European markets closed the week essentially flat** (FTSE −0.07%, DAX +0.17%, CAC −0.31%, STOXX 600 +0.17%), digesting the data mid-session. **The US market itself remains open as of this writing** and is not yet closed — early-session US trading was mixed-to-slightly-lower, a genuinely unresolved tug-of-war between the strong labor data and continued dovish commentary from Fed Governor Waller. Oil eased further from the week's Iran-driven highs (Brent ~$96.90, −2.49%), gold pulled back from Thursday's sharp rally (~$4,468.60, −1.57%) as yields ticked modestly higher, and bitcoin held roughly steady near $81,200–81,300 on continued strong ETF inflows.
