# Taiwan Stock Market Analysis — September 4, 2026 (Friday)

## Headline

Taiwan closed out the week with a broad, powerful rally that erased Thursday's ABF-substrate-driven selloff and then some. The **TAIEX (加權指數) surged 693.47 points (+1.51%) to close at 46,551.13**, reclaiming the 46,500 level and moving back above its 5-day moving average, on turnover of **NT$825.6 billion**. The index posted a second consecutive weekly gain, up **219.68 points (+0.47%) for the week**. The rally directly tracked Thursday's strong Wall Street session (Dow +1.18%, S&P 500 +1.06%, Nasdaq +1.51%) on Fed Governor Waller's dovish disinflation comments and falling US Treasury yields (see `../260903/usa.md`) — Taiwan's Friday session closed at 1:30pm local time, hours before Friday's own US nonfarm payrolls report crossed the wires, so today's move reflects Thursday's US catalyst, not Friday's US jobs data (see `../usa.md` for that).

## What drove the rally

- **A clean, broad-based bounce tracking Wall Street, not a reversal of Thursday's specific selloff story**: electronics and financial names led broadly, with buying concentrated in semiconductor, memory, PCB, and AI-server-linked names, per local commentary — a signal that Thursday's IC-substrate/memory "repricing" selloff (see `../260903/taiwan.md`) was treated as a standalone, sector-specific event rather than the start of a broader downturn.
- **Electronics contract manufacturing led the charge**: **Foxconn / 鴻海 (2317) rose +3.43%** and **Quanta Computer / 廣達 (2382) gained +2.99%**, both benefiting from continued AI-server and data-center demand narratives.
- **Passive components staged a sharp rebound** from Thursday's weakness: **Walton / 華新科** and **AMAX / 信昌電子** both **hit the daily limit-up (漲停)**, a reversal from the sector-wide pressure seen in Thursday's session.
- **State-owned "national team" buying provided a floor across two sessions**: eight major state-owned banks (八大官股行庫) added a combined **NT$3.36 billion** over the two trading days into Friday, with buying concentrated in semiconductor, memory, PCB, and AI-server names — the same sectors hit hardest on Thursday.
- **Shipping-sector optimism added to the tone**: the Baltic Dry Index reached **3,488 points**, cited locally as a positive read-through for shipping-linked names.
- **TSMC / 台積電 (2330) closed at NT$2,410, up +0.84%** — a steadier, more moderate gain than the broader index, consistent with a rally led by second-tier electronics and financials rather than the index's single largest weight.

## Money flow — 三大法人 (three institutional investors)

Same-day net buy/sell figures for the three institutional investor categories (外資, 投信, 自營商) were not yet published by sources checked at writing time — Taiwan's official TWSE statistics and the usual CNA/UDN wrap-up articles for Friday's session had not posted before this file was compiled. Given the scale and breadth of Friday's rally (+1.51%, NT$825.6B turnover, broad electronics/financial buying), a net-buy day across the three categories combined would be the natural read, but this should be treated as an inference from price action rather than a confirmed flow figure — see `../260903/taiwan.md` for Thursday's confirmed NT$63.71B net-sell figure as the most recent verified data point.

## Bottom line

A clean, broad-based reversal of Thursday's IC-substrate/memory-driven selloff — Friday's +1.51% rally, led by electronics contract manufacturers and a sharp rebound in passive components, tracked Thursday's strong Wall Street session rather than resolving any Taiwan-specific overhang on its own. With TSMC posting a more moderate gain than the broader index and same-day institutional flow data not yet available, the clearest read is that Friday's buying was broad and second-tier-led rather than concentrated in the index's largest names — worth watching next week whether the IC-substrate names specifically hit hardest on Thursday (Nan Ya PCB, Unimicron) participate in any follow-through, which would help confirm whether Thursday's selloff was genuinely a one-off "repricing" event.
