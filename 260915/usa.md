# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 15, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

US equities fell for a second straight session as the "wall of worry" heading into Wednesday's FOMC decision kept building: the **S&P 500 closed at 7,585.73, down 0.45%**, the **Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 52,093.11, down 0.63%**, and the **Nasdaq Composite closed at 25,981.57, down 0.78%** — all three levels independently confirmed by Alain Guillot's recap, TheStreet, and Kiplinger, and all three reconciling cleanly against Monday's confirmed close (Dow 52,421.28, S&P 7,619.95, Nasdaq 26,186.41; see `../260914/usa.md`). The proximate driver shifted from Monday's direct AI-safety headlines to their bond-market and oil-market knock-on effects: the **10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.002%** (intraday peak ~5.04%, its highest since 2007) and **WTI crude surged roughly 4% to $105.83/barrel** on the still-unresolved Saudi pipeline disruption.

## What drove the decline

- **Treasury yields crossed 5% and stayed there.** The 10-year closed at **5.002%** (up 4.1bp), its highest close since October 2007, having touched an intraday peak near **5.04–5.05%**. The **2-year yield rose to 4.671%, a fresh 52-week high**, and the **30-year climbed to 5.367%**. The move made bonds meaningfully more attractive relative to equities and pushed the average 30-year mortgage rate to 7.17%, per Investrade's morning note — a rare moment where every part of the yield curve moved against equities simultaneously, one day before the Fed's own rate decision.
- **Oil extended its climb sharply.** **WTI crude jumped roughly 4–4.5% to $105.83–105.84/barrel** and **Brent crude rose about 3% to $108.75/barrel**, with some physical European cargoes reportedly trading above $130/barrel intraday, all still tracing back to Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline disruption (Riyadh formally blamed Iran-backed elements in Iraq) compounding the weekend's Houthi-attack damage — a story now affecting an estimated 4% of global oil supply. **Energy stocks were the session's best-performing sector, up roughly 2.4%**, with **Chevron (CVX) gaining 2.6%**.
- **Fed-hike odds climbed further**: markets were pricing a **91–93% probability of a 25-basis-point hike** at Wednesday's FOMC decision, up from roughly 85–88% a day earlier, with attention now shifting to whether the hike proves a one-time move or the start of further tightening.
- **Semiconductors stabilized after Monday's rout rather than extending it** — a notable divergence from the broader macro story. **Skyworks Solutions (SWKS) surged 13.6%** and **Qorvo (QRVO) jumped 9.3%** on analog/RF-chip-specific strength, while **Nvidia edged up 0.6%** as, per Motley Fool's midday coverage, "investors reassessed doomsday scenarios for artificial intelligence" — even as the broader AI-safety story (Amodei/Altman/Musk) continued to weigh on sentiment elsewhere.
- **Single-stock stories diverged sharply from the macro tone.** Earlier in the session (per TheStreet): **Etsy (ETSY) rose on an Oppenheimer upgrade**, **Enova International (ENVA) plunged 17.49%** after withdrawing regulatory applications, and **Dave & Buster's (PLAY) fell as much as 13.46%** despite beating on EPS (it missed on revenue, per Investrade). By the close, **Axon Enterprise (AXON) was the S&P's weakest large-cap, down 9.8%**, while **Goldman Sachs (GS) fell 1.2%** and **Apple (AAPL) slipped 0.5%**.
- **A late-session crypto-market shock added a second, distinct risk-off leg.** The Senate's **CLARITY Act (digital-asset market-structure legislation) failed its 60-vote cloture threshold**, sending bitcoin and crypto-adjacent equities lower into the close — a reversal from the CLARITY-optimism-driven bitcoin rally seen earlier in the Taipei-evening session (see "Bitcoin" below). **Coinbase Global (COIN) and Robinhood Markets both fell** on the news, extending Coinbase's earlier-session decline.

## Bonds and credit

- **Treasury yields**: 2-year 4.671% (52-week high), 10-year 5.002% (highest close since Oct 2007), 30-year 5.367% — a broad, curve-wide move higher one day ahead of the Fed's decision.
- **Credit spreads**: the **ICE BofA US High Yield OAS closed at 2.76%** (same-day reading), while the **ICE BofA US Investment Grade OAS stood at 0.80%** (as of Sept 14's posting — FRED's IG series typically lags by about one business day). Both remain historically tight despite the equity-market pressure, suggesting credit markets are not yet pricing meaningful default-risk stress from the yield spike.

## Gold

**Spot gold opened at $4,340.30/oz, down 0.3% from Monday's close, and slipped further to around $4,315/oz** by mid-morning — its lowest open in several weeks — pressured by the same Fed-hike repricing hitting equities (CME FedWatch showing 92.5% odds of Wednesday's hike, up from 86.5% the day before) even as the same Middle East oil-supply disruption would typically also support gold's safe-haven bid.

## Bitcoin / crypto

Bitcoin traded higher earlier in the session (Taipei-evening reads had it near $77,983, +1.6%, on CLARITY Act optimism), but that reversed sharply once the **Senate's CLARITY Act cloture vote failed to reach 60 votes**: **bitcoin closed around $75,894.44, down 1.76% on the day**, and **ethereum closed around $2,397.45, down 3.54%**. Coindesk quoted a researcher noting "the downside from a failed vote is largely priced; a surprise passage is not" — yet the actual rejection still weighed on crypto assets and crypto-adjacent equities (Coinbase, Robinhood) into the close, a reminder that even a broadly anticipated outcome can move markets on confirmation.

## Bottom line

Tuesday's session extended Monday's AI-safety-driven de-risking, but the proximate driver rotated to the bond and oil markets: a 10-year Treasury yield closing above 5% for the first time since 2007, a fresh leg higher in oil on the unresolved Saudi pipeline disruption, and Fed-hike odds climbing to 91–93% ahead of Wednesday's decision. Genuine cross-currents complicated the simple "risk-off" read — semiconductors stabilized rather than extending Monday's SOX-led rout (Skyworks +13.6%, Nvidia +0.6%), while a late-session, distinct catalyst (the CLARITY Act's cloture failure) hit crypto assets and crypto-adjacent equities on top of the broader macro pressure. With the Fed decision now less than 24 hours away, Wednesday's outcome — and any signal on whether this is a one-time hike or the start of further tightening — is the market's dominant near-term question.
