# Daily Market Briefing — September 15, 2026 (Tuesday)

Global snapshot: markets extended Monday's AI-safety-driven de-risking into a second day, but the proximate trigger shifted from Monday's direct AI-industry headlines to their knock-on effects. An overnight collapse in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (**SOX −5.86%**) fed into broad Asian tech weakness, while a fresh combination of surging oil prices (**Brent $108.75–$113/barrel**, depending on source and timing, on continued Saudi pipeline disruption) and the **10-year US Treasury yield closing at 5.002% — its highest close since 2007** — became the dominant cross-market story ahead of Wednesday's FOMC decision (a 25-basis-point hike priced at roughly **90–93% probability**). **Hong Kong (−1.00%), Singapore (−1.00%), and Malaysia (−1.11%) led regional declines**, while **Taiwan (−0.77%) and Korea (−0.85%) extended their own multi-day selloffs** (Taiwan's fourth consecutive day of foreign selling; Korea's fifth straight down day). **Japan's Nikkei 225 closed essentially flat (−0.01%)**, but only because SoftBank Group's single-stock **+8.7%** rally masked a much weaker broader market (**TOPIX −0.52%**). **Indonesia (−0.15%) was the region's most resilient market**, a reversal from Monday's cabinet-reshuffle whipsaw. **The US and Europe both closed lower once their sessions ended**: the **S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,585.73**, and Europe posted a genuinely region-wide decline (unlike Monday's FTSE-led divergence), with a late-session **CLARITY Act cloture-vote failure in the US Senate** adding a distinct, non-macro shock that hit bitcoin (−1.76%) and crypto-adjacent equities into the US close.

See `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, `uk_europe.md`, and `usa.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

## Where the money is flowing

- **Taiwan:** the TAIEX's second consecutive decline (−0.77%) was broader-based than Monday's — no institutional category turned net buyer, and foreign investors extended their selling streak to four days (cumulative ~NT$227.3 billion). TSMC's own resilience (+0.21%) against a collapsing SOX overnight is worth tracking as a genuine divergence, and the high-dividend Taiwan ETFs (00878, 00919) held up much better than large-cap-tracking 0050 — see `taiwan.md`.
- **Japan:** a single stock — SoftBank Group, +8.7% on OpenAI IPO-delay relief — single-handedly offset broader TOPIX weakness (−0.52%), a reminder of how much the price-weighted Nikkei can diverge from the underlying market on any given day. See `japan.md`.
- **Korea:** the character of the sell-off shifted from Monday's chip-specific panic (Samsung/SK Hynix) to a broader macro one — shipbuilding, insurance, and financials led Tuesday's declines while Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both stabilized. Foreign/institutional selling against retail dip-buying continued for a second day. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** reversed Monday's outperformance with a broad decline across all three major indices, banking and precious-metals names bearing the brunt while PCB and storage-chip names offered a partial AI-hardware-specific offset. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** Singapore and Malaysia fell in line with the region's broader macro story; Thailand's decline was almost entirely a single-stock story (Delta Electronics' convertible-bond overhang — "excluding DELTA's impact, the SET would have moved into positive territory"); Indonesia was the region's standout, holding a marginal −0.15% loss. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** a genuinely broader, more uniform decline than Monday's FTSE-outperformance pattern, with all four major indices closing lower — though DAX and CAC 40 both pared back sharper intraday losses as oil eased off its intraday peak and yields retreated slightly late in the session. Defense (Rheinmetall, Thales) and steel (Salzgitter) names rallied even as chemicals and banking (Deutsche Bank) lagged; Italy's FTSE MIB remained the region's weakest major index (roughly −1%) on banking-sector weakness. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **United States:** the session's dominant theme was the 10-year Treasury yield's close above 5% (highest since 2007) and oil's further climb, but two genuine divergences stood out — semiconductors stabilized rather than extending Monday's SOX-led rout (Skyworks +13.6%, Qorvo +9.3%, Nvidia +0.6%), and a late-session, distinct catalyst (the Senate's CLARITY Act cloture-vote failure) hit bitcoin and crypto-adjacent equities (Coinbase, Robinhood) on top of the broader macro pressure — a reminder that not every part of Tuesday's decline traced back to the same oil/yields/Fed story. See `usa.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** the 10-year Treasury yield's close at 5.002% (highest since 2007) and Fed-hike odds near 91–93% pressured gold to its lowest open in several weeks (~$4,315–4,340/oz) even as oil continued climbing (Brent $108.75, WTI $105.83) on the unresolved Saudi pipeline disruption. Bitcoin and ethereum, which had traded higher earlier in the session on CLARITY Act optimism, reversed sharply once the Senate vote actually failed, closing down 1.76% and 3.54% respectively — a genuine round-trip within a single session, and a reminder that crypto-native sentiment can move independently of (and sometimes faster than) the broader macro tape.

## Oil

**Brent crude traded in a wide $108.75–$113/barrel range depending on source and timing** (some physical European cargoes reportedly traded above $130/barrel intraday), and **WTI rose roughly 4–4.5% to $105.83/barrel**, both driven by the still-unresolved Saudi Arabia East-West pipeline disruption — Riyadh formally blamed Iran-backed elements in Iraq for the strike, with the disruption now estimated to affect roughly 4% of global oil consumption, compounding the weekend's Houthi-attack damage.

## Gold

**Spot gold opened at $4,340.30/oz (−0.3% from Monday's close) and slipped further to around $4,315/oz** by mid-morning — its lowest open in several weeks — pressured by the same Fed-hike repricing hitting equities (CME FedWatch showing 92.5% odds of Wednesday's hike, up from 86.5% the day before), even as the Middle East supply disruption driving oil higher would typically also support gold's own safe-haven demand.

## Bitcoin

**Bitcoin closed around $75,894.44, down 1.76% on the day, and Ethereum closed around $2,397.45, down 3.54%** — reversing an earlier-session gain (bitcoin had traded near $77,983, +1.6%, on CLARITY Act optimism) once the Senate's cloture vote on the CLARITY Act (crypto-market-structure legislation) actually failed to reach the required 60 votes. Coinbase and Robinhood both fell into the close on the same news, a reminder that even a broadly-anticipated regulatory outcome can move both crypto assets and crypto-adjacent equities on confirmation.
