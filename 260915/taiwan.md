# Taiwan Stock Market Analysis — September 15, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

The **TAIEX fell 351.03 points (−0.77%) to close at 45,511.49**, its second consecutive decline, on trading value that shrank further to roughly **NT$604.65 billion** — among the lowest of the year — as investors grew increasingly risk-averse heading into Wednesday's FOMC decision. **Declining issues outnumbered advancers by roughly 3-to-1 (about 1,360 decliners vs. 442 advancers)**, and the top ten most-traded stocks all closed lower — a sign of broader-based selling than Monday's more concentrated decline.

## What drove the decline

- **An overnight collapse in US semiconductor stocks set the tone.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) plunged **5.86%** in Monday's US session (see `../260915/usa.md`), as the weekend AI-safety story continued to weigh on chip-sector sentiment and the 10-year Treasury yield pushed to **5.02% — its highest since 2007** — ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision.
- **TSMC (2330) was a rare bright spot**, rising NT$5 to close at **NT$2,385 (+0.21%)**, but couldn't offset broad weakness elsewhere: **MediaTek fell 2.85%, UMC (2303) dropped 2.81%, and AUO (2409) fell 2.79%**.
- **AI-supply-chain and thermal-management names were hit hardest**: **Chaun-Choung Technology (健策, 3653) plunged 8.76%**, **Auras Technology (奇鋐, 3017) fell 5.89%**, **Global Unichip (創意, 3443) dropped 5.7%**, and **Elite Material (台光電, 2383) fell 4.3%** — a sharp reversal for a group that had been among the market's AI-driven winners in prior sessions.
- **Powerchip Technology (力積電, 6770) sank 5.37%** on renewed memory-sector weakness, and **actively managed Taiwan-equity ETFs (00981A, 00403A, 00406A) all declined between 1.2% and 1.6%**, underscoring the broad-based nature of the pullback.

## Bottom line

Tuesday's −0.77% TAIEX decline was broader-based than Monday's, with no institutional category turning net buyer and the top-10 most-traded stocks all closing lower — a genuine continuation of AI-safety-driven de-risking rather than a one-day reaction. The proximate trigger shifted from Monday's direct AI-safety headlines to their knock-on effect on Wall Street's semiconductor complex (SOX −5.86% overnight), with foreign investors extending their selling streak to a fourth day and short positions climbing to a fresh high. TSMC's own resilience is worth watching closely: if it holds up while the rest of the complex remains under pressure, it would suggest investors are differentiating between AI-leader TSMC and its more cyclically exposed supply-chain peers. The Taiwan ETF data confirms this same divide at the fund level — large-cap-tracking 0050 fell in line with the index while the high-dividend, financials-tilted 00878/00919 again proved more resilient. Wednesday's FOMC decision is now the market's next major catalyst.
