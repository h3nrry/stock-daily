# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 15, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia fell across the board Tuesday, a more uniform decline than Monday's mixed session: **Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand all closed lower** on the same combination of surging oil prices, a 10-year Treasury yield at its highest since 2007, and caution ahead of Wednesday's FOMC decision, while **Indonesia's IHSG held up comparatively well** with only a marginal loss.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index fell roughly 1% to close at 5,660.75**, down from Monday's confirmed 5,718.02 close — reversing Monday's regional outperformance as the same oil-and-yields combination pressuring every market this project covers today caught up with Singapore's bank-heavy index. Same-day sector/stock-level detail beyond the headline index move was not available from sources checked at writing time.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,534.69, down just 0.15%** — a comparatively resilient session despite the same global headwinds pressuring the rest of the region. Stock-level activity was concentrated in small-caps: **PT Alakasa Industrindo (ALKA) surged 18.88%**, **PT Asuransi Harta Aman Pratama (AHAP) jumped 14.56%**, and **PT Asia Sejahtera Mina (AGAR) rose 13.69%**, while **PT Bank Negara Indonesia (BBNI) fell 3.10%**, among the session's larger-cap decliners.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI fell 18.80 points (−1.11%) to close at 1,679.21**, giving back all of Monday's +0.66% rebound and then some. **Decliners outnumbered advancers 780-to-355**, on turnover of 3.34 billion units valued at RM3.25 billion. **United Plantations (+50 sen to RM33.60)** and **MISC (+14 sen to RM7.95)** bucked the trend, while **Nestlé (Malaysia) fell RM1.12 to RM90.18** and **Malaysian Pacific Industries dropped 30 sen to RM39.70**. Analysts flagged the index as approaching oversold territory, with the 1,670–1,700 range seen as the likely near-term consolidation zone.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index fell 12.41 points (−0.78%) to close at 1,578.66**, on trading value of **฿75.79 billion**, after swinging between 1,577.20 and 1,592.28 intraday. The decline was concentrated almost entirely in one stock: **Delta Electronics (Thailand) came under heavy selling pressure** — including block trades totaling 2.65 million shares — after its Taiwanese parent company issued $1.5 billion in exchangeable bonds with conversion prices well above the current share price, creating negative near-term psychology. Analyst Natthapol Khamthakhrua noted that **"excluding DELTA's impact, the SET would have moved into positive territory,"** as energy and petrochemical stocks gained on rising oil prices. Foreign investors were only modest net sellers, at ฿66.95 million.

## Bottom line

Tuesday marked a genuine shift for Southeast Asia: where Monday's session was defined by domestic-specific catalysts (Indonesia's cabinet reshuffle, Malaysia's banking-led rebound), Tuesday's declines across Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand trace back almost entirely to the same global macro backdrop hitting every market this project covers — oil above $107, the 10-year Treasury yield at its highest since 2007, and pre-FOMC caution. Thailand's session is a useful reminder that even a broadly negative macro day can have a single-stock story (Delta Electronics' convertible-bond overhang) as its largest contributor. Indonesia's relative resilience (−0.15%) stands out as the region's best performer today, a reversal from being whipsawed by domestic politics on Monday.
