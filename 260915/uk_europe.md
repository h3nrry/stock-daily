# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 15, 2026 (Tuesday)

## Headline

European markets fell broadly for a second straight session — a genuinely region-wide decline, unlike Monday's FTSE-led divergence — as oil's climb and Treasury yields above 5% overwhelmed European equities ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision. **FTSE 100 closed at 10,658.13, down 0.38%**, **Germany's DAX closed at 25,402.28, down 0.15%**, **France's CAC 40 closed around 8,090 (down roughly 0.76%)**, and the pan-European **STOXX 600 closed at 634.18 (down roughly 0.56%)** — all figures recomputed against this project's own confirmed Monday closes (FTSE 10,698.90; DAX 25,440.81; CAC 8,151.98; STOXX 600 637.72; see `../260914/uk_europe.md`) given the persistent vendor-to-vendor divergence this project has repeatedly documented for European index prior-close series (see "Sourcing notes" below).

## What drove the session

- **Oil extended its climb further**, with Brent crude reported as high as **$113/barrel intraday** by one wire (ADVFN) before easing back toward **$108/barrel** by the close (Moneyvox), still tracing back to Riyadh's formal accusation that Iran-backed elements in Iraq struck Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — a disruption now estimated to affect roughly 4% of global oil supply, compounding the weekend's Houthi-attack damage. Energy names benefited broadly across the region.
- **The 10-year US Treasury yield closed at 5.002%** (having peaked intraday near 5.03–5.04%, its highest since 2007), with markets pricing a **90% probability of a 25-basis-point Fed hike** at Wednesday's decision — a level that pressured European equity valuations even though the ECB, at 2.50%, is not the immediate rate-setter in question.
- **A late-session recovery partly offset the day's worst levels.** Germany's DAX "fell to its lowest level in more than seven weeks" intraday before a "slight easing in oil prices and bond yields" helped it stabilize into the close (per Ariva.de) — a pattern echoed by the CAC 40, which Moneyvox described as having "limited the damage" as oil eased from its intraday peak and the 10-year yield retreated slightly from its own 5.03% high. This intraday-recovery dynamic likely explains why an earlier Investing.com wire (ca.investing.com), evidently filed before the recovery, showed sharper declines (DAX −0.95%, FTSE/CAC both around −1.03%, STOXX 600 −0.40%) than the closing figures above.
- **Sector divergence within the DAX was notable**: **chemical names extended their slide** (JPMorgan cut Lanxess's price target on energy-cost headwinds; Morgan Stanley downgraded Evonik), and **Deutsche Bank fell 1.9%**, the DAX's weakest component, pressured by weak read-through from Bank of America's own guidance in the US. Offsetting this, **defense stocks rallied on fresh supply-chain and demand signals**: **Rheinmetall gained 2.4%** in Frankfurt and **Thales surged 4.12%** in Paris after the Pentagon acknowledged depleted US munitions stockpiles from Iran-related operations, while **Salzgitter jumped 5.2%** in Frankfurt on Chinese steelmakers' announced production restrictions.
- **Italy's FTSE MIB remained the region's weakest major index**, down close to 1% at last read, with banking-sector weakness flagged as the specific driver — consistent with the broader European banking pressure visible in Deutsche Bank's decline.

## Bottom line

Tuesday's session confirmed Monday's FTSE-led divergence was the exception, not the new pattern — every major European index fell today on the same macro combination (oil above $108-113, the 10-year Treasury above 5%, and pre-FOMC positioning) hitting every market this project covers. The session's more interesting story was the intraday recovery: DAX and CAC 40 both pared back sharper early losses as oil eased off its intraday peak and yields retreated slightly from their own highs, a pattern worth watching into Wednesday's FOMC decision itself, when a "hike now priced in, no surprise" outcome could trigger a larger relief rally than Tuesday's modest stabilization. Sector-level divergence (defense/steel gains vs. chemicals/banking weakness) is also worth tracking independently of the headline index moves.
