# Daily Market Briefing — September 2, 2026 (Wednesday)

Global snapshot: the Iran-driven oil shock that has run through every session since Monday deepened sharply overnight after **President Trump threatened stronger US retaliation if Iran responds to the American airstrikes already carried out**, and Iran itself responded Wednesday morning with **rocket and drone attacks on US military bases** — a genuine escalation that sent **Brent crude above $95/barrel** and pushed the **US 10-year Treasury yield to an intraday high near 4.81–4.82%, its highest in nearly three years**. Asia-Pacific markets bore the brunt: **Korea's KOSPI plunged −3.99% to 6,562.72** (its worst session in weeks, on ₩4.35 trillion of combined foreign/institutional selling), **Japan's Nikkei fell −2.85% to −2.96% to 64,325.64** (all 33 TSE sectors lower), and **Taiwan's TAIEX gave back the bulk of Tuesday's NVIDIA/MediaTek rally, falling −1.67% to 46,164.72** on NT$115 billion of net institutional selling. **Hong Kong was the standout exception — the Hang Seng closed essentially flat (−0.07%)**, propped up by domestic bank strength (China Bank hit a new high) even as its Tech sub-index fell harder (−0.74%, −2% intraday). Southeast Asia was comparatively calm: **Indonesia's IHSG was essentially flat (−0.06%)** with a constructive sector mix, and **Thailand's SET eased a modest −0.86%**; Singapore and Malaysia's same-day closes were not confirmed at writing time. **By the time the US and Europe sessions closed, the picture had turned — both regions actually finished higher/milder than Asia's rout suggested**: US indices **snapped a three-day losing streak** (Dow +0.56%, S&P 500 +0.46%, Nasdaq +0.45%) after NY Fed President John Williams reframed the yield spike as a strong-growth story rather than an inflation one, letting the 10-year ease back to 4.79% off its overnight high; Europe closed lower for a third straight session but with milder losses than Tuesday (DAX −0.50%, FTSE −0.30%, CAC 40 −0.26%, STOXX 600 −0.24%), as the same oil/yield pressure stopped accelerating rather than resolving.

**Reading the table:** with the full day now on the board, today's clearest story is a genuine split between Asia and the rest of the world — Korea and Japan both fell sharply and broadly, and Taiwan reversed the bulk of a single-stock-driven rally, while only Hong Kong (on domestic bank strength) and Indonesia/Thailand (comparatively insulated) bucked the Asian trend. The US and Europe, which were still trading when this Asia-heavy picture first took shape, ended up telling a different story entirely: US equities actually snapped their three-day losing streak once a Fed official reframed the yield spike as growth-driven rather than inflation-driven, and Europe's losses, while still present for a third straight session, were roughly half the size of Tuesday's. The net effect is a genuinely fractured global session — nowhere near the "everything sells off together" pattern of Monday and Tuesday — even though the underlying Iran/oil/yield driver never actually resolved.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** a broad-based bounce that snapped a three-session losing streak — Dow +0.56%, S&P 500 +0.46%, Nasdaq +0.45%, with the Russell 2000 outperforming sharply (+1.13%). The catalyst was NY Fed President John Williams reframing the 10-year yield's push toward a fresh multi-year high (~4.81–4.82% intraday) as reflecting AI/data-center-driven growth rather than inflation, letting yields ease back to 4.79% by the close. A soft ADP report (+38k jobs in August, vs. 47k expected, the slowest since January) reinforced the "growth intact, Fed has room" narrative. Materials, Communications Services, and Financials led; Technology and Energy lagged, with MongoDB and Palo Alto Networks extending Tuesday's software-sector weakness even as the index rallied. Credit spreads ticked up marginally (IG 0.81%, HY 2.66%) but remain historically tight. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** a sharp reversal of Tuesday's NVIDIA/MediaTek-driven rally — TAIEX −1.67% on NT$115.03B of net institutional selling (foreign investors alone: −NT$91.36B), with TSMC (−2.05%) and Delta Electronics (−6.17%) doing the most index-level damage. MediaTek was the lone large-cap gainer (+0.35%), still supported by Tuesday's NVIDIA deal news. Foreign investors also raised bearish TAIEX futures positioning. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** the region's worst session — KOSPI −3.99% on ₩4.35 trillion of combined foreign (−₩1.9T) and institutional (−₩2.43T) selling, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix reversing Tuesday's buyback-driven strength. Individual investors were the lone net buyers, absorbing the institutional exodus. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** the region's standout — Hang Seng −0.07% (recovering from a nearly −1% intraday low) as domestic banks, led by a new high in China Bank, propped up the index; Hang Seng Tech fell a steeper −0.74% (−2% intraday), a more honest read on underlying risk appetite. Brokerage stocks (CICC −7%+) were the session's clear losers. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** comparatively insulated — Indonesia's IHSG essentially flat (−0.06%) with consumer/tech/financials sectors actually higher, and Thailand's SET down a modest −0.86% on post-earnings-season profit-taking and Middle East-driven portfolio de-risking. Singapore and Malaysia same-day closes not confirmed at writing time. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Japan:** a broad, sector-agnostic selloff — Nikkei −2.85% to −2.96%, all 33 TSE sector indices lower, decliners outnumbering advancers nearly 6-to-1. Sumitomo Metal Mining, Kyowa Kirin, and Mitsubishi Materials were the session's biggest single-stock losers. See `japan.md`.
- **Europe:** a third straight down session, but a visibly milder one than Tuesday — DAX −0.50%, FTSE −0.30%, CAC 40 −0.26%, STOXX 600 −0.24% — as Eurozone bond yields extended their climb on rising odds of a 25bp ECB rate hike at the September 10 meeting, while oil's rally (Brent ~$95) stopped accelerating rather than reversing. Stock-level movers included Halma (up, on a $170M US water-quality acquisition) and Wizz Air (up, on August operating data), against Prudential (down despite a $1.47B buyback) and BP (down, on a new-chair appointment). France's CAC 40 carries the steepest 20-day decline in the region (−4.48%), a story tied to its own September 8 confidence-vote overhang as much as the shared Iran/oil pressure. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** **oil stayed elevated but stopped accelerating** — Brent traded near $95.63/barrel (+1.0%) and WTI near $91.01 (+0.9%), still reflecting the unresolved Strait-of-Hormuz conflict but no longer driving the outsized daily moves seen Monday and Tuesday. **Gold edged higher** (December futures ~$4,414.60, +0.4%) even as equities rallied, a sign safe-haven demand hasn't fully unwound despite the day's risk-on tone in US/European equities. **Bitcoin extended its pullback, falling to roughly $77,000 (−1.4%)** — crypto continuing to trade as the most rate-sensitive of the major risk assets covered here, unable to catch the same bid that helped stocks recover.
