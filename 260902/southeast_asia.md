# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 2, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia's reaction to today's Iran-driven oil shock and global bond selloff was notably muted compared with the sharp declines in Korea (−3.99%) and Japan (−2.85% to −2.96%) — Indonesia was essentially flat and Thailand posted only a modest decline, while confirmed same-day closing data for Singapore and Malaysia was not available from sources checked at writing time.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

**The IHSG closed at 6,595.78, down just 4.16 points (−0.06%)** — a genuinely flat session that stands out against the region's broader risk-off tone today. Six of eleven sectors actually finished positive: **consumer non-primary led gains at +1.75%**, followed by **technology (+0.85%)** and **financials (+0.77%)**, with DCI Indonesia, Global Digital Niaga, and Bank Central Asia the key supporting stocks. On the negative side, **raw materials fell −1.45%** and **utilities declined −1.31%**, dragged down by Bayan Resources, Amman Mineral International, and Astra International. Roughly Rp16.80 trillion changed hands (296 advancers, 338 decliners, 329 unchanged), and foreign investors recorded a comparatively modest Rp379.67 billion net sell in the morning session.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,575.07, down 13.61 points (−0.86%)** on THB 64.33 billion in trading value. The decline was attributed to "selling pressure from big-caps in various sectors" following the end of earnings season without fresh catalysts, compounded by foreign and institutional investors "revising their portfolios to avoid risks from the Middle East conflict and U.S. interest rates" — the same Iran/oil/yield thread running through every market in this project today.

## Singapore — STI

**Not confirmed at writing time.** Sources checked showed only Tuesday, September 1's confirmed close (5,710.37, −0.78%); no same-day September 2 closing figure was available. Given the region's generally muted reaction today (Indonesia flat, Thailand only modestly lower), a sharp STI decline seems unlikely, but this should be treated as an open item rather than assumed.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

**Not confirmed at writing time.** Sources checked showed only Tuesday, September 1's confirmed close (1,700.54, −1.47%, a steeper decline than most regional peers that day); no same-day September 2 closing figure was available.

## Bottom line

A genuinely muted day for Southeast Asia relative to the sharper declines seen in Korea, Japan, and Taiwan — Indonesia was essentially flat with a constructive sector mix (consumer, tech, and financials all higher), and Thailand's −0.86% decline was modest by comparison. Singapore and Malaysia should be checked against a later data pull once confirmed closes are available, consistent with this project's practice of flagging gaps rather than guessing (see `../260901/README.md`'s Malaysia footnote for a similar precedent).
