# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 2, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

Japan's Nikkei 225 fell sharply, joining the region-wide selloff triggered by the Iran-driven oil spike and surging global bond yields. The index **closed at 64,325.64, down ¥1,889.70 (−2.85% to −2.96%, depending on source)** — one of its worse sessions in weeks — with **all 33 TSE sector indices closing lower** and declining stocks outnumbering advancers by a lopsided **3,047 to 513** on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

## What drove the decline

- **The same global thread hitting every market this week**: renewed US-Iran fighting sent Brent crude toward $95/barrel and pushed the US 10-year Treasury yield to roughly 4.79–4.81%, its highest level in nearly three years — reviving fears the Fed (and, by extension, global central banks) will need to stay restrictive for longer.
- **Paper & Pulp, Transport, and Communication sectors led losses**, per sector-level TSE data, though the breadth of the decline (all 33 sectors lower) points to a broad macro risk-off move rather than a sector-specific story.
- **Biggest single-stock losers**: **Sumitomo Metal Mining (−11.55%)**, **Kyowa Kirin (−11.05%)**, and **Mitsubishi Materials (−9.80%)** — all suffering outsized declines well beyond the index average.
- **A domestic overlay**: Japanese commentary (Nikkei) framed the session partly around global rate pressure and a market "bracing for a 3% rate era" (「金利3%時代」に身構え) — a reminder that Japan's own slow move away from ultra-low rates adds a domestic layer to the global yield story.

## Bottom line

Japan's selloff was not a domestic story — it's the same Iran/oil/yield thread running through every market covered in this project today (see `taiwan.md`, `korea.md`, `../260901/usa.md`), amplified by Japan's own sensitivity to a rising global and domestic rate environment. The breadth of the decline (all 33 sectors, nearly 6-to-1 decliners-to-advancers) is the clearest signal this was a macro, not stock-specific, event.
