# Korea Stock Market Analysis — September 2, 2026 (Wednesday)

## Headline

Korea had the worst session of any major market covered in this project today. The **KOSPI plunged 273.08 points (−3.99%) to close at 6,562.72**, dropping below the 6,600 level, while the **KOSDAQ fell 17.27 points (−2.10%) to 803.98**. Foreign and institutional investors combined net-sold roughly **₩4.35 trillion** on the KOSPI — a scale of selling far larger than Tuesday's already-cautious flows (see `../260901/korea.md`).

## What drove the drop

- **The same Iran/oil/global-yield shock hitting every market this week**: renewed US-Iran military conflict pushed crude oil sharply higher and drove the US 10-year Treasury yield to roughly 4.79–4.81%, its highest in nearly three years — directly reviving fears of prolonged monetary tightening.
- **Semiconductor giants led the decline**: **Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both suffered significant losses**, a sharp reversal from Tuesday's buyback-driven strength in the same two names.
- **Broad-based selling beyond tech**: major financial, automotive, and battery-sector names also declined broadly as both foreign and institutional investors retreated across the board — not a single-sector story.
- Korean commentary framed it directly: **"global raw material price spikes and prolonged monetary tightening concerns rapidly dampened investor sentiment."**

## Bottom line

A genuinely sharp reversal from Tuesday's buyback-propped +0.23% gain — today's −3.99% KOSPI decline was broad-based, foreign/institution-led, and directly tied to the same Iran/oil/yield shock hitting every market in this project today (see `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, and yesterday's confirmed US/Europe closes in `../260901/`). With Samsung and SK Hynix reversing their buyback-driven Tuesday strength, worth watching whether the buyback support that held up Tuesday's session returns, or whether today's scale of institutional selling (₩4.35T) marks a more durable shift in risk appetite.
