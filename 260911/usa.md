# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 11, 2026 (Friday)

## Headline

US markets snapped a four-day losing streak as oil prices retreated sharply from their overnight Strait-of-Hormuz-driven spike and investors looked past a hotter-than-expected core CPI reading. The **Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 509.19 points (+0.98%) to close at 52,573.29**, the **S&P 500 rose 65.28 points (+0.86%) to 7,656.98**, and the **Nasdaq Composite rose 251.31 points (+0.96%) to 26,333.04** — figures that reconcile almost exactly against Thursday's confirmed close (see `../260910/usa.md`), per Yahoo Finance, TheStreet, and stockmarketwatch.com, which independently agreed on all three closing levels. (A separate Investrade figure of 52,657/7,656/26,333 diverges slightly on the Dow specifically — noted here per this project's established divergence-flagging convention, though the difference is minor.) Despite Friday's gains, **all three indices still finished the week lower**, with the Dow posting its worst week since March.

## What drove the market

- **August CPI landed largely as expected on the headline but hot on core**: consumer prices rose **+0.4% month-over-month and +3.4% year-over-year (in line with the FactSet-polled consensus of 3.3–3.4%)**, while **core CPI (ex-food-and-energy) rose +0.3% m/m — a tenth of a point above the 0.2% forecast**, holding the annual core rate at **2.4%**. The hotter core print pushed the probability of a rate hike at next week's **September 15–16 FOMC meeting to roughly 87–90% per CME FedWatch**, up sharply from about 70% just the day before. Analyst Adam Crisafulli described the data as "still hot" but sufficient justification for a hike rather than a reason for panic — a framing markets appeared to share, given the day's rally.
- **Oil reversed sharply from its overnight peak**: after Brent crude had spiked as high as **$109–110/barrel intraday during Asian hours** on a fresh escalation in the US-Iran Strait of Hormuz standoff, it retreated through the US session to close near **$104.61/barrel (down 2.8–3.6% on the day, sources range from −2.81% to −3.61%)**; **WTI settled near $100.05 (down roughly 2.4%)**. The pullback provided genuine relief after a week in which the same story had pushed Brent above $107 (Thursday) and toward $109+ (Friday's overnight high) — even so, **diesel prices set a fresh record above $6.00–6.05/gallon nationally**, underscoring the conflict's continuing real-economy bite.
- **US Treasury yields stayed near multi-year highs across the curve, with multiple 52-week highs recorded intraday**: the **2-year traded around 4.63–4.65%**, the **10-year briefly approached 5% (reported between 4.959% and 4.974%) before retreating**, and the **30-year held near 5.36–5.38%** — all extending Thursday's already-elevated closes (2Y 4.567%, 10Y 4.954%, 30Y 5.367%).
- **The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) surged 1.81% to 11,824.0**, nearly double the Nasdaq Composite's own 0.96% gain — a continuation of the SOX/broad-index divergence pattern seen repeatedly this month, this time driven by **Oracle's blowout fiscal Q1 report** (cloud infrastructure revenue of $7.4 billion, beating the $7.19 billion estimate, with cloud growth accelerating to 62% year-over-year) after the Thursday-evening close, which fed straight into **Dell (+approximately 10%) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (+9%)** on renewed AI-infrastructure-spending optimism.

## Notable stock stories

- **Oracle (ORCL)** gained more than 4% in extended trading after its cloud infrastructure sales beat estimates ($7.4B vs. $7.19B expected) with 62% cloud growth — the session's single biggest catalyst for the broader AI-infrastructure trade.
- **Dell Technologies** and **Hewlett Packard Enterprise** advanced roughly 10% and 9% respectively, riding Oracle's positive read-through for AI data-center demand.
- **Adobe** declined despite reporting a solid quarter, a reminder that "beat and decline" reactions remained a feature of this earnings season.
- **GameStop (GME)**: CEO Ryan Cohen purchased 1 million shares, bringing his total ownership to 39.3 million shares.
- **SpaceX** gained on a reported $1.1 billion-per-month computing deal.
- Sector breadth was broad-based on the rally: printed-circuit-board and wholesale-computer names were among the session's top-performing groups, tracking the same AI-infrastructure enthusiasm as Oracle/Dell/HPE.

## Treasury yields

**2-year ~4.63–4.65%. 10-year briefly approached 5% (4.959% per TheStreet, 4.974% per Investrade) before retreating. 30-year ~5.36–5.38%.** Trading Economics separately reported the 10-year at 4.97% as of Friday, "scaling its highest levels since October 2023." All three legs extend Thursday's already-elevated closes (2Y 4.567%, 10Y 4.954%, 30Y 5.367%), with intraday trading touching multiple 52-week highs across the curve before easing modestly into the close.

## Credit spreads

FRED's **ICE BofA US High Yield OAS ticked up to 2.70% (dated September 10)**, from 2.67% the prior reading, while the **Investment-Grade OAS series ticked down slightly to 0.80% (also dated September 10)**, from 0.81%. Both moves are minor and stay within the historically tight range seen all week — credit markets continue to read this week's action as a commodity/rate story rather than a credit-quality event, consistent with every prior session this week.

## Oil, gold, bitcoin

- **Oil**: Brent crude eased sharply from its overnight Asian-hours peak above **$109–110/barrel** to close near **$104.61/barrel (down 2.8–3.6% on the day)**; **WTI settled near $100.05 (down roughly 2.4%)** — still well above pre-escalation levels from early September, but a genuine relief session after the week's steady climb. Diesel hit a fresh record above $6.00/gallon nationally.
- **Gold**: sources diverge meaningfully this session — **Investrade reported $4,408.90 (+$1.60)**, a figure that reconciles almost exactly against Thursday's confirmed $4,407.30 close, while **USAGOLD reported a same-day close of $4,385.61 (+$2.61, +0.06%)** on a different reference base. Both agree gold was essentially flat to very slightly higher on the day, holding near the $4,380–4,410 range as the hot core CPI print's upward pressure on rate expectations offset its usual inflation-hedge appeal. **Silver fell sharply, down 2.58% to roughly $64.90** per USAGOLD (roughly flat against Thursday's $64.93 close on a level basis, despite the outlet's own reported percentage implying a materially higher prior-day reference — another same-session sourcing divergence, flagged rather than resolved). The gold-to-silver ratio widened to roughly 67.6, reflecting silver's greater sensitivity to Fed-policy repricing.
- **Bitcoin**: opened the day at $76,535.95 (down 2.2% from Thursday) and briefly traded below $77,000 in early New York hours amid pre-CPI caution, before recovering through the session to close near **$77,308.97 (+$309.70, +0.40%)** per Investrade — a V-shaped session that mirrored equities' own recovery from early softness. **Ethereum** traded in a similar range, opening near $2,437.02 (down 1.2%) before firming to around $2,453.68.

## Bottom line

A genuine, broad-based relief rally that snapped a four-day losing streak — all three major indices gained roughly 0.9–1.0% as oil's sharp overnight-to-close reversal (from a fresh Strait-of-Hormuz-driven spike above $109 back to roughly $104.61) outweighed a hotter-than-expected core CPI print that pushed Fed-hike odds for next week's FOMC to 87–90%. The clearest beneath-the-surface story was Oracle's blowout cloud-infrastructure results spilling into a broader AI-infrastructure rally (Dell +~10%, HPE +9%, SOX +1.81% — nearly double the Nasdaq's own gain), even as GameStop's Ryan Cohen and SpaceX's own AI-computing deal added incremental single-stock color. Treasury yields stayed pinned near multi-year highs across the curve, and credit spreads remained historically tight, continuing to read the week's turbulence as a commodity/rate story rather than a credit event. Despite Friday's bounce, all three indices closed lower for the week — the Dow's worst since March — leaving next week's FOMC decision as the dominant remaining catalyst.
