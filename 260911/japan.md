# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 11, 2026 (Friday)

## Headline

Japan's Nikkei 225 reversed hard, giving back Thursday's dramatic rebound and then some as the same oil-and-yields story hitting nearly every market this week hit Tokyo squarely. The index **closed at 64,011.34, down 1,259.61 points (−1.93%)**, after plunging as much as **roughly 2,000–2,134 points intraday** — briefly touching territory well below 63,000 — before paring about a third of that loss into the close. The **TOPIX fell a much milder 26.28 points (−0.65%) to 4,028.30**, underscoring how concentrated Friday's decline was in a handful of large-cap names. The close reconciles exactly against Thursday's confirmed 65,270.95 (see `../260910/japan.md`).

## What drove the decline

- **Oil's latest leg higher and US Treasury yields nearing 5%** were the dominant global drivers (see `usa.md`, `hongkong.md`): Brent crude pushed above $109/barrel intraday on continued Strait of Hormuz escalation, and the US 10-year yield approached its highest level in nearly three years — both feeding into hotter Fed-hike expectations ahead of tonight's US CPI report and next week's FOMC meeting.
- **The decline was unusually concentrated in one stock**: semiconductor-testing equipment maker **Advantest alone accounted for roughly 617 points of the index's drop** — the single largest drag by a wide margin — with **SoftBank Group** the second-largest negative contributor, followed by **Tokyo Electron, Kioxia Holdings, and Ibiden**. Notably, SoftBank and Advantest were the two stocks that drove nearly all of September 4's rally (see `../260904/japan.md`) — Friday's session reads as a sharp unwind of that same narrow, semiconductor-concentrated trade.
- **USD/JPY weakened toward the mid-154 range** (from Thursday's ~153.4), testing resistance near 154.60–155 on dollar strength tied to the US rate story, even as a disappointing US long-bond auction and hot US Producer Price Index data (+5.4% y/y) added further upward pressure on yields overnight.
- **Sector detail was broadly negative**: non-ferrous metals, electrical machinery, and precision equipment led declines; mining, shipping, and insurance were the only sectors to post gains, and only modestly.
- **The partial recovery off the intraday low** (roughly a third of the ~2,000-point plunge clawed back) mirrors Thursday's own V-shaped pattern, though Friday's rebound was far less complete — a genuinely two-sided week for Japanese equities overall.

## Money flow — 外国人 (foreign investors)

Same-day foreign-investor flow data via JPX's weekly investor-type statistics was not available at writing time, as usual for this file (JPX publishes weekly, not daily). Friday's price action — a sharp, semiconductor-concentrated reversal of Thursday's own narrow SoftBank/Advantest-driven rally — reads as a rapid unwind of very recent positioning in a handful of names rather than a broad-based shift in foreign sentiment, though this cannot be confirmed until JPX's next weekly release.

## Bottom line

A sharp reversal of Thursday's rebound — Nikkei −1.93% to 64,011.34 after an intraday plunge of roughly 2,000 points — driven by the same oil-and-yields story hitting markets globally, but unusually concentrated in a single stock: Advantest's roughly 617-point drag alone accounted for about half of Friday's net decline, directly unwinding the narrow SoftBank/Advantest rally that drove September 4's gain. A weakening yen (testing 154.60–155 against the dollar) even as equities fell underscores how firmly the US rate story is now driving cross-asset moves. With tonight's US CPI report and next week's FOMC meeting both looming, whether Friday's partial intraday recovery holds into Monday is the clearest thing to watch.
