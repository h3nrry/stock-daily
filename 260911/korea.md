# Korea Stock Market Analysis — September 11, 2026 (Friday)

## Headline

Korea's KOSPI fell sharply as surging oil prices and elevated US interest rates hit growth stocks hard, though the index clawed back roughly a third of a much deeper intraday loss into the close. The index **closed at 6,909.91, down 124.01 points (−1.76%)**, having been down as much as **−2.44% (6,862.51) as of 1:00pm** before paring losses. The **KOSDAQ fell 16.28 points (−1.95%) to 820.64**. The close reconciles exactly against Thursday's confirmed 7,033.92 (see `../260910/korea.md`).

## What drove the decline

- **International crude prices surged past $100/barrel amid intensifying Strait of Hormuz tensions**, and **US Treasury yields extended their climb**, directly pressuring growth and technology names — the same global backdrop driving nearly every market covered this week (see `usa.md`, `japan.md`).
- **Semiconductor giants led the decline**: **Samsung Electronics fell 3.53%** and **SK Hynix fell 2.21%** by the close (having been down as sharply as −4.09% and −3.72% respectively at the 1:00pm intraday check), while **SK Square dropped 4.05%**, the day's steepest large-cap decline.
- **Sector losses were broad**: electronics (−2.66%), manufacturing (−2.21%), and medical/precision equipment (−2.18%) led declines; **only KB Financial posted a gain, up 2.60%**.
- **Daishin Securities analyst Lee Kyung-min (이경민)** attributed the weakness directly to "surging international oil prices and U.S. Treasury yields," noting that "market anxiety heightened over producer-price inflation" pressured growth stocks in particular — a direct read-through from Thursday's hot US PPI print (see `../260910/usa.md`).
- **The won weakened alongside stocks**, moving to **1,345.9 per dollar (+6.7 won, i.e. depreciating)** as of 3:30pm — a reversal from Thursday's pattern, when the won had strengthened even as equities fell.

Unlike Thursday, when retail investors alone absorbed nearly all of a heavy foreign-selling day, Friday saw **both foreign and institutional investors selling in tandem**, with retail buying only partially offsetting the combined outflow — the clearest reason the index fell more sharply (−1.76%) than Thursday's more contained (−0.25%) decline despite roughly comparable foreign selling. On the KOSDAQ, retail bought a net ₩511.6 billion while foreign and institutional investors sold ₩312 billion and ₩215.9 billion respectively.

## Bottom line

A sharper and more broadly institutional decline than Thursday's — KOSPI −1.76% to 6,909.91 as both foreign (−₩2.29 trillion) and institutional (−₩1.22 trillion) investors sold in tandem for the first time this week, with retail buying (+₩1.87 trillion) only partially cushioning the combined outflow. Semiconductor names led the slide (Samsung −3.53%, SK Hynix −2.21%) directly tracking the same oil-price surge and Treasury-yield climb pressuring growth stocks globally, while a weakening won reversed Thursday's more unusual currency-equity divergence. The roughly one-third recovery off the session's 1:00pm low (−2.44%) suggests some late dip-buying, but with tonight's US CPI report and next week's FOMC meeting both still ahead, whether 6,900 holds is the clearest thing to watch into next week.
