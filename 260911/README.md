# Daily Market Briefing — September 11, 2026 (Friday)

Global snapshot: Friday closed out a week dominated by the escalating US-Iran Strait of Hormuz conflict with a genuine East/West split. Every fully-closed Asia-Pacific market fell sharply during the daytime session as Brent crude spiked to a fresh escalation-high above $109/barrel intraday and US Treasury yields pushed toward 5% ahead of the evening's US CPI report: **Taiwan's TAIEX fell 1.61%** to 46,184.85 on the heaviest foreign-investor net-selling day (−NT$89.27 billion) of recent weeks, **Korea's KOSPI fell 1.76%** to 6,909.91 as foreign and institutional investors sold in tandem for the first time this week, and **Japan's Nikkei fell 1.93%** to 64,011.34, with Advantest alone responsible for roughly 617 of the index's 1,259.61-point drop. **Hong Kong and Southeast Asia pared back much of their own morning losses by the close**: the **Hang Seng fell just 0.60%** to 24,805.63 (versus roughly −1% intraday), Indonesia's IHSG closed down 0.73% (versus −1.8% at the open), and Malaysia's FBM KLCI closed down 1.10% after touching a two-month low intraday — only Singapore's STI actually finished higher (+0.06% recomputed), while Thailand's SET slipped a modest 0.65%. By the time Europe and the US opened, the backdrop had shifted decisively: **oil reversed hard from its overnight peak**, closing near $104.61/barrel (Brent, −2.8% to −3.6%), and the evening's **US CPI report came in largely as expected on the headline (+0.4% m/m/+3.4% y/y) but hot on core (+0.3% m/m vs. 0.2% forecast)**, pushing Fed-hike odds for next week's September 15–16 FOMC to roughly 87–90%. Rather than triggering fresh selling, the combination of retreating oil and a UK GDP beat drove **broad gains across Europe** (DAX +0.82%, CAC 40 +0.78%, FTSE +0.15% recomputed) and a **relief rally on Wall Street** that snapped a four-day US losing streak (Dow +0.98%, S&P 500 +0.86%, Nasdaq +0.96%) — though all three US indices, like most of Europe, still finished the week lower.

**Reading the table:** Friday told a genuine two-part story. During the Asia-Pacific daytime session, oil's spike to a fresh escalation-high above $109/barrel and Treasury yields pushing toward 5% drove broad declines everywhere — but Hong Kong and most of Southeast Asia clawed back a large share of their morning losses by the close (Hang Seng from roughly −1% to −0.60%; Indonesia from −1.8% to −0.73%), while Taiwan, Korea, and Japan — whose sessions closed earlier in the day — locked in their full declines. By the time the evening's US CPI report landed (hot on core, in line on headline) and oil kept reversing lower, the mood had flipped: Europe and the US both closed broadly higher, treating the CPI print as largely priced-in rather than a fresh shock. The net effect is a week that ends with Asia-Pacific carrying nearly all of Friday's damage while Europe and the US actually posted gains on the day — even though most indices globally, US and European alike, still finished the week lower given the scale of Wednesday/Thursday's oil-and-yields-driven selloff.

## Oil

Oil was the week's dominant story and Friday's defining reversal. Brent crude spiked to a fresh escalation-high **above $109–110/barrel intraday** during Asian trading hours on the ongoing US-Iran Strait of Hormuz standoff, before reversing sharply through the European and US sessions to close near **$104.61/barrel (down 2.8–3.6% on the day)**; **WTI settled near $100.05 (down roughly 2.4%)**. The pullback — likely reflecting some easing of the most acute escalation fears, alongside the IEA's lower demand-forecast revision cited by one UK source — provided genuine relief to European and US equities even as **diesel prices set a fresh record above $6.00–6.05/gallon nationally**, a reminder that the conflict's real-economy impact hasn't fully unwound alongside the futures price. See `usa.md`, `uk_europe.md`.

## Gold

Gold closed essentially flat to very slightly higher, holding in the **$4,385–4,409 range (XAU/USD)** as sources diverged on the exact figure — Investrade's $4,408.90 reconciles almost exactly with Thursday's confirmed $4,407.30 close, while USAGOLD's $4,385.61 uses a different reference base; both agree the metal was little-changed on net. The hot core CPI print's upward pressure on rate-hike odds continued to offset gold's usual inflation-hedge appeal, a pattern that has held all week. **Silver fell more sharply, down roughly 2.6% to around $64.90**, its industrial-metal sensitivity to Fed-policy repricing pushing the gold-to-silver ratio out to roughly 67.6. Hong Kong's copper/mining complex (Jiangxi Copper, MMG, Zijin Mining) also sold off sharply even as COMEX gold itself dipped only modestly, continuing the week's recurring pattern of metals equities under-performing even modest moves in the underlying metal. See `usa.md`, `hongkong.md`.

## Bitcoin

Bitcoin opened the day sharply lower (down 2.2% to $76,535.95) and briefly dipped below $77,000 in early New York trading amid pre-CPI caution, before recovering through the session to close near **$77,308.97 (+0.40% on the day)** — a V-shaped session that mirrored equities' own recovery from early softness, as the hot-but-largely-priced-in CPI print didn't derail the broader relief-rally tone. Ethereum traded in a similarly narrow range, firming from an early $2,437 to around $2,454. See `usa.md`.
