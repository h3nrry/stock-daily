Oil remained the single biggest cross-market driver for a second straight day. Brent crude pushed above **$109/barrel intraday** during Asian trading hours — a fresh high in the escalating US-Iran Strait of Hormuz standoff — before easing to around **$105.50 (−1.98% off that intraday peak)** on the latest available reading; WTI traded near **$103.11 (+0.61% on the day)**. The move fed directly into every confirmed Asian market's decline today and is set to be the dominant backdrop for tonight's European and US sessions as well. See `usa.md`.

## Gold

Gold held near **$4,330 (XAU/USD)**, down roughly 1.7% from Thursday's $4,407.30 close, as strengthening Fed-hike expectations and Treasury yields near 5% continue to outweigh the metal's usual safe-haven bid despite the live oil/geopolitical shock — a continuation of the pattern seen through this entire week. Tonight's CPI report is described as gold's next major catalyst. **Silver** was reported down roughly 0.5% on the same read. Notably, gold-and-metals mining stocks in Hong Kong (Jiangxi Copper, Luoyang Molybdenum) fell sharply in sympathy with COMEX gold's own 2.29% intraday decline. See `usa.md`, `hongkong.md`.
