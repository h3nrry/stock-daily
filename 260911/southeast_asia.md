# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 11, 2026 (Friday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia closed a genuinely mixed session Friday: Singapore eked out a small gain after Thursday's dip, Indonesia and Malaysia both pared back sharp morning losses but still finished lower, and Thailand slipped modestly — all trading against the same backdrop of Brent crude's intraday spike above $109/barrel (before easing back toward $104–105), US Treasury yields near 5%, and positioning ahead of the evening's US CPI report (which ultimately came in hot on core but in line on the headline — see `usa.md`).

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,695.93, up modestly (+0.11% per Investing.com's historical-data series)** from Investing.com's own Thursday figure of 5,689.75 — though recomputed against this project's own confirmed Thursday close of 5,692.67 (see `../260910/southeast_asia.md`), the implied move is a smaller but still-positive **+0.06%**, consistent with a genuinely quiet, roughly-flat session. The only same-day narrative color available was the **open**, where the index rose 12.57 points (+0.22%) to 5,702.32, reclaiming the psychologically important 5,700 level "on bargain hunting in blue-chip stocks" after Thursday's 0.7% decline — no same-day closing-level news wrap could be confirmed beyond that, an honest data gap consistent with this project's recurring note about thin same-day Singapore coverage. Sentiment stayed cautious given oil above $100/barrel, elevated Treasury yields, and the looming CPI print, even as solid earnings and a consensus 12-month STI target of 6,140 provided some underlying support. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−1.83% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 4's confirmed record 5,801.96) and **−0.83% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 14's 5,743.59, per Investing.com's historical-data series).

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,541.38, down 47.96 points (−0.73%)** — a substantial recovery from an opening plunge of as much as 1.8-1.49% intraday. Every sector finished in the red: **energy led losses at −2.29%**, followed by **utilities (−1.62%), non-primary consumer goods (−1.04%), financials (−1.03%), property (−0.98%), and industrials (−0.88%)**. **Bank Rakyat Indonesia, Bank Central Asia, and Bayan Resources were the largest drags**, while **Amman Mineral International provided the strongest offsetting support**. The selloff was attributed to escalating US-Iran tensions, the energy-supply and inflation implications of oil's latest leg higher, and concerns about the interest-rate outlook (US federal debt surpassing $40 trillion and the 10-year Treasury yield's push toward 4.84–4.97% were both cited as headwinds for emerging-market valuations, strengthening the dollar). On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−1.43% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 4's confirmed 6,636.47) and **+2.18% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 14's 6,401.89, per Investing.com's historical-data series) — Friday's pullback still leaves the longer-term trend solidly positive.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,686.74, down 18.78 points (−1.10%)**, its lowest level since July 9, after trading in an intraday range of 1,682.74 to 1,705.69. Market breadth was decisively negative — **810 decliners versus 428 advancers**, and among KLCI component stocks specifically, just 4 gained versus 24 that declined. **Allianz (−56 sen to RM21.12), Hong Leong Bank (−52 sen to RM23.08), Sunway Construction (−47 sen to RM7.44), and F&N (−44 sen to RM23.56)** led the decline, while **PETRONAS Chemicals (+27 sen to RM5.09), Batu Kawan (+12 sen to RM20.16), and Concrete Engineering Products (+12 sen to RM4.00)** were among the session's few gainers. Earlier in the day, glove manufacturers (led by Top Glove, the most actively traded share) had rallied on hopes of higher product prices even as banking and property names slid — a pocket of relative strength that did not prevent the broader index from breaking decisively below the psychological 1,700 level. Surging oil prices, sustained inflation concerns, and (per local brokerage commentary) continued foreign-investor outflow were all cited as drivers. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−1.25% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 4's confirmed 1,708.10) and **−2.35% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 14's 1,727.39, per Investing.com's historical-data series).

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,604.52, down 10.55 points (−0.65%)**, on trading value of **THB 71.21 billion** — extending the morning session's already-negative tone (the index had closed the morning session at 1,604.76, down 0.64%, essentially unchanged into the afternoon). Maybank Securities attributed the decline to "a continued surge in crude oil prices, which has fueled concerns over inflation and driven up U.S. government bond yields," with investors also positioning ahead of the evening's US CPI release — the same catalyst cited across every market in this folder. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+0.56% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 4's confirmed 1,595.58) and **−0.28% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 14's 1,609.06, per Investing.com's historical-data series).

## Bottom line

A genuinely uneven regional session that, in every case, pared back a sharper morning decline: Indonesia's −0.73% close followed an opening plunge of nearly 1.8%, Malaysia's −1.10% close came after touching 1,682.74 intraday, and Thailand's −0.65% barely moved from its own morning-session close — only Singapore actually finished higher, if only marginally. The common driver across all four markets was the same one shaping every market this week: oil's intraday spike above $109/barrel on the Strait of Hormuz standoff, elevated US Treasury yields near 5%, and caution ahead of the evening's US CPI report. Malaysia's break below its psychologically important 1,700 level (a 2-month-plus low) and Indonesia's sector-wide red across the board were the region's clearest casualties, while the longer 20-day trend remains solidly positive for Indonesia (+2.18%) and only modestly negative elsewhere.
