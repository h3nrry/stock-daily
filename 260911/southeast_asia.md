# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 11, 2026 (Friday) — SESSION IN PROGRESS

> **This file is an intraday snapshot, not confirmed closes.** All four Southeast Asian markets covered here were still trading at the time this was researched (Singapore/Malaysia close ~5:00pm Taipei time, Thailand ~5:30pm Taipei time, Indonesia ~5:00pm Taipei time). A follow-up pass is needed after each market's close (see `README.md`'s "Follow-up needed" section) to replace this with confirmed closing data.

## Overview (intraday)

Southeast Asia traded broadly lower Friday, tracking the same oil-price surge (Brent above $109 intraday) and rising US Treasury-yield backdrop hitting nearly every market covered this week, with investors across the region also positioning ahead of tonight's US CPI report.

## Singapore — STI (no same-day data available)

No same-day intraday or closing coverage for the Straits Times Index could be confirmed from sources checked at writing time — an honest data gap, consistent with occasional Singapore-coverage lag noted in this project previously. Last confirmed close: **5,692.67** on September 10 (see `../260910/southeast_asia.md`).

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI (intraday — session 1 close)

The **IHSG fell 1.49% (98.43 points) to 6,490.91 as of the midday session-1 break (11:32am local time)**, extending Thursday's decline. Sector losses were led by **energy (−2.79%)**, followed by non-primary consumer goods (−1.67%), utilities (−1.61%), industrials (−1.56%), and finance (−1.52%) — every sector traded in the red. Market breadth was decisively negative: **545 declining stocks versus 141 advancing**. Drivers cited: the escalating US-Iran/Strait of Hormuz conflict and oil prices, plus concern that **US federal debt surpassing $40 trillion** has pushed the US 10-year Treasury yield to around 4.84%, its highest since October 2023 per this source (other sources this week have cited a slightly higher ~4.95–4.96% read — see `usa.md`) — both together strengthening the dollar and pressuring emerging-market valuations. Last confirmed close: **6,591.90 (−1.29%)** on September 10.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI (intraday, as of 12:30pm)

The **FBM KLCI was down 17.18 points to 1,688.34 as of 12:30pm local time (−1.01%)**, a decisive break below the psychological 1,700 level, as traders fled equities on the same oil-price/Treasury-yield combination. Sector detail was mixed: **construction (−1.37%), financial services (−1.02%), property (−0.89%), and utilities (−0.23%) all declined, while energy rose 0.69%** on Brent's push above $107/barrel. **RHB Bank, AmBank, IHH, Tenaga Nasional, and Press Metal** were all lower; **glove manufacturers rallied** on hopes of higher product prices, with **Top Glove** the day's most actively traded share. Breadth was negative, with decliners outnumbering advancers more than 2:1 (723 to 339). Last confirmed close: **1,706.82 (−0.44%)** on September 10.

## Thailand — SET (intraday — morning session close)

The **SET Index closed the morning session at 1,604.76, down 10.31 points (−0.64%)**, per Kaohoon International, as of 1:14pm Bangkok time. Maybank Securities attributed the decline to "a continued surge in crude oil prices, which has fueled concerns over inflation and driven up U.S. government bond yields," pressuring risk assets regionally, with investors also awaiting tonight's US CPI data ahead of next week's FOMC meeting — the same catalyst cited across every market in this folder. Last confirmed close: **1,615.27 (−0.16%)** on September 10.
