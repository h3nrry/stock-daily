# Hong Kong Stock Market Analysis — September 11, 2026 (Friday)

## Headline

Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index fell for a fifth consecutive session but pared back sharply from the morning's steeper losses, closing at **24,805.63, down 148.84 points (−0.60%)** — a figure that reconciles exactly against Thursday's confirmed 24,954.47 close. The tech-heavy **Hang Seng Tech Index fell a much smaller 0.23% to approximately 4,320.5**, a genuine intraday reversal after having been down as much as 1.36-1.8% at the open and midday. Trading volume reached **HK$228.43 billion**.

## What drove the market

- **Copper and mining stocks were the day's sharpest decliners on White House tariff uncertainty over refined copper**: **Jiangxi Copper fell 9.19% to HK$34.96**, **MMG fell more than 8%**, and **Zijin Mining declined more than 6%** — even as **COMEX gold futures fell 1.94–2.29%** intraday, an echo of Thursday's paradox where metals-linked equities sold off alongside softer gold rather than benefiting from safe-haven flows.
- **Property developers fell broadly (roughly 2.67% to 7.28%)**, led by **New World Development (00016), which dropped 7.28% post-earnings**.
- **Large-cap technology and internet names reversed higher through the session**: **Xiaomi rose 1.70%**, **Tencent and JD.com both gained 0.66%**, and **Alibaba added roughly 0.5–0.56%** — a marked turnaround from the morning's broadly negative tech tape (see the intraday context captured earlier in the session, below).
- **Optical-communications names rallied**: **Innolight climbed 4.37%** and **Yangtze Optical Fiber gained 3.4–3.44%**.
- **Haidilao reversed an early loss to close up 1.46%**, another example of Friday's intraday-recovery pattern.
- **A handful of individual names posted outsized moves**: a robotics name (Guangdong Huayan Robotics) surged as much as 25.62% on news of its inclusion in Stock Connect, and another stock jumped roughly 22% on an executive appointment — while at least one AI-related name was reported down more than 73% from its earlier peak, underscoring how uneven the AI trade's unwind has been this week.
- Despite the index's relatively modest 0.60% decline, **market breadth stayed decisively negative — 742 advancing stocks versus 1,591 declining** — indicating the recovery was concentrated in large caps and a handful of specific stories rather than broad-based.
- The session continued to trade against the same backdrop as the rest of the week: **Brent crude had touched above $109/barrel intraday amid the ongoing Strait of Hormuz standoff** before easing back toward $104–105 by the time Hong Kong closed, and the **US 10-year Treasury yield remained close to 5%** ahead of the US CPI report due after Hong Kong's close.

## Money flow — Stock Connect (港股通 / 南向資金)

Southbound funds were **net buyers of approximately HK$4.431 billion (44.31億港元) as of the close**, per 24topnews — a continuation of mainland investors' underlying support for Hong Kong equities even as the benchmark index itself extended its losing streak to a fifth session. This divergence (index down, southbound money still net-positive) is consistent with a market where large-cap tech/internet names — the biggest beneficiaries of southbound flows — were among the session's better performers, while the index-level pressure came instead from copper/mining and property names less favored by mainland buying.

## Bottom line

A fifth straight losing session for the Hang Seng, but a much gentler one than the morning's intraday declines suggested — the index closed down just 0.60% to 24,805.63 after having been down as much as roughly 1% earlier in the day, with the Hang Seng Tech Index similarly paring back to just a 0.23% decline. The session's clearest story was a rotation rather than a uniform move: copper/mining names (Jiangxi Copper, MMG, Zijin Mining) and property developers (led by New World Development's post-earnings 7.28% drop) did the damage, while large-cap tech/internet names (Xiaomi, Tencent, Alibaba, JD.com) and optical-communications stocks (Innolight, Yangtze Optical Fiber) actually closed higher. Southbound Stock Connect flows stayed net-positive (+HK$4.431 billion), underscoring that mainland money continued supporting the market even as the headline index extended its losing streak — a genuine divergence worth watching into next week, especially with market breadth (742 advancers vs. 1,591 decliners) still pointing to a narrower, more selective rally beneath the surface.
