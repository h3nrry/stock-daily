# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 10, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

European markets fell for a second straight session as the European Central Bank raised interest rates for the second time this year and oil's rally accelerated further. **FTSE 100 fell roughly 0.6% to approximately 10,634.65**, **DAX 40 closed at 25,361.15 (−0.84%)**, **CAC 40 closed at approximately 8,117.05 (−0.49%)**, and the pan-European **STOXX 600 closed at 637.73 (−0.5% as reported, −0.42% recomputed — see note below)** — a broadly synchronized, if more moderate, continuation of Wednesday's sharper selloff.

## What drove the session

- **The ECB raised its deposit rate 25 basis points to 2.50%** — its second increase this year — explicitly to prevent energy-driven inflation "from spreading through the euro zone economy." The central bank simultaneously **raised its growth projection to 0.9% (from 0.8%)** and its **inflation forecast to average 3% for the year**. President Christine Lagarde said "risks to the inflation outlook are to the upside" and signaled readiness for further hikes; traders priced in roughly **60 basis points of additional increases through April 2027**.
- **Oil extended its advance sharply**: Brent crude jumped more than 4% to surpass **$100–105/barrel** amid intensifying Strait of Hormuz/Red Sea tensions disrupting shipping supply lines, directly reinforcing the inflation concerns behind the ECB's move.
- **US markets added their own pressure**: a hotter-than-expected US Producer Price Index (**+5.4% y/y vs. 5.3% forecast**) reinforced expectations for a Fed rate hike next week, while **US Treasury yields surged — the 2-year to about 4.52% (highest since 2024) and the 10-year approaching 4.92%** — making bonds more attractive relative to equities and pressuring growth/tech names in particular. Wall Street itself was lower in sympathy the same session (see `../260910/usa.md`).
- **Germany's DAX and France's CAC 40 both fell**, though more moderately than Wednesday's sharper declines; the **FTSE's own decline was of a similar order of magnitude** despite the UK sitting outside the eurozone/ECB's direct remit — underscoring how Thursday's driver (oil, global yields) was genuinely cross-regional rather than ECB-specific.
- **One country-specific bright spot**: **Ireland's Irish Continental Group jumped 6.7%** after shareholders approved CEO Eamonn Rothwell's €1.2 billion management buyout proposal — a standout single-stock story amid the broader regional decline.

## Bottom line

A second straight lower session for Europe, though a genuinely more moderate one than Wednesday's sharp selloff — Germany's DAX (−0.84%) and France's CAC 40 (−0.49%) led the decline, with the FTSE 100 falling a broadly similar amount despite sitting outside the ECB's direct policy remit. The session's defining event was the **ECB's second rate hike of the year**, delivered explicitly to head off energy-driven inflation as oil pushed toward $105/barrel on an intensifying Strait of Hormuz standoff — with Christine Lagarde's explicitly upside-skewed inflation warning suggesting more hikes are likely still to come. A hot US producer-price print and fresh multi-year highs in Treasury yields added transatlantic pressure on the same session. Ireland's Irish Continental Group (+6.7% on a shareholder-approved buyout) stood out as the region's clearest single-stock bright spot against an otherwise uniformly negative backdrop.
