# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 10, 2026 (Thursday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia traded broadly lower Thursday, tracking the same intensified Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz risk-off tone and oil's push above $101–105/barrel that hit nearly every major market this week. Indonesia led the region's declines by a wide margin, Singapore and Malaysia posted smaller losses, and Thailand was essentially flat in a genuinely two-sided session.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,692.67, down roughly 36.96 points (−0.65%*)** from Wednesday's confirmed 5,729.63, per Investing.com's historical-data series — no same-day Singapore-specific news wrap could be confirmed from sources checked at writing time, an honest data gap consistent with occasional Singapore-coverage lag in this project. The move is directionally consistent with the region-wide risk-off tone (oil, ECB rate hike, US yields) hitting every other market covered this folder. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−0.96% over 5 trading days** (~1 week, vs. September 3's confirmed 5,747.71) and **−0.48% over 20 trading days** (~1 month, vs. August 13's 5,720.05), per Investing.com's historical-data series.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,591.90, down 86.30 points (−1.29%)** — the region's sharpest decline by far — battered by the same oil-price surge and rising US bond yields hitting markets globally. **Energy led sector declines at −2.21%**, followed by **healthcare (−1.69%), industrials (−1.53%), materials (−1.22%), and financials (−1.18%)**; **property stocks were the most resilient, down just −0.07%**. **Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BBRI) was the single largest drag, contributing roughly −15.52 points**, with **Bayan Resources and Bank Central Asia** also weighing heavily. Market breadth was decisively negative: of **963 traded securities, 159 advanced versus 541 declined**. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−1.14% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 3's confirmed 6,667.89) and **+4.60% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 13's 6,301.77) — Thursday's sharp pullback still leaves the longer-term trend solidly positive.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,706.82, down 7.52 points (−0.44%)**, as oil prices above $101/barrel stoked cautious sentiment around sustained inflation and energy costs. Market breadth was negative (**353 gainers vs. 512 decliners**) on trading volume of **1.51 billion shares worth RM864.49 million**. Energy and plantation names found some support even amid the broader caution — **Petronas Dagangan gained 16 sen to RM20.46** and **United Plantations rose 68 sen to RM33.68** on crude-palm-oil price strength, while **Nestlé added 38 sen to RM93.38**. Decliners included **Telekom (−23 sen to RM7.95), Hong Leong Financial (−12 sen to RM18.92), and Sime Darby (−10 sen to RM2.55)**. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **−0.48% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 3's confirmed 1,715.13) and **−1.61% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 13's 1,734.71).

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,615.27, down 2.62 points (−0.16%)**, on trading value of **THB 43.15 billion**, in a genuinely two-sided, sideways session per Kaohoon/AIRA Securities: **profit-taking hit energy stocks after their recent gains**, while **banking and hospital sectors provided offsetting support**. Investors stayed focused on **Friday's US CPI data** ahead of next week's FOMC meeting — the same catalyst weighing on sentiment across every market covered this week. On a multi-horizon basis, the index is **+2.43% over 5 trading days** (vs. September 3's confirmed 1,576.92) and **+0.06% over 20 trading days** (vs. August 13's 1,614.36, measured over roughly 19 trading days given August 12's Thai holiday).

## Bottom line

A genuinely uneven session across the region — Indonesia's sharp −1.29% decline, the clearest single casualty of oil's latest leg higher and rising US yields, stood well apart from Singapore's and Malaysia's more modest declines (−0.65%/−0.44%) and Thailand's essentially flat close (−0.16%), where energy-sector profit-taking directly offset the same oil-driven headwind pressuring energy importers elsewhere in the region. Every market's 20-day figure remains flat-to-solidly positive (Indonesia's +4.60% the standout), suggesting Thursday's softness reads as a pause — sharper in Jakarta than elsewhere — within otherwise still-constructive medium-term trends.
