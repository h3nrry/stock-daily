# Korea Stock Market Analysis — September 10, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

Korea's KOSPI pulled back from Wednesday's 33-session-high close as heavy foreign selling met a strong wave of retail buying that defended the psychologically important 7,000 level. The index **closed at 7,033.92, down 17.72 points (−0.25%)**, holding above 7,000 by less than 34 points despite **foreign investors net selling roughly ₩2.71 trillion**. The close reconciles exactly against Wednesday's confirmed 7,051.64 (see `../260909/korea.md`).

## What drove the pullback

- **Weakness in US technology shares overnight and renewed concern about slowing global growth** dampened domestic sentiment — a reversal of Wednesday's own chip-driven optimism.
- **Foreign investors net sold approximately ₩2,714 billion** — a sharp swing from Wednesday's roughly +₩382 billion net buy — while **individual/retail investors net bought approximately ₩2,542 billion**, absorbing almost all of the foreign selling and keeping the index above 7,000. **Institutional investors were essentially neutral, net buying about ₩158 billion.**
- **The won still strengthened modestly**, to **1,339.20 per dollar (down 4.3 won)** from Wednesday, even as equities fell — a genuine divergence from Wednesday's pattern, when currency and equities moved in the same reinforcing direction.
- The pullback tracked the same global oil/yield/ECB-driven risk-off tone hitting nearly every market Thursday (see `../260910/usa.md`, `uk_europe.md`) — a contrast to Wednesday, when Korea's own chip-cycle story ran directly counter to a negative US/Europe session.

## Bottom line

A session that looks calm at the index level but was anything but underneath — KOSPI −0.25% to 7,033.92 as foreign investors reversed hard from Wednesday's buying spree to sell roughly ₩2.71 trillion, only for individual investors to step in and absorb almost all of it, defending the psychologically important 7,000 level by less than 34 points. Institutional investors sat essentially on the sidelines. That this happened alongside a strengthening won — rather than the currency and equity markets reinforcing each other as they did Wednesday — is a genuine change in the day's cross-asset signal. Whether retail's support holds if foreign selling continues, and whether 7,000 survives another session like Thursday's, are the clearest things to watch into Friday.
