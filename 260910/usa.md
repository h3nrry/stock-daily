# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 10, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

US markets fell for a fourth consecutive session as a hotter-than-expected wholesale inflation reading collided with an intensifying Strait of Hormuz oil shock and fresh multi-year highs in Treasury yields. The **Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 316.20 points (−0.60%) to 52,064.46**, the **S&P 500 fell 44.57 points (−0.58%) to 7,591.79**, and the **Nasdaq Composite fell 171.61 points (−0.65%) to 26,081.73**. All three figures reconcile arithmetically against Wednesday's confirmed close (see `../260909/usa.md`).

## What drove the market

- **Pre-market futures had pointed to a firmer open** (Dow +0.27%, S&P +0.17%, Nasdaq −0.06%) as stocks tried to "regain their footing after three days of declines" — but that early optimism reversed hard once the session's economic data and oil moves came through, and the S&P 500 was already down 0.33-0.59% by the open/midday.
- **Producer Price Index (PPI) came in hot**: +0.4% month-over-month (matching estimates), but **+5.4% year-over-year — up from 4.8% in July and above the 5.3% forecast** — directly feeding higher Fed-hike odds. **Existing home sales fell 2% month-over-month to a 3.98 million seasonally adjusted annual rate**, the slowest pace since June 2025, and **initial jobless claims came in at 206,000**, a modest miss versus the 205,000 forecast.
- **Odds of a hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting rose to roughly 70%** per CME FedWatch, up from about 60% Wednesday.
- **Oil's rally intensified through the day and into the close**: after breaking above Brent $105.37/barrel (+3.6%, highest since May) and WTI $100.10 (+4.2%) by late morning, the advance kept accelerating — Brent settled near **$107.63/barrel (+6.34%)** and WTI near **$102.48 (+6.69%)** by the close, as the Strait of Hormuz/Red Sea shipping-disruption story kept intensifying (see `../260909/usa.md`, `hongkong.md`) — the same story cited explicitly as a trigger for the European Central Bank's rate hike the same session (see `uk_europe.md`).
- **Treasury yields pushed to fresh multi-year highs across the curve**: the **2-year rose to 4.567% (+14bp), its highest since 2024**, the **10-year to 4.954% (+11.4bp), its highest in nearly three years**, and the **30-year to 5.367% (+8.1bp)**, among its highest levels in over two decades — all extending Wednesday's already-elevated closes (2Y 4.427%, 10Y 4.843%, 30Y 5.294%).
- Market breadth stayed weak, consistent with a fourth straight lower session for all three major indices.

## Notable stock stories

- **Cooper Companies** fell nearly 14% (intraday reports ranged as low as −18%) after a disappointing strategic review and guidance cut.
- **Reddit** rose 4.7% on user-growth data.
- **Macy's** beat earnings estimates but still declined on the day.
- **Chipmakers were hit hard**: **Nvidia fell about 2.1%**, **Micron dropped roughly 4–5%**, and **SK Hynix's US-listed shares fell about 2.7%** — even as **Apple bucked the trend, gaining roughly 1.4% to more than 3%** depending on the source, extending optimism around its new foldable iPhone Duo and iPhone 18 Pro launch (see `../260909/usa.md`).
- **AeroVironment surged** after announcing record revenue and a strong order backlog.
- **SpaceX** traded down about 0.5% intraday after a share-unlock expiration.
- The semiconductor-heavy weakness shows up clearly in the ETF flow data below: **QQQ and QQQM both fell roughly 1.06–1.07%**, well beyond the Nasdaq Composite's own −0.65% decline — a genuine divergence between chip-heavy positioning and the broader tech-index headline, echoing the SOX/TSMC-ADR divergence pattern seen in earlier sessions this month.

## Treasury yields

**2-year 4.567% (+14bp), highest since 2024. 10-year 4.954% (+11.4bp), highest in nearly three years** (per TheStreet; a separate same-day cross-check from Investrade put it slightly lower at 4.943%, +10.4bp). **30-year 5.367% (+8.1bp)**, among its highest levels in over two decades (Investrade's cross-check: 5.35%, +6bp). All three legs extend Wednesday's already-elevated closes (2Y 4.427%, 10Y 4.843%, 30Y 5.294%) to fresh multi-year highs, exactly as the intraday reads captured earlier the same session had anticipated.

## Credit spreads

FRED's **ICE BofA US High Yield OAS remains dated September 8 at 2.67%** — unchanged for a third straight reading and still historically tight. The **Investment-Grade OAS series has ticked one day more current, now dated September 9 at 0.81%**, also unchanged in level. Neither series shows any sign yet of credit markets repricing for the oil shock or the sharp rise in Treasury yields — fixed income continues to read Thursday's action as a commodity/geopolitical and rate story rather than a credit-quality event.

## Oil, gold, bitcoin

- **Oil**: intensified through the day — from a pre-market WTI read near $96.85 (+0.83%), to a late-morning Brent break above $105.37 (+3.6%) and WTI above $100.10 (+4.2%), to a close near **Brent $107.63/barrel (+6.34%)** and **WTI $102.48 (+6.69%)** — the sharpest single-day oil move so far in this week's escalating Strait of Hormuz story (see `../260909/usa.md`, `hongkong.md`).
- **Gold**: fell through the session and into the close — from a pre-market $4,443.60 (−0.38%) through a midday $4,427.40–$4,412.10 (−0.75% to −1.09%) to a close near **$4,407.30 (−1.2%)** per Investrade — a continuation of the pattern seen Wednesday, where gold's usual safe-haven bid is being outweighed by higher-for-longer rate repricing even amid a genuine geopolitical/oil shock. **Silver fell far more sharply, down roughly 5.4% to $64.93.**
- **Bitcoin**: traded down through the session, from a $78,291.64 open to roughly **$77,300–$77,900** by the afternoon (readings ranged from −1.68% to −1.75% on the day), its softest level of the week; **Ethereum was similarly soft**, opening near $2,467 (−0.7%) and drifting toward the $2,460s. Both cryptocurrencies stayed pressured by the same rate-hike-odds/inflation-data anticipation weighing on equities, ahead of Friday's CPI report and next week's FOMC meeting. Since bitcoin trades 24/7, these are same-day range reads rather than a single "close."

## Bottom line

A genuinely negative, broad-based session — the fourth straight decline for all three major indices — driven by the same two forces that have dominated this week: a hot PPI print (headline +5.4% y/y) pushing Fed-hike odds for next week's FOMC to roughly 70%, and an intensifying Strait of Hormuz oil shock that pushed Brent above $107 and WTI above $102 by the close, well beyond even the elevated levels captured intraday the same session. Treasury yields extended to fresh multi-year highs across the curve. Credit spreads stayed historically tight, still reading this as a commodity/rate story rather than a credit event. The clearest divergence beneath the headline numbers was semiconductor weakness (Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix) dragging QQQ/QQQM down roughly 1.06–1.07% — nearly double the Nasdaq Composite's own decline — even as Apple's foldable-iPhone momentum pushed the opposite direction. Friday's CPI report remains the single biggest scheduled catalyst into next week's Fed decision.
