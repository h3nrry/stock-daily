# Daily Market Briefing — September 10, 2026 (Thursday)

Global snapshot: a hot US Producer Price Index reading and an intensifying Strait of Hormuz oil shock hit almost every market covered here on the same calendar day. **Brent crude accelerated from an already-elevated intraday level above $105/barrel to a close near $107.63 (+6.34%)**, and **US Treasury yields pushed to fresh multi-year highs across the curve** (2-year 4.567%, 10-year 4.954%, 30-year 5.367%). **US markets fell for a fourth straight session** (Dow −0.60%, S&P −0.58%, Nasdaq −0.65%), with semiconductor weakness (Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix) dragging QQQ/QQQM down roughly twice as much as the Nasdaq Composite itself. **Europe fell for a second straight session**, more moderately than Wednesday, as the **European Central Bank raised rates 25bp** explicitly to head off energy-driven inflation (DAX −0.84%, CAC 40 −0.49%, FTSE −0.60%‡, STOXX 600 −0.42%). Asia was broadly negative too, though for a genuinely mixed set of reasons: **Taiwan's TAIEX fell 0.51%** on a lukewarm reaction to Apple's new iPhone lineup that hit the electronics supply chain, **Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell a sharper 1.27%** to a six-week low as AI-application names (Zhipu, MiniMax-W) led a broad tech selloff, and **Korea's KOSPI slipped just 0.25%**, holding 7,000 as heavy retail buying absorbed a large wave of foreign selling. **Japan's Nikkei was the day's outlier, closing up 0.19%** despite a nearly 1,000-point intraday plunge, recovering the entire loss and more in a dramatic final-hour rally helped by a low arbitrage-buying overhang. Southeast Asia was uneven — Indonesia's IHSG led declines at −1.29% while Thailand's SET was essentially flat (−0.16%) as energy-sector profit-taking offset the region-wide oil headwind. Gold and silver fell further despite the escalating geopolitical backdrop, and bitcoin/ethereum both softened as inflation data and Fed-hike odds (now ~70% for next week's FOMC) weighed on risk assets ahead of Friday's CPI report.

**Reading the table:** Thursday's clearest through-line is a single global catalyst — the US PPI print and the accelerating Strait of Hormuz oil shock — landing on almost every market, but with genuinely different magnitudes and even directions. The US, Europe, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Singapore, and Malaysia all fell in step with the oil/yield/inflation story. Taiwan's decline was driven by a company-specific catalyst (Apple's underwhelming-to-investors iPhone launch) layered on top of the same macro backdrop. Korea and Thailand were the region's relative outperformers, with heavy retail buying and energy-sector profit-taking respectively cushioning the same headwind. Japan's Nikkei is the standout number in the table — a violently two-sided session that still closed higher, illustrating just how sensitive intraday positioning has become to the same handful of headlines. On a multi-horizon view, Korea's KOSPI (+6.91% over 5 days) and Taiwan's TAIEX (+2.36%) remain the strongest medium-term performers in the table by a wide margin, a reminder that Thursday's softness across most of Asia is a pause within otherwise still-strong recent trends, not a reversal of them.

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** a fourth straight lower session — Dow −0.60%, S&P −0.58%, Nasdaq −0.65% — driven by a hot PPI print (+5.4% y/y) that pushed Fed-hike odds for next week's FOMC to roughly 70%, and an oil shock that accelerated into the close (Brent $107.63, +6.34%; WTI $102.48, +6.69%). Treasury yields hit fresh multi-year highs across the curve. The clearest divergence beneath the headline was semiconductor weakness (Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix) dragging QQQ/QQQM down roughly 1.06–1.07%, nearly double the Nasdaq Composite's own decline, even as Apple's foldable-iPhone momentum pushed the opposite direction. Credit spreads (HY OAS 2.67%, IG OAS 0.81%) stayed historically tight. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** a genuinely negative session beneath a still-orderly headline number — TAIEX −0.51% to 46,940.49 despite a 600-plus-point intraday plunge that tested the 10-day moving average before recovering about half its loss. Apple's underwhelming-to-investors iPhone Duo/iPhone 18 Pro launch reversed Wednesday's optics-driven optimism and pressured the broader electronics supply chain (TSMC, Delta Electronics −6.69%, Hon Hai), compounding the same oil/yield/ECB backdrop hitting markets globally. Foreign investors reversed abruptly from more than NT$209 billion of buying Wednesday to over NT$38 billion of selling Thursday, ending a four-day streak. See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** a session that looked calm at the index level but was anything but underneath — KOSPI −0.25% to 7,033.92 as foreign investors reversed hard to sell roughly ₩2.71 trillion, only for individual/retail investors to step in and absorb almost all of it, defending the psychologically important 7,000 level by less than 34 points. Institutional investors sat essentially on the sidelines, while the won strengthened even as stocks fell — a genuine divergence from Wednesday's reinforcing pattern. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** the region's sharpest decline — Hang Seng −1.27% to 24,954.47, breaking below the psychologically important 25,000 level to a six-week low, as AI-application names (Zhipu, MiniMax-W, down 7–10.5%) led a much more uniformly negative tech tape than Wednesday's genuinely mixed session, spreading into large-cap internet bellwethers (Alibaba, Meituan, Baidu, Tencent), software, semiconductors, and heavy machinery. Shrinking volume was flagged locally as a sign of directionless, foreign-investor-light trading. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Asia — Japan:** the day's most dramatic single session — Nikkei 225 +0.19% to 65,270.95 masks an intraday round-trip of nearly 1,100 points, from a 10:36am low of 64,186.68 driven by the same deteriorating Middle East/oil backdrop to a day's-high close. A 10-month-low arbitrage-buying overhang (裁定買い残) appears to have provided real technical support for the final-hour rebound, even as a Bank of Japan official's continued-rate-hike comments kept the yen on its own separate, slightly weaker path. See `japan.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** a genuinely uneven region — Indonesia's IHSG led declines at −1.29% (energy −2.21%, BBRI the largest single drag) on the same oil/yield headwind, Singapore (−0.65%†) and Malaysia (−0.44%) posted smaller losses, and Thailand's SET was essentially flat (−0.16%) as banking/hospital-sector strength offset energy-stock profit-taking. Every market's 20-day figure remains flat-to-solidly positive (Indonesia's +4.60% the standout), suggesting Thursday's softness is a pause within otherwise still-constructive medium-term trends. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **England & Europe:** a second straight lower session, though more moderate than Wednesday's sharp selloff — DAX −0.84%, CAC 40 −0.49%, FTSE −0.60%‡, STOXX 600 −0.42%. The defining event was the **ECB's second rate hike of the year** (deposit rate to 2.50%), delivered explicitly to head off energy-driven inflation as oil pushed toward $105+/barrel, with Christine Lagarde's explicitly upside-skewed inflation warning suggesting more hikes are likely still to come. A hot US PPI print and fresh multi-year highs in Treasury yields added transatlantic pressure the same session. Ireland's Irish Continental Group (+6.7% on a shareholder-approved buyout) stood out as the region's clearest single-stock bright spot. See `uk_europe.md`.

## Oil

Oil was Thursday's single biggest cross-market driver, and its rally accelerated all day rather than fading: from a pre-market WTI read near $96.85 (+0.83%), through a late-morning break above Brent $105.37 (+3.6%, highest since May) and WTI $100.10 (+4.2%), to a close near **Brent $107.63/barrel (+6.34%)** and **WTI $102.48 (+6.69%)** — the sharpest single-day move so far in this week's escalating Strait of Hormuz standoff. The move fed directly into the ECB's rate-hike decision in Europe, into fresh multi-year-high US Treasury yields, and into a broadly risk-off tone across Hong Kong, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Singapore, while providing an offsetting tailwind for energy/plantation names in Malaysia and a source of profit-taking (rather than further gains) in Thailand's own energy sector. See `usa.md`.

## Gold

Gold fell further despite the escalating geopolitical backdrop, continuing Wednesday's pattern of higher-for-longer rate repricing outweighing the metal's usual safe-haven bid: from a pre-market $4,443.60 (−0.38%) through a midday $4,427.40–$4,412.10 (−0.75% to −1.09%) to a close near **$4,407.30 (−1.2%)**. **Silver fell far more sharply, down roughly 5.4% to $64.93** — a much steeper decline than gold's, consistent with silver's own sharper Wednesday drop. See `usa.md`.

## Bitcoin

Bitcoin traded down through the session, from a $78,291.64 open to roughly **$77,300–$77,900** by the afternoon (readings ranged from −1.68% to −1.75% on the day), its softest level of the week. **Ethereum was similarly soft**, opening near $2,467 (−0.7%) and drifting toward the $2,460s. Both cryptocurrencies stayed pressured by the same rate-hike-odds/inflation-data anticipation weighing on equities, ahead of Friday's CPI report and next week's FOMC meeting. Since bitcoin trades 24/7, these are same-day range reads rather than a single "close." See `usa.md`.
