# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 14, 2026 (Monday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia had a genuinely mixed session, in contrast to Northeast Asia's broadly negative reaction to the weekend AI-safety story: Thailand fell on direct read-through from the global tech selloff, Indonesia was whipsawed by a surprise cabinet reshuffle before paring most of its losses, Malaysia bucked the regional trend entirely with a genuine rebound, and Singapore also closed higher (+0.40%), insulated by its bank-heavy index composition.

## Singapore — STI

Confirmed with next-day coverage: **the Straits Times Index closed at 5,718.02, up 22.09 points (+0.40%)**, from Sept 11's confirmed 5,695.93 close — genuinely bucking the regional trend on a day the weekend AI-safety story pressured most tech-adjacent markets. This confirms the inference made at writing time: Singapore's bank-heavy (rather than tech-heavy) index composition left it comparatively insulated from the same-day selloff hitting Korea, Taiwan, and Japan. The index ranged between 5,677.79 and 5,725.56 intraday. Notable single-stock activity: **CapAllianz (594) surged over 100%** after announcing a placement of 1.95 billion shares to raise roughly SG$2.1 million, while **MetaOptics (9MT) fell over 5%** on its own SG$1.1 million capital-raise plan — both small-cap-specific stories rather than index-level drivers.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,534, down 6.68 points (−0.10%)**, after a volatile session that saw it plunge as low as **6,371 intraday** — a much sharper swing than the modest closing loss suggests. The catalyst was a surprise **cabinet reshuffle**: President Prabowo removed Finance Minister Purbaya and appointed Suahasil Nazara in his place, triggering an immediate risk-off reaction (the market opened down roughly 1% and extended losses through the morning) before recovering more than half the intraday damage by the close ("IHSG Mantul & Pangkas Koreksi" / IHSG bounces back and trims the correction). **Eight of eleven sectors weakened, with healthcare leading losses at −2.15%.** Market breadth: 429 decliners versus 219 advancers (141 unchanged), on transaction value of Rp17.91 trillion. Big-bank stocks (BBRI, BBCA, BMRI, BBNI) were reported higher into the close per one source, suggesting financials helped anchor the afternoon recovery, similar to the pattern seen in Taiwan today.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI rose 11.27 points (+0.66%) to close at 1,698.01**, snapping a four-day losing streak and closing at its intraday high — a genuine outperformance versus the rest of the region on a day when the dominant global narrative (AI-deceleration fears) was broadly negative for risk assets. **Banking stocks led the rebound**: Maybank (+8 sen to RM10.46), Public Bank (+5 sen to RM4.85), and CIMB (+18 sen to RM7.97); utility name Tenaga Nasional also added 4 sen to RM13.64. Berjaya Research noted the index "has formed a bullish candlestick and could take a jab towards the 1,700 psychological level." Market breadth remained negative, however — 764 losers versus 421 gainers — meaning the index-level gain was concentrated in large-cap banking/utility names rather than broad-based, a similar "narrow, large-cap-led" pattern to several other markets' rebound sessions this project has tracked.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index fell 13.45 points (−0.84%) to close at 1,591.07**, on trading volume of THB 61.89 billion. The decline tracked the global tech selloff directly: **selling pressure in Delta Electronics (DELTA) and other electronics shares** mirrored the semiconductor-led weakness hitting Taiwan, Japan, and Korea, compounded by **concerns over a Federal Reserve rate hike and elevated bond yields** ahead of Wednesday's FOMC decision. **Banking and energy sectors cushioned the decline**, echoing the same financials-as-stabilizer pattern seen in Taiwan and Indonesia today. Analysts expect a "wait-and-see" posture from investors heading into the Fed decision.

## Bottom line

Southeast Asia's genuinely mixed session today — Malaysia rallying, Indonesia whipsawed by domestic politics rather than the global AI story, and only Thailand tracking the regional tech-selloff narrative directly — stands in sharp contrast to Northeast Asia's broadly negative reaction. Indonesia's cabinet reshuffle is a reminder that domestic political catalysts can dominate a session's price action even on a day when a major global story (the weekend AI-safety essay) is driving every other market this project covers. Malaysia's banking-led rebound and Thailand's own banking/energy cushion both echo the "financials as stabilizer" pattern seen across multiple markets today, suggesting this is a genuinely cross-regional dynamic rather than a market-specific quirk.
