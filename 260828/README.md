# Daily Market Briefing — August 28, 2026 (Friday)

Global snapshot: the week's dominant story — full-throttle AI/tech optimism following Nvidia's earnings — met its first real macro test today, **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first major speech as Fed Chair at Jackson Hole**, and the result was a genuine shift in tone. Warsh struck a hawkish note on inflation ("the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices"), triggering a bear-flattener in US Treasury yields and pulling **US indices modestly lower (S&P −0.12%, Nasdaq −0.34%)** into the weekend. **Korea's KOSPI plunged 1.79%** on a concentrated wave of foreign/institutional selling in Samsung and SK Hynix ahead of the speech — the clearest casualty of pre-Jackson-Hole de-risking. Against that, **Taiwan's TAIEX defied the caution entirely**, surging **+0.77% to a fresh record close of 46,331.45** on an extraordinary **+NT$579.13B** day of institutional buying and a second straight limit-up in Largan Precision. **Hong Kong (+0.11-0.30%)** and **Japan's Nikkei (+0.41%)** both posted modest gains, while Southeast Asia and Europe traded mostly sideways to higher ahead of the speech's full digestion. See `usa.md`, `taiwan.md`, `japan.md`, `korea.md`, `hongkong.md`, `southeast_asia.md`, and `uk_europe.md` in this folder for the full write-ups.

**Reading the table:** today is a genuine story of divergence within Asia itself. Korea's sharp reversal (−1.79%, on top of already-flagged margin-debt warning signs from Thursday) shows a market that had run ahead of itself getting a real gut-check from concentrated foreign selling in its two largest chip names. Taiwan did the exact opposite — record close, record institutional buying, a second straight limit-up in Largan Precision — a genuinely bifurcated regional reaction to the same Jackson Hole anticipation. The US itself gave back its early-session gains once Warsh's actual tone landed, a pattern that — if Europe's pre-speech readings above turn out to have moderated similarly by their own close — suggests today's real theme was "hawkish surprise, priced unevenly across markets that had different amounts of froth to begin with."

## Where the money is flowing

- **United States:** Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone triggered a bear-flattener — short-term Treasury yields rose ~8bps while longer-dated yields fell, and the 10-year climbed to ~4.72-4.73% from ~4.66% Thursday. Equities gave back early gains as the tone was digested. See `usa.md`.
- **Taiwan:** an extraordinary institutional buying day — **foreign +NT$366.13B, investment trusts +NT$33.66B, dealers +NT$179.34B (incl. NT$131.95B hedging), combined +NT$579.13B** — roughly nine times Thursday's already-solid figure, alongside continued futures short-covering (down another 1,181 contracts to 83,655 net short). See `taiwan.md`.
- **Korea:** foreign investors sold ₩1.76 trillion and institutions sold ₩284.1 billion, concentrated almost entirely in Samsung Electronics (−3.38%) and SK Hynix (−4.45%); retail investors bought a net ₩424.1 billion trying to stabilize the market, but were outweighed. See `korea.md`.
- **Hong Kong:** offshore capital rotated back toward Hong Kong and away from mainland Shanghai listings, a reversal of Thursday's pattern. See `hongkong.md`.
- **Southeast Asia:** a quiet, mixed session — Indonesia's IHSG paused essentially flat after Thursday's surge, with sector leadership rotating toward smaller speculative names; Thailand's SET fell nearly 0.8% on concentrated big-cap (DELTA, AOT, ADVANC) selling. See `southeast_asia.md`.
- **Europe:** traded higher through the session on hopes for a dovish Warsh outcome — France's CAC 40 notably reversed Thursday's political-risk-driven selloff — but the actual hawkish tone of the speech means these gains are worth re-checking against Monday's open. See `uk_europe.md`.
- **Global cross-asset:** the dollar ticked up slightly (**DXY ~99.19**), gold eased to **~$4,631.5/oz (−1.0% week, +12.8% month)** on the more hawkish Fed tone, and **bitcoin fell to ~$77,907 (−2.9% day)** while oil declined alongside the broader risk-adjustment (WTI −0.42% to $83.18, Brent −0.58% to $88.01).

**Net read:** today was the week's real inflection point. Four days of unambiguous AI-optimism (Nvidia's earnings, enterprise-software blowouts, broadening institutional buying across Taiwan, Korea, and Japan) ran into Fed Chair Warsh's first major policy speech, which came in more hawkish than markets had hoped. The reaction was genuinely uneven: Korea's most-leveraged, most AI-exposed names took the sharpest hit, US indices gave back modest gains, but Taiwan's institutional buying wave was powerful enough to push straight through the macro headwind to a fresh record close. Whether that divergence resolves toward Korea's caution or Taiwan's conviction is the question for next week.

## United States

US indices closed a strong week on a softer note: **S&P 500 7,722.06 (−0.12%)**, **Nasdaq 26,450.59 (−0.34%)**, **Dow 53,598.14 (+0.05%)**, as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks ("the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices," with PCE inflation at 3.7% y/y) triggered a bear-flattener in yields. PayPal plunged 11-14.6% after Stripe/Advent abandoned their ~$50-53B takeover bid, and Marvell fell 7-9% on soft margin guidance despite an earnings beat. Full write-up in **`usa.md`**.

## Taiwan (TAIEX / 加權指數)

Surged **356.23 points (+0.77%) to a fresh record close of 46,331.45**, on turnover back above NT$1 trillion for the first time in ten sessions. **Largan Precision hit a second consecutive limit-up**, breaking through NT$7,000 for the first time, on continued CPO supply-chain speculation. Institutional investors delivered an extraordinary **+NT$579.13B** combined net buy — nearly nine times Thursday's figure. Full breakdown in **`taiwan.md`**.

## Japan (Nikkei 225)

Rebounded to **66,405.56, up 273.58 points (+0.41%)**, as IT, software, and automaker shares tracked the same enterprise-software strength that drove Thursday's US rally, though the index gave back roughly half an intraday 700-point gain as caution set in ahead of Jackson Hole. TOPIX extended its winning streak to seven sessions. Full write-up in **`japan.md`**.

## Korea (KOSPI)

**KOSPI plunged 1.79% to 6,788.88** on a concentrated wave of foreign (−₩1.76T) and institutional (−₩284.1B) selling in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix specifically, ahead of the Jackson Hole speech. **KOSDAQ closed slightly higher**, underscoring that this was a large-cap-chip-specific move rather than a broad Korea selloff. Full write-up in **`korea.md`**.

## Hong Kong (Hang Seng)

**Hang Seng Index** rose to **25,593 (+0.11% to +0.30%)**, erasing an opening dip as offshore capital rotated back toward Hong Kong and away from mainland Shanghai listings — a reversal of Thursday's pattern. Full write-up in **`hongkong.md`**.

## Southeast Asia (Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand)

A quiet session region-wide. **Indonesia's IHSG** closed essentially flat (**−0.06% to 6,518**) after Thursday's strong rebound, with sector leadership shifting toward smaller, speculative names. **Thailand's SET fell 0.78% to 1,588.22** on concentrated large-cap (DELTA, AOT, ADVANC) selling. Singapore and Malaysia closing figures were not confirmed among sources checked. Full write-up in **`southeast_asia.md`**.

## UK & Europe

European markets traded higher ahead of the Jackson Hole speech — **CAC 40 rebounded ~1.1%** from Thursday's political-risk selloff, **DAX pushed toward record highs (~+0.7%)**, and **FTSE 100 rose modestly (~+0.25%)** — but these readings were taken before Warsh's hawkish tone fully landed; given the US market's own late-session reversal, Monday's open is the real test of whether these gains held.
