# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — August 28, 2026 (Friday)

## Headline

European markets traded higher through the session ahead of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, with continental Europe outpacing London. As of readings taken ahead of the speech's full market digestion: **FTSE 100 was up ~0.25% at 10,819.46**, **DAX rose ~0.69% to 26,538.32** — pushing "once again... up to near all-time highs" — and **CAC 40 led the region, up ~1.11% to 8,412.03**, a strong recovery from Thursday's political-risk-driven selloff (see `../260827/uk_europe.md`). *(These figures reflect readings taken during the session ahead of Warsh's speech; given the hawkish tone of his remarks and the US market's own late-session pullback — see `usa.md` — final closing levels may have moderated somewhat from these intraday readings.)*

## What drove the session

- **France's rebound**: CAC 40's recovery came a day after French bank stocks (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole) fell 5-7% on PM Bayrou's confidence-vote announcement — Friday's bounce suggests markets treated Thursday's move as an overreaction, or at least paused their selling pending more clarity ahead of the actual September 8 vote.
- **Jackson Hole was described as "the only event that matters into the weekend"** for European markets, with the key uncertainty being which "reaction function" Warsh would hand markets — September rate-hike pricing remained "alive but still a minority view" heading into the speech, and the Fed's own reaction function had been left ambiguous after July's FOMC meeting.
- **UK-specific headwind**: defence-budget uncertainty provided a modest drag on London specifically, even as the broader European tone stayed constructive.
- **DAX's push toward record highs** reflects the market pricing a favorable Warsh outcome — one analyst noted that if Warsh "sends the right signals, the DAX could say goodbye to the weekend with a new record high," while flagging that rising bond yields into the speech suggested "the market is pricing in rather restrictive tonality" as the more likely risk.

## Bottom line

A session defined entirely by anticipation — France's bounce-back from Thursday's political-risk selloff, and DAX pushing toward record highs, both reflected optimism heading into Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks rather than any resolved catalyst. Given that Warsh's actual tone came in more hawkish than markets hoped (triggering a bear-flattener in US Treasury yields and a late-session equity pullback — see `usa.md`), Europe's Friday gains were likely tested into the close and are worth re-checking against Monday's opening levels; the DAX's "new record high" scenario in particular hinged on a dovish surprise that, per the US market reaction, did not clearly arrive.
