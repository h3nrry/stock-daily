# Taiwan Stock Market Analysis — September 3, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

Taiwan gave back part of Wednesday's session in a genuinely two-part day: the market opened firmer, tracking Wall Street's overnight strength, but a midday plunge in Japan's and Korea's markets dragged sentiment lower and Taiwan's own ABF-substrate and memory-chip names sold off hard on sector-specific news. The **TAIEX (加權指數) fell 307.06 points (−0.67%) to close at 45,857.66**, losing the psychologically important 46,000 level, on turnover of **NT$946.27 billion (成交值9,462.71億元)**. The index swung roughly 680 points intraday between its high and low. Three institutional investors turned net sellers, with a combined **NT$63.71 billion (三大法人合計賣超637.11億元)** net sell — a smaller outflow than Wednesday's NT$115.03B but still a clear risk-off signal (see `../260902/taiwan.md`).

## What drove the drop

- **A regional whipsaw, not a Taiwan-specific story**: Taiwan's market opened higher on Wall Street's overnight gains, but Japan's and Korea's own sharp intraday reversals around midday (see `japan.md` and `korea.md`) pulled Taiwan into the red alongside them — the same synchronized Asia-Pacific pattern that has run through the week's Iran/oil/yield narrative.
- **IC substrate / ABF names sold off hard on a supply-side repricing story, not falling demand**: **Nan Ya PCB / 南亞電路板 (8046) hit the daily limit-down**, closing at NT$1,080 (−120 points), while **King Yuan Electronics / 京元電子 (3189) fell −8.50%** and **Zhen Ding Technology / 臻鼎 (4958) fell −8.48%**, and **Unimicron / 欣興電子 (3037) fell −5.14%**. The trigger was twofold: Japan's Nitto Denko signaling it will expand ABF substrate production capacity in 2027–2028 (shifting the market narrative from "shortage now" to "how long does the shortage last"), and Nan Ya itself disclosing that it has **no further price increases planned for Q4** — local commentary characterized this explicitly as a **"repricing" (重新定價)** of the substrate-shortage story rather than any deterioration in underlying demand.
- **Memory chips and other electronics sub-sectors also fell**: the broader electronics index (電子類指數) declined **−0.85%**; **Nanya Technology / 南亞科 dropped −7.82%**; **Largan Precision / 大立光 (optics) plunged −8.45%**; **Yageo / 國巨 (passive components) fell −5.37%**; and the **Formosa Plastics group (台塑集團)** retreated broadly (roughly **−2% to −6.95%** across its listed members).
- **Several formerly "thousand-dollar club" stocks fell out of that tier**, including **ASUS / 華碩**, **Acer / 宏碁**, and **Global Unichip / 創意** (per local commentary — high-priced names disproportionately affected by the session's volatility).
- **TSMC / 台積電 closed at NT$2,390**, moving in line with the broader index's decline as the market lost the 46,000 level in late trading.

## Bottom line

A genuinely two-layered session: Taiwan's market was first pulled into the red by the same regional Iran/oil/yield-driven whipsaw hitting Japan and Korea at midday, and then hit a second, more Taiwan-specific wave of selling in IC substrate and memory names on a supply-side "repricing" story (Nitto Denko's capacity expansion, Nan Ya's own Q4 pricing freeze) that local commentary is explicitly framing as valuation normalization rather than demand deterioration. With combined institutional net selling (NT$63.71B) roughly half of Wednesday's pace, and TSMC itself moving in line with — rather than leading — the broader index's decline, today's session reads as more contained and sector-concentrated than Wednesday's broader institutional exodus. Worth watching whether the ABF-substrate names stabilize once the market fully digests the difference between "no further price hikes this quarter" and "the multi-year shortage story is over."
