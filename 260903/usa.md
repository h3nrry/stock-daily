# USA Stock Market Analysis — September 3, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

US indices posted their best session in a month, with all three major benchmarks rallying more than 1% as Treasury yields retreated from the week's highs. **Dow Jones closed at 53,686.11 (+1.18%)**, **S&P 500 at 7,747.71 (+1.06%)**, and the **Nasdaq Composite at 26,612.37 (+1.51%)** — a clean, broad-based bounce that builds directly on Wednesday's smaller recovery (see `../260902/usa.md`) and puts the Nasdaq back into positive territory for the trailing month.

## What drove the session

- **A dovish Fed voice pulled rate-hike odds back down**: Fed Governor **Christopher Waller** highlighted a steady disinflation trend — noting that three-month annualized inflation has fallen from 4.76% in February — and signaled he would support holding rates steady if that trend continues. Markets read this as a direct counterweight to Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh's** more hawkish, hike-leaning tone since his Jackson Hole remarks (see `../260828/usa.md` and subsequent files), and **odds of a September rate hike fell from roughly 63% to about 50%** on the day.
- **Treasury yields eased in response**: the **10-year Treasury yield dropped roughly 5 basis points to about 4.74–4.75%**, down from Wednesday's close near 4.79%, directly supporting the equity rally (and, separately, a strong bounce in gold — see below).
- **The Iran/oil risk premium stayed elevated but didn't escalate further**: President Trump said the US had conducted a "very heavy attack" against Iran but suggested the campaign "wouldn't take too long," which markets read as capping — rather than resolving — the conflict. Brent crude spiked intraday toward $99/barrel before easing back (see Global cross-asset section), keeping energy-driven inflation risk in the background rather than the foreground.
- **Labor data was a non-event**: weekly jobless claims ticked up marginally to 206,000 (vs. 205,000 expected) — a modest, unremarkable cooling signal ahead of Friday's August nonfarm payrolls report, the week's next major catalyst.
- **Sector leadership**: Industrials, Financials, and Consumer Cyclical stocks led the rally — a similar rotation pattern to Wednesday's, favoring domestically-oriented and rate-sensitive groups as yields fell.

## Stock-level detail

- **Nvidia rose about +1%** after confirming a **$12.9–13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face**, the widely-used open-source AI model/platform hub — a defensive-and-offensive move to keep the open-weight AI ecosystem within Nvidia's orbit as competition in AI infrastructure intensifies.
- **Broadcom fell −3.07% to $355.96** despite beating Q3 estimates and reporting that AI-chip revenue nearly tripled year-over-year — the stock declined because **Q4 guidance came in cautious**, illustrating that with AI-linked names now priced for continued acceleration, a "beat but don't raise enough" quarter is being punished rather than rewarded.
- **Snowflake soared over +20%** on a blowout Q2 report, the session's standout single-stock mover.
- **Tesla and SpaceX-linked names surged roughly +7%**, with Tesla's gain tied to anticipation around an upcoming Cybercab event.
- **Robinhood Markets advanced** on sell-side analyst upgrades.

## What to watch next

1. **Friday's August nonfarm payrolls report** — the week's culminating data point, now carrying extra weight given Waller's disinflation argument and the resulting swing in rate-hike odds; a hot print could quickly reverse today's yield-driven rally.
2. **Whether Waller's dovish framing continues to outweigh Chair Warsh's hawkish stance** — the two Fed voices are pulling in opposite directions on rate-hike odds, and further public commentary from either (or from other FOMC members) could move markets sharply in either direction.
3. **Whether Iran/oil tensions stay "capped but elevated"** — Trump's "wouldn't take too long" framing kept a lid on today's oil spike, but any fresh escalation (or de-escalation) remains the single clearest swing factor for both equities and yields.
4. **AI-capex-linked earnings reactions**, following Broadcom's "beat but guide cautiously, sell off anyway" pattern — a signal that the bar for AI-infrastructure names has risen further, worth watching into upcoming reports from other AI-supply-chain names.

## Bottom line

A genuinely strong, broad session — all three major indices up more than 1%, with the Nasdaq's +1.51% the standout — built on a straightforward catalyst: a dovish Fed governor pulling rate-hike odds down and Treasury yields lower, which in turn lifted both equities and gold simultaneously. The rally papers over a real tension, though — Fed Chair Warsh's own hawkish stance hasn't gone away, oil is still trading near $97–99, and Friday's jobs report is a genuine swing risk. Broadcom's post-earnings decline despite a beat is a useful reminder that AI-linked names are now being judged on the trajectory of guidance, not just the current quarter's results.
