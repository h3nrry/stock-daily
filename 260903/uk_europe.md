# UK & Europe Stock Market Analysis — September 3, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

European markets snapped their three-session losing streak, rebounding as the global bond selloff eased. **FTSE 100 closed at 10,835.89 (+0.74%)**, **DAX at 25,997.78 (+0.61%)**, **CAC 40 at 8,286.40 (+0.07%)**, and the pan-European **STOXX 600 at 649.10 (+0.49%)** — a broad, if uneven, recovery that directly mirrors the US session's own yield-driven bounce (see `usa.md`) and ends Germany's three-day run as the region's weakest major index (see `../260902/uk_europe.md`).

## What drove the session

- **A global bond selloff eased, directly lifting equities**: the same Fed-driven yield pullback described in `usa.md` — Fed Governor Waller's dovish disinflation comments pulling US rate-hike odds down and the 10-year Treasury yield off its highs — rippled into European government bond yields, which themselves retreated from multi-year highs after three straight sessions of climbing.
- **Oil stayed elevated but the direction, not just the level, mattered**: Brent traded above $90/barrel (having spiked intraday toward $99 before easing, see `usa.md`) — still a genuine headwind for energy-import-dependent Europe, but the session's relief rally shows markets reacting more to the yield move than to the still-elevated oil price itself.
- **ECB rate-hike expectations firmed further**: traders now expect the **ECB to raise its policy rate to 2.5% at next week's meeting**, with **two additional 25bp hikes priced in by mid-2027** — a continuation of the theme flagged in Wednesday's file, now extending beyond the immediate September decision into a longer hiking path.
- **Investors stayed focused on Friday's US nonfarm payrolls report**, the same catalyst driving caution across every market in this project this week, following the recent run of hawkish Fed commentary from Chair Warsh.

## Stock-level detail

- **Soitec surged +10%** after the French chipmaker raised its 2027 revenue-growth forecast to 50% year-over-year — the session's standout single-stock mover in Europe.
- **Deutsche Telekom rose +1.7%** after activist investor Elliott Management built a stake in the company.
- **Sofina (Belgian holding company) climbed +3.6%** after reporting strong first-half growth; the firm's largest private-fund position is in SpaceX, giving it indirect exposure to the same name driving Thursday's US rally (see `usa.md`).
- **M&G slipped a modest −0.2%** after the UK insurer/asset manager reported its first-half results — a muted reaction relative to the broader market's gains.

## What to watch next

1. **Next week's ECB meeting**, with expectations now firming around a hike to 2.5% and a longer path of further increases through mid-2027 — a more hawkish multi-meeting picture than earlier in the week.
2. **Friday's US nonfarm payrolls report** — the same catalyst flagged in `usa.md`, with obvious read-through for both US and European yields and, by extension, today's relief-rally thesis.
3. **Whether Germany's return to outperformance (ending its three-session run as the region's weakest major index) holds**, or whether today's +0.61% is a one-day bounce within a still-negative 5-day trend (−1.40%).
4. **France's September 8 confidence vote**, still the region's clearest idiosyncratic risk, continuing to weigh on the CAC 40 (−4.75% over 20 days) even as the broader European market recovers.

## Bottom line

A genuine, broad relief rally that snapped Europe's three-session losing streak — FTSE, DAX, and STOXX 600 all posted solid gains, while CAC 40's much smaller +0.07% gain reflects the index's own separate domestic political overhang rather than the shared Iran/oil/yield driver behind the rest of the region's move. The rally's underlying cause — a global bond selloff easing on dovish Fed commentary — is the same story driving today's US rally (see `usa.md`), underscoring how tightly linked European markets remain to the US rate-policy narrative even as the ECB pursues its own, increasingly hawkish, tightening path.
