# Southeast Asia Stock Market Analysis — September 3, 2026 (Thursday)

## Overview

Southeast Asia had a genuinely positive session across the board on Thursday — a contrast to the region's mixed-to-negative tone earlier in the week — as easing global bond yields (see `../usa.md`) lifted risk appetite broadly. Singapore touched a fresh intraday record high, Indonesia rallied more than 1%, Malaysia extended its gains on a central-bank rate hold, and Thailand posted a modest but genuine rebound.

## Singapore — STI

The **Straits Times Index closed at 5,747.71, up 0.06%** from Wednesday's 5,744.11 (Investing.com's series confirms both figures) — but the flat-looking close masks a much stronger session underneath: the index **jumped as much as 1% intraday to a fresh record high of 5,807.16 points** before paring nearly all of those gains into the close. The intraday strength was driven by **Singapore's three largest banks — DBS, OCBC, and UOB — rising between 0.7% and 1.2%**, a major boost to the index, alongside a **real-estate rally led by HongKong Land, which jumped 4.1%** on Bloomberg reporting that the company is in talks to enter Japan's resurgent property market. The broader driver was the same one lifting risk assets globally: **investors cheered an overnight drop in US Treasury yields amid cooling bets on an imminent Fed rate hike** (see `usa.md`), plus continued strength in technology and AI-linked stocks regionally.

## Indonesia — IHSG/JCI

The **IHSG closed at 6,667.89, up 72.12 points (+1.09%)**, a clean, broad-based rally on turnover of **Rp18.64 trillion** across 40.79 billion shares traded (402 gainers, 213 losers, 180 unchanged) — a marked improvement on Wednesday's essentially flat session (see `../260902/southeast_asia.md`). **Transportation led all sectors at +2.45%**, followed by **industrials (+1.89%)** and **non-cyclical consumer stocks (+1.62%)**. On the losing side, **technology fell −1.62%** and **healthcare dipped a marginal −0.02%**. Top gainers included **Erajaya Swasembada (ERAA)**, **Indomobil Sukses Internasional (IMAS)**, and **Samudera Indonesia (SMDR)**; top losers included **DMS Propertindo (KOTA)**, **Kawasan Industri Jababeka (KIJA)**, and **Dharma Satya Nusantara (DSNG)**.

## Malaysia — FBM KLCI

The **FBM KLCI closed at 1,715.13, up 6.39 points (+0.37%)**, extending Wednesday's own +0.48% gain (1,708.74 close) for a second straight positive session. The rally was driven by two factors: **Bank Negara Malaysia holding its Overnight Policy Rate unchanged at 2.75%**, removing a near-term source of uncertainty, and the market **tracking overnight gains on Wall Street**. Breadth was positive (510 gainers vs. 442 decliners, 549 unchanged) on trading value of **RM3.21 billion**. The broader **FBM 70 index rose 120.71 points to 17,905.28**, the **FBM Emas climbed 56.51 to 12,683.34**, and the **FBM Shariah gained 32.22 to 12,442.57** — a genuinely broad rally rather than one confined to the large-cap KLCI constituents. Notable actively-traded gainers included **Zetrix AI Bhd** and **Salutica Bhd**.

## Thailand — SET

The **SET Index closed at 1,576.92, up 1.85 points (+0.12%)**, on trading value of **THB 64.48 billion** — a modest rebound after "moving sideways-up in early trading before paring some gains in the afternoon session," per local commentary, which characterized the broader multi-week trend as still "trending sideways-down" despite today's positive close. Market participants remained focused on the upcoming **US nonfarm payrolls report and inflation data**, the same catalyst driving caution across every market in this project this week.

## Bottom line

A genuinely constructive session across all four Southeast Asian markets covered here — Singapore's record intraday high (even if it faded to a flat close), Indonesia's clean +1.09% broad-based rally, Malaysia's rate-hold-driven extension of Wednesday's gains, and Thailand's modest but real rebound all point the same direction: easing global Treasury yields and a calmer Iran/oil narrative (see `usa.md`) gave the region's markets room to recover after a cautious start to the week. Indonesia's transportation-sector leadership and Singapore's bank/property-driven record high stand out as the region's clearest positive stories today, though Thailand's own characterization of the broader trend as still "sideways-down" is a useful reminder that one green session doesn't yet mark a clean reversal.
