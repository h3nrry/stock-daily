# Korea Stock Market Analysis — September 3, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

Korea's KOSPI had one of its most volatile sessions in weeks — swinging nearly 4 percentage points from peak to trough — before closing only modestly higher. The index **opened up 87.61 points (+1.33%) at 6,650.33**, climbed as high as **6,682.97 (+1.83%)**, then plunged to **6,439.49 (−1.88%)** in roughly ten minutes around 2pm local time, before recovering into the close at **6,579.48, up just 16.76 points (+0.26%)**. The **KOSDAQ fared worse, closing at 790.21, down 13.77 points (−1.71%)** after opening higher and failing to recover. This follows Wednesday's sharp −3.99% plunge (see `../260902/korea.md`).

## What drove the session

- **A strong open on easing global pressure**: falling international oil prices and US Treasury yields overnight created favorable early conditions, extending the "yields easing, growth narrative intact" read that also lifted US markets (see `usa.md`).
- **An unexplained afternoon collapse**: around 2pm, the index plunged nearly 4 percentage points from its intraday high within about ten minutes. Local market commentary explicitly noted **"no clear negative catalyst was identified"** for the drop, pointing instead to **institutional and pension-fund selling** rather than any fundamental deterioration — a technical/positioning-driven air pocket rather than a news-driven selloff.
- **Retail investors sold into the volatility**: individual investors net-sold roughly **₩955.2 billion**, while foreign investors and institutions also sold positions during the session — a reversal from the pattern seen in some recent sessions where retail has been the buyer absorbing institutional selling.
- **Share buybacks were the key stabilizing force again**: combined Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix buyback purchases totaled **₩1.61 trillion** on the day, providing crucial support that prevented a deeper close-to-close loss — directly continuing the pattern flagged in Wednesday's file, where buybacks are explicitly described as having "emerged as a new net-buying force supporting the index floor."
- **Semiconductor giants reversed intraday**: both Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix moved from morning strength into afternoon declines, amplifying the broader downward pressure during the 2pm selloff; **SK Hynix closed −1.05%** and **Samsung Electronics closed −0.2%**.

## Bottom line

A session that looked calm on the closing tape (+0.26%) but was genuinely turbulent underneath — a nearly 4-percentage-point intraday swing driven by an afternoon institutional/pension-fund selloff that local commentary could not attribute to any specific catalyst. Samsung and SK Hynix's combined ₩1.61 trillion daily buyback pace was, once again, the difference between a calm green close and what could otherwise have been a second consecutive sharp decline following Wednesday's −3.99% plunge. With retail investors turning net sellers today (a reversal from their usual role absorbing institutional selling) and the "no clear catalyst" framing of the afternoon drop, Friday's session is worth watching closely for whether this was a one-off technical air pocket or the start of a more genuine shift in positioning.
