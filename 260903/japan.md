# Japan Stock Market Analysis — September 3, 2026 (Thursday)

## Headline

Japan's Nikkei 225 fell for a fourth consecutive session, though the decline was far milder than Wednesday's sharp drop. The index **closed at 64,214.48, down 111.16 points (−0.17%)** (Nikkei's own reporting rounds this to "¥111安"), a session that opened with roughly ¥400 of recovery on overnight US strength and oversold conditions, but faded as the day wore on. The **TOPIX held support at its 25-day moving average**, preventing a broader deterioration even as the Nikkei itself slipped into the red.

## What drove the session

- **A morning bounce that ran out of steam**: the index opened higher, tracking Wednesday's US equity gains and reflecting oversold conditions after three straight losing sessions (see `../260902/japan.md`), but "buy-side momentum exhausted, and the index fell into negative territory due to insufficient underlying demand," per local commentary — a telling sign that Wednesday's rout had not fully cleared out cautious positioning.
- **Regional strength offered partial support**: strong performance in South Korea's and Taiwan's tech-heavy markets earlier in the session provided some underlying support for Japanese equities, though — as detailed in `korea.md` and `taiwan.md` — those markets themselves reversed sharply intraday, meaning the "support" was itself unstable through the day.
- **The same oil/yield headwinds as every other market this week**: rising crude oil prices and still-elevated global interest rates weighed on sentiment throughout the session, extending the theme that has run through this entire week's coverage.
- **A domestic rate-policy overlay**: concerns about a **potential acceleration of Bank of Japan rate hikes**, partly attributed to pressure from the US side, limited buying enthusiasm — a continuation of the "3% rate era" bracing theme flagged in Wednesday's file.
- **Semiconductor-related names showed particular instability**, with market participants notably less aggressive than their US and other Asian counterparts on the day.

## Money flow — 外国人 (foreign investors)

Same-day foreign-investor flow data via JPX's weekly investor-type statistics was not available at writing time (JPX publishes on a weekly, not daily, cadence, and the most recent published week predates September 3). The qualitative picture from Thursday's session — a morning bounce that faded on "insufficient underlying demand," per local commentary — is consistent with buyers staying cautious rather than a broad-based foreign-investor exodus; this should be treated as a read on sentiment rather than a confirmed flow figure.

## Stocks/sectors to watch

| Stock/Sector | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor-related names | Flagged specifically as unstable in Thursday's session, tracking the same repricing pressure hitting Taiwan's IC-substrate and memory names (see `taiwan.md`) | Directly exposed to any further deterioration in the regional AI-supply-chain sentiment that has been volatile all week |
| TOPIX-tracking large caps | The TOPIX held its 25-day moving average even as the Nikkei fell — a signal of underlying support broader than the headline index suggests | A break of that 25-day support level on any renewed Iran/oil escalation would remove the session's one clear stabilizing signal |
| Rate-sensitive domestic names | The BOJ rate-acceleration overhang (partly attributed to US pressure) is a Japan-specific risk factor distinct from the shared Iran/oil/yield story elsewhere in this project | A confirmed acceleration in BOJ tightening expectations would pressure precisely the sectors that have been relatively resilient through this week's broader selloff |

## Bottom line

A fourth straight down day for the Nikkei, but a genuinely mild one relative to Wednesday's sharp −2.85% to −2.96% drop — today's −0.17% decline reflects a morning bounce that simply ran out of buying conviction rather than a fresh wave of selling. The TOPIX holding its 25-day moving average is the clearest sign that the broader market's underlying support held up better than the Nikkei's own headline number suggests, even as semiconductor names and a domestic BOJ-rate overlay kept a lid on any full recovery.
